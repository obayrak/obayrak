📐   Theory — Deriving and solving the longitudinal dynamics equation ▲
01Forces Acting on the Car — Newton's 2nd Law

Consider a car of mass m moving along a flat road. Four longitudinal forces determine acceleration:

  • F
    Engine/Traction force F_e — delivered at the wheels. Depends on throttle, gear ratio, and engine torque curve.
  • D
    Aerodynamic drag F_D — quadratic: opposes motion, proportional to v². Dominates at high speed.
  • R
    Rolling resistance F_R — nearly constant: tire deformation, bearing friction. Dominates at low speed.
  • B
    Braking force F_B — applied by driver, limited by tire–road friction μ·m·g.

Applying ΣF = ma in the longitudinal direction:

m·a = F_e − F_D − F_R − F_B
m·(dv/dt) = F_e − ½ρCd·A·v² − Cr·m·g − F_B

Note: on a slope, add −m·g·sinα. Here we treat flat terrain. The equation is nonlinear due to the v² drag term — no simple closed form when all forces coexist.

02Aerodynamic Drag & Rolling Resistance

Aerodynamic drag arises from pressure difference front-to-rear and skin friction:

F_D = ½ · ρ · Cd · A · v²

At low speeds rolling resistance dominates; at ~80 km/h for a typical car, drag and rolling resistance are roughly equal. Above that, drag grows quadratically and dominates completely.

03Analytical Solution — Terminal Velocity

At constant throttle and no braking, define:

k = ½ρCd·A/m    α = Cr·g
F_net = F_e/m − α

The ODE becomes: v̇ = F_net − k·v²

At terminal velocity v̇ = 0:

v_∞ = √(F_net / k) = √((F_e/m − Cr·g) / (½ρCd·A/m))

The general solution (v₀ = 0) via separation of variables:

v(t) = v_∞ · tanh(t / τ)
τ = 1 / √(k · F_net) = m / (v_∞ · ½ρCd·A)

Position integrates to:

x(t) = (v_∞/α_k) · ln[cosh(t/τ)]  where α_k = √(k·F_net)

For non-zero v₀: v(t) = v_∞·tanh(α_k·t + arctanh(v₀/v_∞)). Matches RK4 to 6 significant figures.

04Engine Force, Power & Gear Ratios

The traction force at the wheels is determined by engine torque, gear ratio, and wheel radius:

F_e = (T_engine · G_total · η) / r_wheel

where G_total = G_gear · G_final is the combined gear ratio and η is drivetrain efficiency (~0.85–0.92).

Engine torque peaks at a specific RPM and drops off. Engine speed relates to wheel speed by:

RPM = (v · G_total · 60) / (2π · r_wheel)

Power limit caps maximum traction force:

F_e = min(F_e_torque, P_max / v)

05Numerical Integration — RK4

The equation m·dv/dt = F_e − ½ρCd·A·v² − Cr·m·g is a 1st-order nonlinear ODE. With position added, the state vector is [x, v]:

ẋ = v
v̇ = [F_e(v,t) − ½ρCd·A·v² − Cr·m·g − F_B] / m

RK4 at each time step h = 1/200 s:

k₁
f(tₙ, vₙ)
k₂
f(t+h/2, v+h·k₁/2)
k₃
f(t+h/2, v+h·k₂/2)
k₄
f(t+h, v+h·k₃)
vₙ₊₁ = vₙ + (h/6)(k₁ + 2k₂ + 2k₃ + k₄)

With constant throttle and no braking, the analytical tanh solution and RK4 agree to 6+ decimal places, validating the numerical scheme. Gear changes, braking, and speed-dependent torque curves require numerical treatment.

06Braking Distance & Energy Dissipation

During braking with force F_B = μ_brk·m·g, the ODE becomes:

m·v̇ = − ½ρCd·A·v² − (Cr + μ_brk)·m·g

The minimum stopping distance (from work-energy theorem, ignoring drag):

d_stop = v₀² / (2·μ_brk·g)

With drag included, the car stops slightly shorter. The kinetic energy ½mv² must equal the work done by all resistive forces:

½mv₀² = ∫₀^d [F_B + F_D + F_R] dx
