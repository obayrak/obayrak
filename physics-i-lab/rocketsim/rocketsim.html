Physics Equations & Solution Method
1 · Tsiolkovsky Rocket Equation

The ideal rocket equation relates velocity gain to mass ratio. Derived from conservation of momentum for a variable-mass system:

Fthrust = v_e · |ṁ|
Δvideal = v_e · ln(m₀ / m_f)
m(t) = m₀ − ṁ · t (while fuel > 0)

v_e = exhaust velocity, m₀ = initial mass, ṁ = mass flow rate. After burnout, m = m_dry = constant.

2 · Equations of Motion (3D)

Newton's 2nd law in 3D. The net force vector determines acceleration:

m(t) · a⃗ = F⃗_thrust + F⃗_drag + F⃗_gravity + F⃗_wind
ṙ⃗ = v⃗ , v̇⃗ = a⃗ = F⃗_net / m(t)
State: [x, y, z, vx, vy, vz, m]ᵀ → 7 ODEs

Integrated simultaneously at each timestep using 4th-order Runge-Kutta (RK4).

3 · Thrust Vector

Thrust acts along the rocket axis, defined by launch pitch θ and azimuth φ:

n̂ = [sin(θ)cos(φ), sin(θ)sin(φ), cos(θ)]
F⃗_thrust = v_e · ṁ · n̂ (powered phase)
F⃗_thrust = 0⃗ (coast & descent)

θ = pitch angle from vertical, φ = azimuth bearing. n̂ is the unit thrust direction vector.

4 · Aerodynamic Drag

Drag opposes the velocity vector, depends on atmospheric density and speed:

ρ(z) = ρ₀ · exp(−z / H)
|F_drag| = ½ · ρ(z) · C_d · A · |v|²
F⃗_drag = −|F_drag| · v̂ (opposes motion)

H = atmospheric scale height (~8500 m Earth), ρ₀ = surface density. Drag vanishes as density → 0 at high altitude.

5 · Gravity Model

Gravity varies with altitude. Using inverse-square law from planet center (radius R_planet):

g(z) = g₀ · (R_p / (R_p + z))²
F⃗_gravity = −m(t) · g(z) · ẑ
R_p = 6,371,000 m (Earth)

For low altitudes g(z) ≈ g₀. For high altitudes (> 100 km), the 1/r² reduction becomes significant.

6 · RK4 Numerical Integration

The 7-component state vector is advanced each timestep h = 0.1 s using RK4, giving O(h⁴) global error:

k₁ = f(tₙ, yₙ)
k₂ = f(tₙ+h/2, yₙ+h·k₁/2)
k₃ = f(tₙ+h/2, yₙ+h·k₂/2)
k₄ = f(tₙ+h, yₙ+h·k₃)
yn+1 = yₙ + (h/6)·(k₁+2k₂+2k₃+k₄)
