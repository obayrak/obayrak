01 Overview & System Model

WHAT THIS SIMULATOR COMPUTES

This tool integrates Newton's second law for a rigid point-mass car moving along a 1-D road profile. Four forces act along the direction of travel: the drive force from the engine/motor, aerodynamic drag, rolling friction, and the gravity component on any incline. The resulting ODE is solved two independent ways — a closed-form analytical solution (valid on flat ground with constant drive force) and a 4th-order Runge-Kutta numerical integration (valid for any road profile, since it re-evaluates forces at every sub-step) — so you can see the numerical method converge onto the exact solution in the simplified case, then diverge from it once hills are introduced.

1-D longitudinal motion point mass, no suspension quasi-static incline quadratic aerodynamic drag Coulomb-style rolling resistance no wheel slip constant driver-set force F

03 Force Components

WHAT EACH TERM IN THE MODEL REPRESENTS

Aerodynamic Drag

Quadratic in speed and always opposes motion (hence to keep the correct sign when v could be negative). is standard sea-level air density, Cd the drag coefficient, and A the frontal cross-sectional area. This term dominates at highway speed since it grows with v².

Rolling Resistance

Modeled as Coulomb friction proportional to the normal force the road exerts on the tires. On an incline the normal force shrinks by , so rolling resistance is slightly lower going uphill than on flat ground at the same μ.

Gravity Component

The component of weight acting along the slope. Positive on an uphill segment (opposes motion), negative on a downhill segment (assists motion) — this is the term that produces the deceleration/acceleration bump you see crossing the hill in mixed road mode.

Drive Force

F is held constant for the whole run — the simulator does not model a throttle map, gearbox, or torque curve. Think of it as a simplified "constant net tractive effort" car, similar to how introductory dynamics problems treat a car under constant thrust.

05 Numerical Integration — RK4

WHY RUNGE-KUTTA, AND HOW THE FOUR STAGES WORK

Once the road isn't flat, depends on position, coupling the x and v equations nonlinearly — there's no general closed form. The simulator instead advances the state forward by discrete steps using classical 4th-order Runge-Kutta, which is accurate to per step (global error ) — far better than simple Euler integration for the same step size.

  1. k₁ — evaluate the derivative (acceleration and velocity) at the start of the interval, using the current state.
  2. k₂ — step half a timestep forward using k₁, then re-evaluate the derivative at that midpoint estimate.
  3. k₃ — step half a timestep forward again, this time using the improved k₂ slope, and re-evaluate at the midpoint.
  4. k₄ — step a full timestep forward using k₃, and evaluate the derivative at this endpoint estimate.
  5. Combine — blend the four slope estimates with weights 1:2:2:1 (Simpson's-rule-like weighting) to get the best single estimate of the average slope across the interval, then advance both velocity and position by one full .
In code, each k-stage calls accelAt(x, v, p), which re-reads the road angle at that stage's estimated position — this is exactly what lets the integrator correctly "feel" the hill boundary crossing mid-step, something the flat-road analytical formula cannot do.

06 Road Profile Model

HOW INCLINE ANGLE IS DETERMINED FROM POSITION
Modeθ(x)Behaviour
Flat0No grade anywhere; pure drag + rolling resistance problem, matches the analytical solution exactly.
Uphill+θ (constant)Constant climb for the whole run; car approaches a lower terminal velocity than on flat ground.
Downhill−θ (constant)Constant descent; gravity assists the drive force, terminal velocity is higher (or the car keeps accelerating if F+gravity permanently exceeds drag).
Mixed0 for x<L, +θ for L≤x<2L, 0 for x≥2LFlat run-up, one hill of length L at grade θ, then flat again — this is a step function in θ(x), so acceleration is discontinuous at the two boundaries.

L is the "Flat section length" slider. In mixed mode the hill itself is also length L, so the total profile is flat / hill / flat with each segment the same length.

08 Energy Accounting

WORK-ENERGY THEOREM CHECK

The Energy panel tracks three running quantities in kJ, each accumulated by trapezoidal-style summation as the simulation plays:

Kinetic Energy

Instantaneous, not cumulative — computed directly from current speed each frame.

Work Done by Engine

Running integral of drive-force power delivered over time.

Dissipated Energy

Running integral of power lost to drag and rolling friction combined.

To first order, Work Done ≈ Kinetic Energy gained + Dissipated Energy, modulo the potential energy exchanged with gravity on inclines (not separately plotted) and the coarseness of the dt used for accumulation.
