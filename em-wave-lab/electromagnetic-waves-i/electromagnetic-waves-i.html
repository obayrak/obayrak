MAXWELL'S EQUATIONS

All electromagnetic phenomena — light, radio, X-rays — follow four equations derived by Maxwell in 1865. The crucial insight was the displacement current term, predicting self-sustaining waves.

Gauss (Electric)

\[\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\rho/\varepsilon_0\ \Rightarrow\ \nabla\cdot\mathbf E=0\quad\text{(free space)}\]

Gauss (Magnetic)

\[\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0\quad\text{(no magnetic monopoles)}\]

Faraday's Law

\[\nabla\times\mathbf E=-\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial t}\quad\text{(changing }\mathbf B\text{ induces }\mathbf E\text{)}\]

Ampère–Maxwell Law

\[\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mu_0\mathbf J+\mu_0\varepsilon_0\frac{\partial\mathbf E}{\partial t}\]

Wave Equation

\[\nabla^2\mathbf E=\frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2\mathbf E}{\partial t^2}\]
\(c=1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}=299\,792\,458\) m/s (exact, SI definition)

Plane Wave Solution

\[\begin{aligned}\mathbf E(z,t)&=E_0\cos(kz-\omega t)\,\hat{\mathbf x}\\ \mathbf B(z,t)&=\frac{E_0}{c}\cos(kz-\omega t)\,\hat{\mathbf y}\end{aligned}\]

\(\mathbf E\perp\mathbf B\perp\hat{\mathbf k}\) always. In the Wave tab the red curve is the E-field and the blue curve is B, rotated 90° (B is drawn at 0.45× its relative size for clarity).

REFLECTION & REFRACTION

Snell's Law

\[n_1\sin\theta_1=n_2\sin\theta_2\]

Critical Angle (TIR)

\[\theta_c=\arcsin(n_2/n_1)\quad[\text{requires }n_1>n_2]\]

For \(\theta_i>\theta_c\), total internal reflection occurs — no energy crosses the interface. This is the principle behind fiber optics.

Fresnel Equations (s-pol)

\[r_s=\frac{n_1\cos\theta_i-n_2\cos\theta_t}{n_1\cos\theta_i+n_2\cos\theta_t},\qquad t_s=\frac{2n_1\cos\theta_i}{n_1\cos\theta_i+n_2\cos\theta_t}\]

Fresnel Equations (p-pol)

\[r_p=\frac{n_2\cos\theta_i-n_1\cos\theta_t}{n_2\cos\theta_i+n_1\cos\theta_t},\qquad t_p=\frac{2n_1\cos\theta_i}{n_2\cos\theta_i+n_1\cos\theta_t}\]

Brewster's Angle

\[\theta_B=\arctan(n_2/n_1)\ \Rightarrow\ r_p=0\]

At Brewster's angle, reflected light is completely s-polarized. Used in polarizing sunglasses and laser Brewster windows.

Poynting Vector

\[\mathbf S=\frac{1}{\mu_0}\,\mathbf E\times\mathbf B\quad[\mathrm{W/m^2}]\]\[\langle I\rangle=\tfrac12\varepsilon_0 c\,|E_0|^2=\frac{|E_0|^2}{2\eta_0}\]
\(\eta_0=\sqrt{\mu_0/\varepsilon_0}=376.73\ \Omega\) (impedance of free space)

INTERFERENCE & POLARIZATION

Superposition

\[\mathbf E_{\text{tot}}=\mathbf E_1+\mathbf E_2\quad\text{(linearity of Maxwell)}\]\[I=I_1+I_2+2\sqrt{I_1I_2}\,\cos\Delta\varphi\quad(f_1=f_2)\]
Constructive: \(\Delta\varphi=2n\pi\ \Rightarrow\ I=(\sqrt{I_1}+\sqrt{I_2})^2\)
Destructive: \(\Delta\varphi=(2n+1)\pi\ \Rightarrow\ I=(\sqrt{I_1}-\sqrt{I_2})^2\)

Fringe visibility: \(V=2\sqrt{I_1I_2}/(I_1+I_2)\). Beats occur when \(f_1\ne f_2\).

Standing Waves (Reflection)

\[E=2E_0\sin(kz)\sin(\omega t)\quad[r_s=-1]\]

The incident + reflected waves form a pattern with nodes at \(kz=n\pi\) and antinodes at \(kz=(n+\tfrac12)\pi\).

Linear Polarization

\[\mathbf E(z,t)=E_0\sin(kz-\omega t)\left(\cos\varphi\,\hat{\mathbf x}+\sin\varphi\,\hat{\mathbf y}\right)\]

Circular Polarization (RHCP)

\[\mathbf E=E_0\left[\cos(kz-\omega t)\,\hat{\mathbf x}+\sin(kz-\omega t)\,\hat{\mathbf y}\right]\]

The E-field traces a right-handed helix through space — visible directly in the Polarization tab.

Stokes Parameters

\[\begin{aligned}S_0&=I_0, & S_1&=I_0\cos2\chi\cos2\psi,\\ S_2&=I_0\cos2\chi\sin2\psi, & S_3&=I_0\sin2\chi\end{aligned}\]

FIBER OPTICS

Optical fibers exploit total internal reflection to confine light within the core (\(n_{\text{core}}>n_{\text{clad}}\)). Light bounces along the fiber with near-zero loss.

\[NA=\sqrt{n_{\text{core}}^2-n_{\text{clad}}^2},\qquad\theta_{\text{accept}}=\arcsin(NA)\]acceptance cone half-angle (launch from air)

Single-Mode Fiber (SMF-28)

Core ≈ 9 μm. One guided mode (HE₁₁). Used for long-haul internet (transatlantic cables). Attenuation: 0.18 dB/km at 1550 nm.

Single-mode when \(V\lt 2.405\), with \(V=\dfrac{2\pi a}{\lambda}\,NA\)

Multi-Mode Fiber (MMF)

Core ≈ 50 μm. Hundreds of modes. Modal dispersion limits bandwidth to ~500 MHz·km. Used for short-distance campus/datacenter links.

Graded-Index (GRIN)

\[n(r)=n_{\text{core}}\sqrt{1-2\Delta\,(r/a)^2}\]

Parabolic index profile equalizes path lengths — all modes travel at nearly the same group velocity, minimizing modal dispersion.

EDFA & WDM

Erbium-doped fiber amplifiers provide ~35 dB gain at 1550 nm. Wavelength-division multiplexing (WDM) carries 80–160 channels per fiber at 100 GHz spacing → Tbps per fiber pair.

ANTENNA THEORY

Antennas convert guided EM power to/from free-space radiation. The oscillating current creates a time-varying electric dipole that radiates.

Hertzian Dipole (Far Field)

\[E_\theta=\frac{j\eta I_0\,\Delta z}{2\lambda r}\sin\theta\;e^{-jkr},\qquad D=1.5\ (1.76\ \mathrm{dBi})\]

Half-Wave Dipole (λ/2)

\[F(\theta)=\frac{\cos^2\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\cos\theta\right)}{\sin^2\theta}\]\[D=1.64\ (2.15\ \mathrm{dBi}),\qquad Z_{\text{in}}\approx 73+j42.5\ \Omega\]

Friis Transmission

\[\frac{P_r}{P_t}=G_tG_r\left(\frac{\lambda}{4\pi R}\right)^2\]

The received-power factor \((\lambda/4\pi R)^2\) falls with distance and with frequency (shorter \(\lambda\)), so free-space path loss grows with both. Used for link budget calculations in all wireless systems.

Phased Array / MIMO

\[AF(\theta)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_n\,e^{j(n-1)(kd\cos\theta+\beta)}\]

5G massive MIMO uses 64–256 elements for spatial multiplexing, boosting spectral efficiency 10× over legacy SISO. Beam steering requires only phase shifts (\(\beta\)), no mechanical motion.

Applications

5G mm-wave · AESA radar · GPS patch · radio astronomy (SKA) · RFID · radar altimeters · EM compatibility

FURTHER APPLICATIONS

Radar & Doppler

\[R=\frac{c\,\Delta t}{2},\qquad\Delta f=\frac{2vf_0}{c}\]

Pulse-Doppler radar resolves range AND velocity simultaneously. SAR (synthetic aperture radar) achieves <0.5 m ground resolution from satellites at 500 km altitude.

MRI

RF at Larmor frequency (42.58 MHz/T for ¹H) flips nuclear spins. T₁/T₂ relaxation encodes tissue contrast. 3T scanners reach sub-millimeter resolution without ionizing radiation.

Thin-Film Optical Coatings

\[n_{\text{film}}=\sqrt{n_1n_2},\qquad t=\frac{\lambda}{4\,n_{\text{film}}}\]

Quarter-wave layers produce destructive interference of reflections. Modern DSLR lenses use 7–14 layers, reducing each surface from 4% → <0.1% reflection.

Metamaterials & Negative Index

\[n_{\text{eff}}=-\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\text{eff}}\,\mu_{\text{eff}}}\quad\text{when both }\varepsilon_{\text{eff}},\mu_{\text{eff}}\lt 0\]

Double-negative metamaterials reverse Snell's law, enable sub-diffraction imaging (superlens), and are actively researched for EM cloaking and perfect absorbers.
