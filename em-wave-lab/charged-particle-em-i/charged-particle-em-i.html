The Lorentz Force

A charged particle moving through electric and magnetic fields experiences the Lorentz force — the fundamental law driving every trajectory in this simulation:

\[\mathbf F=\cP{q}\left(\cE{\mathbf E}+\cv{\mathbf v}\times\cB{\mathbf B}\right)\]

where \(\cP{q}\) is charge, \(\cE{\mathbf E}\) is electric field, \(\cv{\mathbf v}\) is velocity, and \(\cB{\mathbf B}\) is magnetic field.

Equations of Motion

By Newton's 2nd law (\(\mathbf F=m\mathbf a\)), dividing by mass gives the acceleration. In component form:

\[\begin{aligned} \frac{d\cX{v_x}}{dt}&=\frac{\cP{q}}{m}\left[\cE{E_x}+\cY{v_y}\,\cB{B_z}-\cZ{v_z}\,\cB{B_y}\right]\\[4pt] \frac{d\cY{v_y}}{dt}&=\frac{\cP{q}}{m}\left[\cE{E_y}+\cZ{v_z}\,\cB{B_x}-\cX{v_x}\,\cB{B_z}\right]\\[4pt] \frac{d\cZ{v_z}}{dt}&=\frac{\cP{q}}{m}\left[\cE{E_z}+\cX{v_x}\,\cB{B_y}-\cY{v_y}\,\cB{B_x}\right] \end{aligned}\]

The terms \(\cE{E_i}\) give electric acceleration; the cross-product terms \(\cv{\mathbf v}\times\cB{\mathbf B}\) are the magnetic deflection — always perpendicular to velocity.

Numerical Integration (RK4)

These 6 coupled ODEs are solved each step using 4th-order Runge-Kutta, a high-accuracy method:

\[\begin{aligned} k_1&=f(t,\,y)\\ k_2&=f\!\left(t+\tfrac h2,\ y+\tfrac h2 k_1\right)\\ k_3&=f\!\left(t+\tfrac h2,\ y+\tfrac h2 k_2\right)\\ k_4&=f\!\left(t+h,\ y+h\,k_3\right)\\ y_{n+1}&=y_n+\tfrac h6\left(k_1+2k_2+2k_3+k_4\right) \end{aligned}\]

Each frame advances the simulation by speed × dt seconds. Smaller dt → more accuracy; larger speed → faster playback.

Energy
⚡ The magnetic force does NO work — since \(\cB{\mathbf F_B}=q(\cv{\mathbf v}\times\cB{\mathbf B})\) is always perpendicular to \(\cv{\mathbf v}\), it cannot change the particle's speed.

Only the electric field changes kinetic energy:
\(\dfrac{d\,KE}{dt}=q\,\cE{\mathbf E}\cdot\cv{\mathbf v}\)

This means with \(\mathbf E=0\), the particle's speed \(|\cv{\mathbf v}|\) is conserved — only its direction changes.

Special Cases by Preset
🔵 Cyclotron Motion
\(\mathbf E=0,\ \mathbf B\parallel\hat{\mathbf z},\ \mathbf v\perp\mathbf B\)

Pure magnetic field causes circular orbit in the XY plane. The Lorentz force acts as centripetal force:

\[\begin{aligned} r&=\frac{m\,|\cv{v_\perp}|}{|\cP{q}|\,|\cB{B}|} &&\text{Larmor radius}\\[3pt] \omega&=\frac{|\cP{q}|\,|\cB{B}|}{m} &&\text{cyclotron frequency}\\[3pt] T&=\frac{2\pi m}{|\cP{q}|\,|\cB{B}|} &&\text{period} \end{aligned}\]

Notice: the period is independent of velocity — the basis of the cyclotron particle accelerator.

🌀 Helical Motion
\(\mathbf E=0,\ \mathbf B\parallel\hat{\mathbf z}\), v has a Z component

When velocity has a component parallel to B, that component is unaffected by the magnetic force (\(\mathbf v_\parallel\times\mathbf B=0\)). The particle spirals along the field line:

\[\text{pitch}=\cv{v_\parallel}\,T=\frac{2\pi\,\cv{v_\parallel}\,m}{|\cP{q}|\,|\cB{B}|}\]
➡ E×B Drift
\(\mathbf E\perp\mathbf B\) (e.g. E along Y, B along Z)

When E and B are perpendicular, the guiding center drifts in the E×B direction, regardless of q or m:

\[\cv{\mathbf v}_{\text{drift}}=\frac{\cE{\mathbf E}\times\cB{\mathbf B}}{|\cB{\mathbf B}|^2}\]

This universal drift (same for ions and electrons) is key to plasma confinement physics.

〜 Cycloid Motion
\(\mathbf E\perp\mathbf B\), particle starts at rest

Starting from rest with crossed fields, the particle traces a cycloid — a series of loops like a point on a rolling wheel. The guiding center drifts at \(v_d=E/B\), while the instantaneous speed oscillates between 0 and \(2E/B\).

⚡ E + B (oblique E)
\(\mathbf E\perp\mathbf B\), with initial velocity

With a velocity already present, the particle gyrates about B while its guiding center drifts at \(\mathbf E\times\mathbf B/B^2\). Because \(\mathbf E\perp\mathbf B\), the kinetic energy oscillates rather than growing without bound; sustained acceleration requires a component of \(\mathbf E\) parallel to \(\mathbf B\).

🌐 Crossed Fields (3D)
E and B both in oblique directions

With all field components non-zero, the trajectory is a complex 3D path combining drift, spiraling, and acceleration along the part of E parallel to B. Numerical integration is the practical way to follow it.

Key Relationships
\[\begin{aligned} r&=\frac{m\,v_\perp}{|q|\,B} &&\text{Larmor radius}\\[3pt] \omega&=\frac{|q|\,B}{m} &&\text{cyclotron frequency}\\[3pt] v_d&=\frac{E}{B} &&\text{drift speed } (\mathbf E\perp\mathbf B)\\[3pt] \Delta KE&=q\,\mathbf E\cdot\Delta\mathbf x &&\text{work by } \mathbf E \end{aligned}\]
⚠ Simulation uses normalized units (not SI). Scale factors for q, m, E, B are arbitrary — only their ratios determine the trajectory shape.

Parameter Guide
Particle
mMass — heavier particles have larger Larmor radius and lower cyclotron frequency.
qCharge — positive curves one way, negative the other. Zero charge means no force at all (electric or magnetic), so the particle moves in a straight line at constant velocity.
Fields
EElectric field — accelerates particle along field direction. Does work → changes KE.
BMagnetic field — deflects perpendicular to motion. Does no work → speed unchanged.
Simulation Quality
dtTime step — smaller = higher accuracy, slower. Use ≤0.01 for smooth orbits at high field strength.
spdSpeed multiplier — steps per animation frame. Higher values run faster but don't affect accuracy.
trailTrail length — how many past positions to display. Long trails reveal periodic structure.
