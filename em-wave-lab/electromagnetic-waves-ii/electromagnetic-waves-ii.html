Maxwell's Equations

The Foundation of Electromagnetism

Four compact equations by James Clerk Maxwell (1865) unify electricity, magnetism, and optics, and predict EM wave propagation at the speed of light.

\[ \begin{aligned} \nabla\cdot\mathbf{E} &= \frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0} && \text{Gauss (electric): charges create } \mathbf{E}\\ \nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} &= 0 && \text{Gauss (magnetic): no monopoles}\\ \nabla\times\mathbf{E} &= -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t} && \text{Faraday: changing } \mathbf{B} \text{ induces } \mathbf{E}\\ \nabla\times\mathbf{B} &= \mu_0\mathbf{J}+\mu_0\varepsilon_0\frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t} && \text{Ampère–Maxwell} \end{aligned} \]

⚡ Electric Field E

Force per unit charge, measured in V/m. It oscillates transverse to propagation, and its curl equals \(-\partial\mathbf{B}/\partial t\).

🧲 Magnetic Field B

Force on moving charges, measured in tesla. It is always perpendicular to \(\mathbf{E}\) and \(\mathbf{k}\), with \(B_0=E_0/c\) in vacuum.

Wave Equation & Propagation

Transverse EM Waves

\[ \frac{\partial^2 \mathbf{E}}{\partial z^2}=\mu_0\varepsilon_0\,\frac{\partial^2 \mathbf{E}}{\partial t^2} \] \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{E}(z,t) &= E_0\cos(kz-\omega t)\,\hat{\mathbf{x}}\\ \mathbf{B}(z,t) &= \frac{E_0}{c}\cos(kz-\omega t)\,\hat{\mathbf{y}} \end{aligned} \] \[ c=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}}=2.998\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s},\qquad k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda},\quad \omega=2\pi f,\quad c=f\lambda=\frac{\omega}{k} \] \[ \text{In a medium of index } n:\quad v=\frac{c}{n},\qquad \lambda_m=\frac{\lambda_0}{n} \]
Reflection & Refraction

Fresnel Equations & Snell's Law

\[ n_1\sin\theta_i = n_2\sin\theta_t \qquad\text{(Snell)} \] \[ \begin{aligned} r_s &= \frac{n_1\cos\theta_i-n_2\cos\theta_t}{n_1\cos\theta_i+n_2\cos\theta_t}, & t_s &= \frac{2n_1\cos\theta_i}{n_1\cos\theta_i+n_2\cos\theta_t}\\[4pt] r_p &= \frac{n_2\cos\theta_i-n_1\cos\theta_t}{n_2\cos\theta_i+n_1\cos\theta_t}, & t_p &= \frac{2n_1\cos\theta_i}{n_2\cos\theta_i+n_1\cos\theta_t} \end{aligned} \] \[ R=|r|^2,\qquad T=\frac{n_2\cos\theta_t}{n_1\cos\theta_i}\,|t|^2,\qquad R+T=1 \] \[ \tan\theta_B=\frac{n_2}{n_1}\ \text{(Brewster)},\qquad \sin\theta_c=\frac{n_2}{n_1}\ \text{(TIR, } n_1>n_2) \]

The \(s\)-polarization (TE) has \(\mathbf{E}\) perpendicular to the plane of incidence; the \(p\)-polarization (TM) has \(\mathbf{E}\) in that plane.

Interference

Superposition Principle

\[ \mathbf{E}_{tot}=\mathbf{E}_1+\mathbf{E}_2,\qquad I=\tfrac12 c\varepsilon_0E_0^2\propto E_0^2 \] \[ I_{tot}=I_1+I_2+2\sqrt{I_1I_2}\,\cos\Delta\varphi \] \[ \begin{aligned} \text{Constructive: } \Delta\varphi&=2m\pi &\Rightarrow I_{max}&=(\sqrt{I_1}+\sqrt{I_2})^2\\ \text{Destructive: } \Delta\varphi&=(2m+1)\pi &\Rightarrow I_{min}&=(\sqrt{I_1}-\sqrt{I_2})^2 \end{aligned} \] \[ V=\frac{I_{max}-I_{min}}{I_{max}+I_{min}} \]
Polarization

Orientation of E-field Oscillation

\[ \mathbf{E}(z,t)=E_x\cos(kz-\omega t)\,\hat{\mathbf{x}}+E_y\cos(kz-\omega t+\delta)\,\hat{\mathbf{y}} \] \[ \begin{aligned} \text{Linear:}&\quad \delta=0\ \text{or}\ \pi &&\text{(E traces a line)}\\ \text{Circular:}&\quad E_x=E_y,\ \delta=\pm\tfrac{\pi}{2} &&\text{(E traces a circle, a helix in 3D)}\\ \text{Elliptical:}&\quad \text{general } E_x,E_y,\delta &&\text{(E traces an ellipse)} \end{aligned} \] \[ I=I_0\cos^2\theta\qquad\text{(Malus's law)} \] \[ S_0=E_x^2+E_y^2,\quad S_1=E_x^2-E_y^2,\quad S_2=2E_xE_y\cos\delta,\quad S_3=2E_xE_y\sin\delta \]
Energy & Momentum

Poynting Vector

\[ \mathbf{S}=\frac{1}{\mu_0}\,\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}\qquad[\mathrm{W/m^2}] \] \[ \langle S\rangle=\tfrac12 c\varepsilon_0E_0^2,\qquad u=\frac{\varepsilon_0E^2}{2}+\frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}\ \ (\text{electric}=\text{magnetic share}) \] \[ P_{rad}=\frac{I}{c}\ \text{(absorber)},\qquad P_{rad}=\frac{2I}{c}\ \text{(mirror)} \]
Applications of Electromagnetic Waves

🔆 Fiber Optic Communication

Total internal reflection (TIR) at core/cladding interface guides photons over thousands of km with <0.2 dB/km loss at 1550 nm.

  • Core \(n\approx1.48\), cladding \(n\approx1.46\) → \(\theta_c\approx80.6^\circ\)
  • Single-mode (Ø8 µm) vs multi-mode (Ø50 µm)
  • WDM: 160+ channels per fiber (50 THz bandwidth)
  • EDFA amplifiers every 80 km eliminate regeneration
  • Capacity: >1 Pb/s on transoceanic cables

📡 Antenna Theory

Accelerating charges radiate EM waves. A half-wave dipole (length = λ/2) resonates and radiates efficiently. Gain describes directional concentration of power.

  • Dipole: 2.15 dBi, omnidirectional
  • Yagi-Uda: 6–20 dBi, directional
  • Parabolic dish: 20–50+ dBi
  • Phased array: electronic beam steering (MIMO, 5G)
  • \(A_\text{eff}=G\lambda^2/4\pi\), reciprocal for TX and RX

📻 Radio & Microwave Systems

EM waves from kHz to GHz carry modulated information across continental and global distances by sky-wave reflection and line-of-sight links.

  • HF (3–30 MHz): ionospheric sky-wave, global reach
  • Wi-Fi 6E: 2.4/5/6 GHz, OFDM modulation
  • 5G mmWave: 24–100 GHz, massive MIMO
  • Radar: pulse reflection for ranging and speed
  • Satellite Ku-band: 12–18 GHz downlink

🏥 Medical Imaging

Different wavelengths probe tissue with varying penetration depth and resolution, from radio-frequency MRI pulses to hard X-rays.

  • MRI: 64 MHz (1.5 T) RF pulses, Larmor precession
  • X-ray CT: 10⁻¹¹ m, photoelectric absorption contrast
  • OCT: 1300 nm, 10 µm axial resolution in retina
  • Infrared thermography: 8–14 µm surface temperature
  • Photoacoustic: pulsed 700 nm + ultrasound detection

🌞 Photovoltaics & Solar

Semiconductors absorb photons above their bandgap energy (E = hf), liberating electron-hole pairs and generating voltage via the built-in p-n junction field.

  • AM1.5 solar spectrum peaks at ~500 nm, 1000 W/m²
  • Si bandgap 1.12 eV → \(\lambda_\text{max}=hc/E_g\approx1100\) nm
  • Multi-junction GaInP/GaAs/Ge: >47% efficiency
  • Perovskite tandems: 33%+ certified efficiency
  • Concentrating PV uses Fresnel lens reflection optics

🔭 Telescopes & Observatories

Optical instruments exploit diffraction-limited imaging across the full EM spectrum — from 10 m radio dishes to X-ray grazing-incidence mirrors in space.

  • VLA radio array: 27 dishes, 36 km baseline
  • JWST: 6.5 m primary mirror, 0.6–28 µm
  • Adaptive optics: 1 kHz deformable mirror corrections
  • ELT (39 m): resolves planets around nearby stars
  • VLBI: Earth-baseline interferometry, µas resolution
