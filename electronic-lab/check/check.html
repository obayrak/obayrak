🎨 Resistor

A passive component that limits current flow. Measured in ohms (Ω). Tested with multimeter in resistance mode — always de-powered.

Step-by-step procedure
  1. !Power OFF the circuit completely. Never measure resistance in a live circuit — damage guaranteed.
  2. 1Set multimeter dial to Ω (resistance) mode. Select a range above the expected value or use auto-range.
  3. 2Discharge any nearby capacitors. Touch probes together — display should read near 0 Ω (lead resistance).
  4. 3Touch the red probe to one resistor lead and black probe to the other. Polarity does not matter for resistors.
  5. 4Read the displayed value. It should be within the tolerance band of the color-coded value.
  6. 5If checking in-circuit, disconnect at least one lead to eliminate parallel paths that skew the reading.
⚠️ Never apply voltage while measuring resistance. The meter applies its own small internal voltage. External voltage destroys the meter.

⚡ Capacitor

Stores electrical charge between two conductive plates. Measured in farads (F). Check with multimeter in capacitance mode or observe charge/discharge behavior.

Step-by-step procedure
  1. !Fully discharge capacitor first: short leads with a 1kΩ resistor for several seconds. Never short directly — spark risk.
  2. 1Set multimeter to capacitance mode (F symbol). Choose a range above the rated value.
  3. 2For electrolytic caps: insert into meter's cap socket (CX). Match polarity — + lead to + terminal.
  4. 3Alternatively: touch red probe to positive lead (+, longer leg or marked side), black to negative.
  5. 4Read the capacitance. Typical tolerance is ±20%, so 100µF cap reads 80–120µF.
  6. 5For ESR (equivalent series resistance) testing, use a dedicated ESR meter or LCR meter.
⚠️ Large electrolytic caps (1000µF+) hold lethal charge in high-voltage circuits. Always discharge before handling. Never reverse polarity.

🌀 Inductor

Stores energy in a magnetic field. Measured in henries (H). Basic check: continuity + DC resistance. Full inductance requires an LCR meter.

Continuity modeResistance (Ω)LCR meter (H)
Probe animation
RESISTANCE MODE (DCR check)
2.4Ω
Continuity beeps ON | coil is intact
Step-by-step procedure
  1. !Inductors in power circuits can hold high back-EMF voltages. Power off and discharge any associated caps.
  2. 1Continuity test first: set meter to continuity (beep) mode. Touch probes to each lead. Coil should beep — confirms winding is intact.
  3. 2DC resistance: switch to Ω mode. Measure DCR. Should be low — milliohms to a few ohms. Check datasheet.
  4. 3Inductance: requires an LCR meter. Set to L mode and measure in henries or millihenries.
  5. 4Check for shorted turns: DCR significantly lower than datasheet indicates shorted windings.
  6. 5Visually inspect for burn marks, cracked core, or loose wire on toroid/bobbin.
⚠️ Switching an inductor off abruptly causes a voltage spike (back-EMF) that can be many times the supply voltage. Always include a flyback diode in inductive circuits.

▶ Diode

Allows current in one direction only. Test with diode mode: forward biased shows ~0.6V (silicon), reverse biased shows OL.

Diode modeForward voltage VfReverse blocking
Forward / reverse bias animation
Step-by-step procedure
  1. !Remove diode from circuit or disconnect one end to avoid parallel paths affecting reading.
  2. 1Set multimeter to diode mode (symbol: ▶|). Meter applies small forward current (~1 mA) and measures voltage.
  3. 2Forward bias: Touch red to Anode (+, unmarked end), black to Cathode (−, banded end).
  4. 3Meter should display 0.5–0.7V (silicon) or 0.2–0.3V (germanium).
  5. 4Reverse bias: Swap probes. Meter should show OL (open, no current).
  6. 5If both directions show 0 → shorted. Both show OL → open. Both = replace.
⚠️ Never exceed reverse breakdown voltage (PIV). Zener diodes intentionally conduct in reverse — test them in Ω mode or with a variable voltage source.

🔺 BJT Transistor

Bipolar Junction Transistor — NPN or PNP. Two diode junctions (B-E and B-C). Test each junction like a diode. Also measure hFE (current gain).

Diode junction testhFE (gain) modeNPN / PNP
BJT junction diagram
B-E JUNCTION — DIODE MODE
0.618V
NPN: Red→B, Black→E
Step-by-step procedure
  1. !Identify pinout from datasheet. Common TO-92: EBC or CBE left-to-right (varies by part).
  2. 1Set to diode mode. Test B-E: Red→Base, Black→Emitter. Expect 0.55–0.70V (silicon NPN).
  3. 2Test B-C: Red→Base, Black→Collector. Expect similar 0.55–0.70V.
  4. 3Reverse both tests: should show OL. Any low reading = shorted junction.
  5. 4hFE gain: Insert into meter's hFE socket. Switch to hFE mode. Typical: 100–500.
  6. 5For PNP: all probe polarities reverse. Red→Emitter, Black→Base for B-E junction forward.
⚠️ High-power transistors (TO-220, TO-3) may have collector connected to the metal tab. Isolate from heatsink before testing.

🔷 MOSFET

Voltage-controlled transistor. Test body diode, gate isolation, and channel resistance (Rds_on). N-channel and P-channel types.

Body diode testGate insulationRds_on check
MOSFET symbol & gate control
0.0 V
Gate = 0V → channel OFF → OL drain-to-source
Step-by-step procedure
  1. !Static electricity can destroy gate oxide. Handle with ESD precautions — touch grounded metal before handling.
  2. 1Body diode test: Set to diode mode. Red→Source, Black→Drain (N-ch). Expect ~0.5–0.7V.
  3. 2Reverse: Red→Drain, Black→Source. Should show OL.
  4. 3Gate isolation: Red→Gate, Black→Source. Meter should show OL (very high resistance — MΩ).
  5. 4Threshold test: Briefly touch Gate to Drain. Then measure D-S in Ω mode — should show low resistance (channel ON).
  6. 5Touch Gate to Source to discharge → channel turns OFF → D-S returns to OL.
⚠️ Floating gate = unpredictable behavior. Always include a gate pull-down (10kΩ to GND) in MOSFET circuits.

💡 LED

Light-Emitting Diode. Forward voltage Vf varies by color (1.8V red to 3.3V blue/white). Always use current-limiting resistor.

Diode modeForward voltage VfCurrent limiting
LED probe animation
Step-by-step procedure
  1. !Never connect LED directly to voltage source without a current-limiting resistor. Exceeding max current destroys the LED instantly.
  2. 1Set multimeter to diode mode. The meter's ~1–2mA test current is safe and may briefly light the LED.
  3. 2Touch red probe to longer leg (anode +) and black to shorter leg (cathode −).
  4. 3LED may dim-glow. Meter shows Vf — 1.8–2.2V (red/yellow/green), 2.8–3.4V (blue/white).
  5. 4Swap probes → should show OL.
  6. 5R calc: R = (Vsupply − Vf) / If. For 5V, green LED (2.1V), 20mA: R = (5−2.1)/0.02 = 145Ω → use 150Ω.
⚠️ High-power LEDs (1W+) generate significant heat. Use proper heatsinking. Thermal runaway can occur if current is not limited.

🔌 Fuse

Overcurrent protection device. A good fuse reads 0Ω, a blown fuse reads OL. Never bypass a blown fuse without finding the root cause.

Continuity modeResistance (Ω)
Good fuse vs blown fuse animation
Step-by-step procedure
  1. !Remove fuse from its holder before testing. Never test in-circuit — parallel paths give false readings.
  2. 1Visually inspect fuse first. Blown glass fuses often show burn marks or broken wire inside.
  3. 2Set multimeter to continuity mode. Touch probes to each end cap of the fuse.
  4. 3Good fuse: meter beeps and shows 0.1–0.5 Ω.
  5. 4Blown fuse: no beep, shows OL.
  6. 5Before replacing, find WHY it blew. Replace with same amperage AND voltage rating. Never uprate.
⚠️ NEVER replace a blown fuse with a higher-amperage fuse or short it with wire. The fuse protects wiring from fire.

🔘 Switch

Mechanical contact device. Open = OL, closed = near-0Ω and beep. Test all positions and poles.

Continuity modeContact resistanceAll poles
Switch state animation
Step-by-step procedure
  1. !Power OFF. Disconnect at least one wire from the switch to avoid false readings from parallel paths.
  2. 1Set multimeter to continuity mode (beep).
  3. 2Probes across COM and NO. In open position: meter must show OL, no beep.
  4. 3Actuate to closed position: meter must beep and show near-0Ω (<1Ω is good).
  5. 4For DPDT or multi-pole switches: test each pole independently.
  6. 5Rapidly toggle switch while monitoring. Intermittent continuity indicates worn contacts — replace.
⚠️ High-voltage / high-current switches (relays, contactors) can arc dangerously when contacts open under load.

🖥️ IC Chip

Integrated circuits contain many transistors and components. Basic checks: power supply verification, static test of outputs, swap with known-good reference.

Step-by-step procedure
  1. !ICs can look fine but fail electrically. Hot to touch = bad. Cold when should be warm = not getting power. Always check supply first.
  2. 1Power ON. Set meter to DC voltage. Measure GND pin to VCC pin. Must equal supply voltage ±5%.
  3. 2Check supply current: if much higher than datasheet Icc, IC may be shorted internally.
  4. 3Probe input pins: verify correct logic levels. Floating inputs cause unpredictable behavior.
  5. 4Probe output pins with expected inputs applied. Compare to truth table or timing diagram.
  6. 5Swap test: replace IC with known-good identical part. If problem disappears → IC was faulty.
⚠️ CMOS ICs are ESD-sensitive. Handle with ESD wrist strap. Never insert/remove ICs while power is on.

🔋 Voltmeter

A voltmeter (or multimeter in V mode) measures electrical potential difference between two points. It operates in DC mode for batteries and circuits, AC mode for mains and signals. Always connect in parallel with the component under test — never in series.

DC mode: measures steady-state voltage polarity. Shows sign (+/−). Use for batteries, regulators, logic rails, sensor outputs.

Step-by-step procedure
  1. !A voltmeter is ALWAYS connected in parallel (across) the component or point you're measuring. Never in series — that creates a short through the meter.
  2. 1Insert probes: Red → V/VΩ jack, Black → COM jack. Select V DC or V AC mode on the dial.
  3. 2Range: if unknown, start at highest range and step down. Auto-range meters handle this automatically.
  4. 3Touch red probe to the positive point (higher potential) and black probe to the reference point (GND or lower potential).
  5. 4Read the display. A negative sign means polarity is reversed — swap probes if needed. For AC, sign doesn't apply.
  6. 5To measure voltage across a component in a live circuit: probe both leads of that component simultaneously while circuit is running.
  7. 6For AC mains (120V/230V): use CAT II or CAT III rated meter only. Keep probes away from each other and from ground while inserting.
⚠️ NEVER put a voltmeter in series in a circuit. The internal resistance is ~10MΩ — it will block all current. Also never exceed the meter's max input voltage (typically 600–1000V). AC mains can kill.

🔔 Buzzer

An electro-acoustic transducer that converts electrical energy into sound. Two types: active (internal oscillator — just apply DC voltage) and passive (requires an external AC/PWM signal to produce sound). Test with DC voltage and continuity/resistance check.

Buzzer type
Active buzzer: contains an internal oscillator. Apply rated DC voltage (typically 3–12V) and it immediately produces a fixed tone. Polarity matters — red/longer leg = +. Resistance: 16–100Ω.
Simulate test
Status: No voltage applied — buzzer silent
Step-by-step procedure
  1. !First determine if the buzzer is active (self-oscillating) or passive (needs AC/PWM signal). This determines the test method.
  2. 1Visual ID: Active buzzers usually have a sticker or sealed top. Check the datasheet or PCB silkscreen. Active: connect DC and it buzzes. Passive: silence with DC, needs PWM.
  3. 2Resistance check: Set meter to Ω mode. Measure across buzzer terminals. Active buzzer: 16–100Ω. Passive piezo: can be very high (MΩ) or low (8–32Ω for electromagnetic).
  4. 3DC voltage test (active): Set meter to V DC. Apply rated voltage (3V, 5V, or 12V — check label). Measure voltage at terminals. Should equal supply. Buzzer should emit tone.
  5. 4Passive buzzer test: Connect a signal generator or MCU PWM (1–4 kHz) to the buzzer. No DC-only tone. If still silent with PWM signal present at correct voltage → buzzer is faulty.
  6. 5Polarity check: Most buzzers are polarity-sensitive. Red wire or longer leg = positive (+). Reversing polarity on an active buzzer usually makes it silent (not damaged). Some electromagnetic types are non-polar.
  7. 6Continuity / quick test: Touch a 9V battery briefly to an active buzzer — it should beep immediately. This is the fastest functional test on the bench.
⚠️ Do not exceed the buzzer's rated voltage — this destroys the internal oscillator of active buzzers or the piezo disc of passive ones. Typical max is 12–15V.
