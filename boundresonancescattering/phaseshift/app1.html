The Partial Wave Expansion

For a central potential V(r), the scattering problem separates into partial waves labelled by angular momentum ℓ. Each partial wave contributes a phase shift δℓ to the scattering amplitude:

f(θ) = (1/k) Σℓ (2ℓ+1) eiδℓ sin(δℓ) Pℓ(cosθ)

The differential cross section is dσ/dΩ = |f(θ)|².

Radial Schrödinger Equation

The reduced radial wavefunction u(r) obeys the effective-potential form

-ℏ²/2μ u''(r) + [Veff(r)] u(r) = E u(r)

where Veff(r) = V(r) + ℓ(ℓ+1) ħ²/(2μ r²) + VC(r) and V(r) is the chosen nuclear/short-range potential.

The Three Computational Methods for δℓ

The simulator implements and cross-validates three independent methods. For piecewise-constant potentials (Step / Square Well) all three are exactly identical to machine precision. For all other potentials only the numerical method is used.

1. Numerical (Wronskian Method)
The radial equation is integrated with the 4th-order Numerov algorithm (h = 0.005 fm, rmax = 200 fm). At two distant points (r₁ = 90 fm, r₂ = 110 fm) the Wronskian eliminates the unknown amplitude:
tan δℓ = [u(r₁) ĵℓ(kr₂) − u(r₂) ĵℓ(kr₁)] / [u(r₁) n̂ℓ(kr₂) − u(r₂) n̂ℓ(kr₁)]
Works for any potential shape, including Coulomb.
2. Analytical (Exact Transfer Matrix / R-matrix)
For piecewise-constant potentials the solution in each region is expressed exactly with Riccati-Bessel or modified Bessel functions. The logarithmic derivative L = u′/u is propagated analytically from the origin to the outer radius. The final matching gives the exact phase shift.

3. Single-point R-matrix
Uses the numerical solution but evaluates L = (du/dr)/u at a single large radius (r = 100 fm) and matches directly to the free outgoing wave. Numerically indistinguishable from the Wronskian result.

Numerov Algorithm

Fourth-order implicit integrator for u'' = f(r)u:

un+1 = [2un(1 + 5h²fn/12) − un-1(1 − h²fn-1/12)] / (1 − h²fn+1/12)

Levinson's Theorem

δℓ(E=0) = nb π, where nb is the number of bound states in partial wave ℓ. As E → ∞, δℓ → 0.
