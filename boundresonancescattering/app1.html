Equations and methods

Writing \(\psi(\mathbf r) = \dfrac{u(r)}{r}\,Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi)\), the radial equation for the relative motion of two particles with reduced mass \(\mu = m_1 m_2/(m_1+m_2)\) is

\[ -\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}\,\frac{d^2u}{dr^2} + \left[ V(r) + \frac{\hbar^2\,\ell(\ell+1)}{2\mu r^2} \right] u(r) = E\,u(r), \qquad \frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu} = \frac{(\hbar c)^2}{2\mu c^2}. \]

Bound states \((E < 0)\)

Finite differences (FD). With a uniform grid \(r_i = r_0 + i h\) the second derivative becomes \(u''_i \approx (u_{i+1} - 2u_i + u_{i-1})/h^2\), so the equation is a symmetric tridiagonal eigenvalue problem. Its eigenvalues are found by Sturm-sequence bisection, the eigenvectors by inverse iteration.

Numerov shooting. Writing \(u'' = g(r)\,u\) with \(g = \ell(\ell+1)/r^2 + (V-E)\,2\mu/\hbar^2\), the sixth-order step

\[ \left(1 - \tfrac{h^2}{12}g_{i+1}\right)u_{i+1} = 2\left(1 + \tfrac{5h^2}{12}g_i\right)u_i - \left(1 - \tfrac{h^2}{12}g_{i-1}\right)u_{i-1} \]

is integrated outward and the nodes are counted; the energy at which the count changes is the eigenvalue. RK4 does the same with the classical fourth-order Runge–Kutta method for \((u, u')\). Near a Coulomb singularity both start from the exact behaviour \(u \approx r^{\ell+1}\left[1 + \dfrac{\mu\, rV(r)}{(\ell+1)\hbar^2}\,r\right]\).

Resonances (complex \(E\))

A resonance is a solution with purely outgoing behaviour, \(u \propto e^{ikr}\), at complex energy

\[ E = E_R - \frac{i}{2}\Gamma, \qquad \tau = \frac{\hbar}{\Gamma}. \]

In exterior complex scaling the coordinate beyond \(R_0\) is rotated, \(r = R_0 + (x - R_0)\,e^{i\theta}\); outgoing waves then decay and the resonance becomes an ordinary eigenvalue. FD uses shifted inverse iteration; Numerov and RK4 match the inner and the outgoing solutions at \(R_0\) and solve for complex \(E\) with the secant method. Very narrow resonances \((\Gamma/E < 10^{-3})\) are located from the real-energy phase shift: \(\delta(E_R) = \pi/2 \pmod \pi\) and \(\Gamma = 2\big/ \tfrac{d\delta}{dE}\).

Scattering \((E > 0)\)

For a short-range potential the solution is matched to the Riccati–Bessel functions,

\[ u_\ell(r) \;\propto\; \hat\jmath_\ell(kr)\cos\delta_\ell - \hat y_\ell(kr)\sin\delta_\ell \;\xrightarrow{\;r\to\infty\;}\; \sin\!\left(kr - \tfrac{\ell\pi}{2} + \delta_\ell\right), \qquad k = \frac{\sqrt{2\mu E}}{\hbar}, \]

and the multiple of \(\pi\) is fixed by counting nodes, so that Levinson’s theorem \(\delta_\ell(0) - \delta_\ell(\infty) = N_\ell\,\pi\) counts the bound states. Then

\[ \sigma = \frac{4\pi}{k^2}\sum_\ell (2\ell+1)\sin^2\delta_\ell, \qquad S_\ell = e^{2i\delta_\ell}, \qquad k\cot\delta_0 = -\frac{1}{a} + \frac{r_0}{2}k^2 + \dots \]

With a Coulomb tail the nuclear phase shift is the phase difference between the full solution and the pure-Coulomb solution far outside the nucleus.
