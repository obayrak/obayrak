Theory

Quantum Potential Solver — Theory & Reference

This tab documents the equations actually used by the Bound States, Resonance States, and Scattering States solvers. The numerical problem is the radial Schrödinger equation for the reduced radial wavefunction \(u(r)=rR(r)\), with a centrifugal contribution included whenever \(\ell>0\).

Primary convention
The code sets \( \hbar^2/(2m)=1 \). Therefore \(k=\sqrt{E}\) for positive-energy scattering and \(\kappa=\sqrt{-E}\) for negative-energy bound states. Energies and lengths are dimensionless numerical quantities; the labels \(V_0\) and \(a\) are scale references rather than universal physical units.
Bound
Real negative-energy eigenvalues from regular-origin shooting and boundary matching.
Resonance
Quasi-bound states characterised by a complex pole \(E_R-i\Gamma/2\).
Scattering
Positive-energy phase shifts, \(S_\ell\), partial cross sections and amplitudes.
Important correction
The previous theory text incorrectly called the Numerov method “\(O(h^6)\) accuracy” without distinguishing local truncation from global convergence, and it gave an incomplete Kratzer spectrum. This version states the orders and exact formulas consistently with the implementation.

Radial Schrödinger Equation

Separation of variables

$$ \psi(\mathbf r)=R_\ell(r)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi),\qquad u_\ell(r)=rR_\ell(r). $$

The substitution \(u=rR\) removes the first-derivative term from the radial equation. The physical radial probability is \( |u(r)|^2\,dr \).

Radial equation

$$ -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2u}{dr^2} +\left[ V(r)+\frac{\hbar^2\ell(\ell+1)}{2mr^2} \right]u =Eu. $$
$$ V_{\rm eff}(r)=V(r)+\frac{\hbar^2\ell(\ell+1)}{2mr^2}. $$

Dimensionless equation used by the code

$$ \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}=1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad -u''(r)+V_{\rm eff}(r)u(r)=Eu(r), $$ $$ u''(r)=\bigl[V_{\rm eff}(r)-E\bigr]u(r). $$

Origin boundary condition

$$ u(0)=0,\qquad u(r)\propto r^{\ell+1}\quad(r\rightarrow0). $$

This regular solution is essential for the radial problem. For singular \(1/r^2\) potentials, the local power changes according to the singular coefficient; the implementation treats the ordinary Kratzer singularity specially when constructing the Numerov initial behaviour.

Normalisation

$$ \int_0^\infty |u(r)|^2\,dr=1. $$

For bound states the displayed wavefunction is normalised numerically with composite Simpson integration on the uniform grid. Scattering and Gamow states are not ordinary square-integrable bound states, so their plotted amplitudes should not be interpreted as unit-probability bound-state normalisations.

Numerical Methods

Numerov equation

The code writes the ODE as \(u''=f(r)u\), with

$$ f_i=\frac{V_{\rm eff}(r_i)-E}{\hbar^2/(2m)}. $$
$$ u_{i+1}= \frac{ 2u_i\left(1-\frac{5h^2f_i}{12}\right) -u_{i-1}\left(1+\frac{h^2f_{i-1}}{12}\right) }{ 1+\frac{h^2f_{i+1}}{12} }. $$
Order of accuracy
For a smooth equation of the Numerov form, the local truncation error is \(O(h^6)\), while the usual global solution error is \(O(h^4)\). Thus “\(O(h^6)\)” must not be reported as the global convergence order.

Shooting and root finding

$$ F(E)=u(r_{\max};E). $$

A bound-state eigenvalue is located where the regular solution satisfies the outer boundary condition. The code scans the energy range for sign changes and refines candidate roots with bisection. The finite numerical \(r_{\max}\) means \(F(E)=0\) is a finite-domain approximation to the true condition \(u(\infty)=0\).

Finite-difference and RK4 checks

The convergence tables can compare finite-difference, Numerov and RK4 calculations. Agreement between independent discretisations is a numerical consistency check; it is not, by itself, an analytical proof.

Grid and convergence controls

$$ h=\frac{r_{\max}-r_{\min}}{N-1}. $$

The selected \(r_{\min}\), \(r_{\max}\), grid size \(N\), and tolerance determine the numerical domain and root refinement. Singular potentials require particular care with \(r_{\min}\); moving it too close to zero can make the \(1/r^2\) term dominate the discretisation.

Bound States

Physical condition
A bound state has real \(E\) below the continuum threshold and is square-integrable. For the implemented potentials whose asymptotic value is zero, this normally means \(E<0\).

Asymptotic form

$$ u(r)\sim e^{-\kappa r},\qquad \kappa=\frac{\sqrt{2m|E|}}{\hbar}=\sqrt{|E|} \quad\text{in the code units}. $$

Quantisation

$$ u(0)=0,\qquad u(r\rightarrow\infty)=0. $$

The radial quantum number \(n_r\) counts interior nodes of the regular reduced radial wavefunction. The ground state in a fixed \(\ell\) channel has \(n_r=0\). Increasing \(\ell\) adds the repulsive centrifugal term and generally raises the eigenvalues and can remove bound states.

Infinite spherical well — exact benchmark

$$ V(r)= \begin{cases} 0,&0<r<a,\\ \infty,&r\ge a, \end{cases} \qquad j_\ell(ka)=0. $$
$$ k_{n\ell}a=x_{n\ell}, \qquad E_{n\ell}=\frac{x_{n\ell}^2}{a^2}, $$

Here \(x_{n\ell}\) is the \(n\)-th positive zero of the spherical Bessel function \(j_\ell\). This is the cleanest bound-state verification because the spectrum is known exactly.

Morse full-line reference

$$ V(r)=D_e\left[\left(1-e^{-\alpha(r-r_e)}\right)^2-1\right], $$ $$ E_v=-D_e+2\alpha\sqrt{D_e}\left(v+\frac12\right) -\alpha^2\left(v+\frac12\right)^2. $$

This closed form is exact for the standard full-line Morse problem in the code's \(\hbar^2/(2m)=1\) convention. Because the numerical radial problem is restricted to \(r\ge0\), it is a reference rather than an exact radial benchmark in general.

Kratzer exact spectrum — corrected

$$ V(r)=D_e\left(\frac{r_e^2}{r^2}-\frac{2r_e}{r}\right), $$ $$ \lambda_\ell= \sqrt{\left(\ell+\frac12\right)^2+D_e r_e^2}, $$ $$ \boxed{ E_{n_r\ell} = -\frac{(D_e r_e)^2} {\left(n_r+\frac12+\lambda_\ell\right)^2} }. $$

The squared numerator \((D_e r_e)^2\) is essential. The earlier theory card omitted one factor of \(D_e\). The formula above matches the analytic spectrum used by the code. Equivalently, defining \(\beta=\lambda_\ell-\tfrac12\), the denominator is \(n_r+\beta+\tfrac12\).

Resonance States

Physical picture
A resonance is a metastable state embedded in the continuum. It is localised temporarily by an effective barrier and decays by outgoing flux.

Complex-energy definition

$$ E_{\rm pole}=E_R-\frac{i\Gamma}{2}. $$
$$ \Gamma=-2\,\operatorname{Im}E_{\rm pole}, \qquad \tau=\frac{\hbar}{\Gamma}. $$

In the code units \(\hbar=1\) and \(\hbar^2/(2m)=1\), so the displayed lifetime is \(\tau=1/\Gamma\).

Barrier picture

$$ V_{\rm eff}(r)=V(r)+\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2} \qquad(\hbar^2/2m=1). $$

For a centrifugal resonance, a pocket plus barrier can trap the wave temporarily. The resonance energy is generally associated with a rapid phase variation and need not equal the barrier maximum exactly.

Phase-shift signature

$$ \delta_\ell(E)\approx \delta_{\rm bg}(E)+ \arctan\!\left(\frac{\Gamma/2}{E_R-E}\right), $$
$$ \left.\frac{d\delta_\ell}{dE}\right|_{E_R} \approx \frac{2}{\Gamma} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\Gamma\approx \frac{2}{\left|d\delta_\ell/dE\right|_{E_R}} }. $$

The absolute value is appropriate for the numerical detector because an \(\operatorname{atan2}\)-based phase can jump branches. The derivative relation is a narrow, isolated Breit–Wigner approximation; it is not a universal identity for broad or overlapping resonances.

Breit–Wigner partial cross section

$$ \sigma_\ell(E)= \frac{4\pi(2\ell+1)}{k^2}\sin^2\delta_\ell(E), $$ $$ \sigma_\ell^{\rm BW}(E)\simeq \frac{4\pi(2\ell+1)}{k^2} \frac{(\Gamma/2)^2} {(E-E_R)^2+(\Gamma/2)^2}. $$

The second expression is the isolated-resonance approximation. The first is the exact elastic partial-wave expression used by the scattering calculation.

WKB interpretation

$$ \Gamma\propto \exp\!\left[-2\int_{r_2}^{r_3}\kappa(r)\,dr\right], \qquad \kappa(r)=\sqrt{V_{\rm eff}(r)-E_R}, $$

The proportionality hides a model-dependent assault-frequency/prefactor. Therefore the exponential is a qualitative tunnelling law, not the complete numerical width formula used by this app.

Complex poles versus real-axis resonances
A real-axis phase-shift peak and a complex pole are related but are not numerically identical procedures. The app's analytical complex-pole references should therefore only be compared with the numerical method that uses the same complex-scaled Hamiltonian and the same potential definition.

Scattering Theory

Positive-energy channel
For the implemented zero-asymptotic potentials, \(E>0\) gives \(k=\sqrt{E}\) in code units and an oscillatory asymptotic wave.

Asymptotic radial solution

$$ u_\ell(r)\longrightarrow A\left[ j_\ell(kr)\cos\delta_\ell -n_\ell(kr)\sin\delta_\ell \right]. $$

Since \(j_\ell\sim\sin(kr-\ell\pi/2)/(kr)\) and \(n_\ell\sim-\cos(kr-\ell\pi/2)/(kr)\), this corresponds to

$$ u_\ell(r)\propto \frac{\sin(kr-\ell\pi/2+\delta_\ell)}{k}. $$

Two-point phase extraction used in the code

$$ \tan\delta_\ell= \frac{ u_1j_\ell(kr_2)-u_2j_\ell(kr_1) }{ u_2n_\ell(kr_1)-u_1n_\ell(kr_2) }. $$

The code evaluates this at two late-grid points, approximately 88% and 94% of the numerical grid, where the solution is intended to be asymptotic. These points must lie beyond the effective interaction region for the extraction to be reliable.

S-matrix and partial amplitude

$$ S_\ell=e^{2i\delta_\ell}, \qquad |S_\ell|=1 \quad\text{for elastic scattering}, $$ $$ f_\ell=\frac{S_\ell-1}{2ik} =\frac{e^{i\delta_\ell}\sin\delta_\ell}{k}. $$

The displayed quantity \(T_\ell=1-S_\ell\) in the results panel is a diagnostic transition quantity in this app's convention. It should not be confused with the standard partial-wave \(T\)-matrix convention \(t_\ell=(S_\ell-1)/(2i)\).

Scattering amplitude and cross sections

$$ f(\theta)= \frac{1}{2ik} \sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty} (2\ell+1)(S_\ell-1)P_\ell(\cos\theta), $$ $$ \sigma_\ell(E)= \frac{4\pi(2\ell+1)}{k^2}\sin^2\delta_\ell, $$ $$ \sigma_{\rm tot}= \frac{4\pi}{k^2} \sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\sin^2\delta_\ell. $$

The last expression is the elastic total cross section. The partial-wave expression is a consequence of partial-wave unitarity; it should not be labelled “the optical theorem” by itself. The optical theorem is instead

$$ \sigma_{\rm tot}=\frac{4\pi}{k}\operatorname{Im}f(0). $$

Unitarity limit

$$ \sigma_\ell\le \frac{4\pi(2\ell+1)}{k^2}, $$ $$\sigma_\ell^{\max}= \frac{4\pi(2\ell+1)}{k^2} \quad\text{when}\quad \delta_\ell=\frac{\pi}{2}\;(\mathrm{mod}\ \pi). $$

Infinite spherical well phase shift

$$ j_\ell(ka)\cos\delta_\ell -n_\ell(ka)\sin\delta_\ell=0, $$ $$ \tan\delta_\ell=\frac{j_\ell(ka)}{n_\ell(ka)}. $$

The code chooses the branch \(\delta_\ell=-\operatorname{atan2}[j_\ell(ka),\,n_\ell(ka)]\), which differs from an unwrapped phase by integer multiples of \(\pi\). Since \(S_\ell=e^{2i\delta_\ell}\), branch changes by \(\pi\) do not change the physical \(S\)-matrix.

Conventions, Branches & Validity Limits

Units

$$ \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}=1,\qquad k=\sqrt{E}\;(E>0),\qquad \kappa=\sqrt{-E}\;(E<0). $$

Because the numerical Hamiltonian is dimensionless, the values shown as \(E\), \(V_0\), \(D_e\), \(C\), etc. are model parameters in the solver's chosen scale. A physical MeV/fm conversion requires an external choice of \(m\) and length scale.

Angular momentum

$$ V_{\rm cent}(r)=\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2}. $$

In the dimensionless convention this term is added directly to \(V(r)\). It diverges at the origin, so the numerical grid must start at a positive \(r_{\min}\).

Phase branch

Phase shifts obtained with \(\operatorname{atan2}\) are defined modulo \(\pi\). The physically invariant quantities \(S_\ell=e^{2i\delta_\ell}\) and \(\sin^2\delta_\ell\) are unaffected by \(\delta_\ell\rightarrow\delta_\ell+n\pi\). Derivatives \(d\delta/dE\) require an unwrapped, locally continuous branch.

Resonance interpretation

A peak in \(|d\delta/dE|\) is a practical real-axis resonance indicator, not a universal definition of a pole. For broad, overlapping, threshold, or strongly energy-dependent resonances, \(E_R\), \(\Gamma\), FWHM and pole parameters can differ.

Analytical verification policy

Like-for-like comparison
An analytical formula is a valid numerical benchmark only when the potential, radial domain, boundary condition, angular momentum term, energy convention and asymptotic condition are the same. This is why the Morse and IHO references carry explicit domain/scope warnings.

What the plots mean

Bound: \(u(r)\) is a normalised square-integrable state. Resonance: the complex wavefunction is associated with a pole and is not an ordinary bound-state probability density. Scattering: the wave oscillates asymptotically and the physically meaningful observables are \(\delta_\ell\), \(S_\ell\), \(f_\ell\), and cross sections.
