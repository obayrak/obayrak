1 · Two-Body Problem & Reduced Mass

The relative motion of two particles (masses m₁, m₂; charges Z₁, Z₂) interacting via a central potential V(r) reduces exactly to a one-body problem with reduced mass μ:

μ = m₁·m₂ / (m₁ + m₂) [MeV/c²] ħc = 197.3269804 MeV·fm (exact CODATA value) e² = α·ħc = 1.43996 MeV·fm Coulomb coupling α = 1/137.035999 fine-structure constant mp = 938.272 MeV/c², mn = 939.565 MeV/c², mα = 3727.38 MeV/c²

The centre-of-mass moves as a free particle (ignored). The relative wavefunction ψ(r) satisfies the Schrödinger equation with μ replacing the mass. For a central potential angular momentum is conserved; writing ψ = u(r)/r · Y_ℓm(θ,φ) yields the radial equation.

2 · Radial Schrödinger Equation

d²u/dr² = f(r)·u(r) f(r) = (2μc²/(ħc)²)·[V_eff(r) − E] [fm⁻²] V_eff(r) = V(r) + (ħc)²·ℓ(ℓ+1) / (2μc²·r²) └── centrifugal barrier ──┘ Unit check: 2μc² [MeV] / (ħc)² [MeV²·fm²] × V [MeV] = fm⁻² ✓ k = √(2μc²·E) / (ħc) [fm⁻¹] (wave number for E > 0)
Startup: u[0]=0 (always). u[1] = h^(ℓ+1) for regular solution u ~ r^(ℓ+1) near origin. This enforces the inner boundary condition automatically.

3 · Boundary Conditions — Three Regimes

Bound States (E < 0 or E < V(∞))

u(0) = 0 (regularity: wavefunction finite at origin) u(r→∞) = 0 (normalizable: exponential decay κ=√(2μ|E|)/ħc) u(r) ~ e^{−κr} for r > r_classical ← forbidden region Energy is quantised: only discrete E_n satisfy both BCs simultaneously

Resonance States (E = E_R − iΓ/2, complex)

u(0) = 0 (regularity) u(r→∞) ~ H_ℓ^+(kr) = n_ℓ(kr) + i·j_ℓ(kr) (purely outgoing Hankel) No incoming wave — Siegert (outgoing-only) boundary condition E_R = resonance energy, Γ = width, τ = ħ/Γ = lifetime Detected via: rapid π-jump in δ_ℓ(E) at E = E_R Γ = 2/(dδ/dE)|_{E=E_R} from peak of |dδ/dE|

Scattering States (E > 0, real)

u(0) = 0 (regularity) u(r→∞) ~ cos(δ_ℓ)·j_ℓ(kr) − sin(δ_ℓ)·n_ℓ(kr) (asymptotic) Phase shift δ_ℓ extracted by matching at two large-r points S_ℓ = e^{2iδ_ℓ} (S-matrix element, |S_ℓ|=1 for real V)

4 · Numerov Method

Optimised 4th-order method for equations of the form u'' = f(r)·u (no first derivative). Achieves 6th-order local truncation error with a three-point stencil:

u[n+1] = [ 2u[n](1 − 5h²f[n]/12) − u[n−1](1 + h²f[n−1]/12) ] / (1 + h²f[n+1]/12) Global error O(h⁴). More efficient than RK4 for this equation class. Bisection root-finding: scan F(E)=u(r_max), bracket sign changes, refine to tol=10⁻⁸ MeV (≤80 iterations per root).

5 · Runge-Kutta 4 (RK4)

System: y₁=u, y₂=u' → dy₁/dr=y₂, dy₂/dr=f(r)·y₁ k₁=h·F(r,y), k₂=h·F(r+h/2, y+k₁/2) k₃=h·F(r+h/2, y+k₂/2), k₄=h·F(r+h, y+k₃) y(r+h) = y(r) + (k₁+2k₂+2k₃+k₄)/6 [O(h⁴) global]

6 · WKB Semiclassical Approximation

Classically allowed (E > V_eff): u(r) ≈ A/√k(r) · sin[∫k(r)dr + φ], k(r)=√(2μ(E−V_eff))/ħc Classically forbidden (E < V_eff): u(r) ≈ B/√κ(r) · exp[−∫κ(r)dr], κ(r)=√(2μ(V_eff−E))/ħc Bohr-Sommerfeld quantisation: ∫_{r₁}^{r₂} k(r)dr = (n + ½)π (n=0,1,2,...)

7 · Simpson Normalisation & Expectation Values

∫|u|² dr ≈ (h/3)[f₀ + 4f₁ + 2f₂ + 4f₃ + ··· + f_N] [O(h⁴)] Normalised: u_norm = u / √(∫|u|²dr) ⟨r⟩ = ∫ u*(r)·r·u(r) dr ⟨r²⟩ = ∫ u*(r)·r²·u(r) dr ⟨T⟩ = E − ⟨V⟩ (virial theorem: check numerical accuracy) ⟨p²⟩ = ∫ |u'(r)|² dr × (ħc)² (from integration by parts)

8 · Scattering Theory — Phase Shifts

Two-point Bessel matching at r₁, r₂ in asymptotic zone: tan(δ_ℓ) = [u(r₁)j_ℓ(kr₂)−u(r₂)j_ℓ(kr₁)] / [u(r₂)n_ℓ(kr₁)−u(r₁)n_ℓ(kr₂)] Scattering amplitude: f(θ) = (1/k) Σ_ℓ (2ℓ+1) e^{iδ_ℓ} sin(δ_ℓ) P_ℓ(cosθ) Differential cross section: dσ/dΩ = |f(θ)|² [fm²/sr] Total cross section (optical theorem): σ_total = (4π/k²) Σ_ℓ (2ℓ+1) sin²(δ_ℓ) [fm²] (1 fm² = 10 mb) S-matrix: S_ℓ = e^{2iδ_ℓ}, Re(S_ℓ)=cos(2δ_ℓ), Im(S_ℓ)=sin(2δ_ℓ)

9 · Resonance Theory — Breit-Wigner

Complex energy: E_res = E_R − iΓ/2 E_R = resonance energy, Γ = width (FWHM), τ = ħ/Γ = 6.582×10⁻²²/Γ[MeV] s Breit-Wigner phase shift near resonance: tan(δ_ℓ) = −(Γ/2)/(E − E_R) → δ_ℓ = π/2 at E = E_R Detection algorithm: 1. Scan δ_ℓ(E) at many energies 2. Find rapid π-jump → resonance bracket 3. Peak of |dδ/dE| at E_R 4. Γ = 2/(dδ/dE)|_{E_R} from FWHM of peak 5. τ = ħ/Γ, Q = E_R/Γ (quality factor) BW cross section: σ_ℓ(E) = (4π(2ℓ+1)/k²)·(Γ/2)²/[(E−E_R)²+(Γ/2)²]

11 · Usage Guide

  • m₁, m₂: MeV/c² (proton=938.272, neutron=939.565, α=3727.38, electron=0.511)
  • Z₁, Z₂: charge numbers — only affect Coulomb-type potentials
  • ℓ: orbital quantum number; adds ħ²ℓ(ℓ+1)/(2μr²) centrifugal barrier automatically
  • r_max: must extend well into classically forbidden region (typically 20–50 fm for nuclear)
  • E range: set below V_min for bound states; above 0 for resonances/scattering
  • Numerov is most accurate for this equation; RK4 for independent cross-check; WKB for semiclassical insight
  • Green values in tables = analytical solution available for comparison
  • For resonances: use Well+Barrier, or any potential with centrifugal barrier (ℓ>0)
