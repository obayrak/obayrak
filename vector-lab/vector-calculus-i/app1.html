\(\nabla f\) Gradient
The gradient of a scalar field \(f(x,y,z)\) is a vector field pointing in the direction of steepest ascent, with magnitude equal to the rate of change.
\[\nabla f=\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x},\frac{\partial f}{\partial y},\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\right)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\,\hat{\imath}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\,\hat{\jmath}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\,\hat{k}\]
PHYSICS In electrostatics, \(\mathbf E=-\nabla\varphi\) relates the electric field to the electric potential. In mechanics, \(\mathbf F=-\nabla U\) gives the conservative force from a potential energy surface.
MATH The gradient transforms as a covariant vector under coordinate changes. It is orthogonal to all level surfaces \(f=\text{const}\).
Electric field: \(\mathbf E=-\nabla\varphi,\ \ \varphi=\dfrac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r}\)
Gravitational: \(\mathbf g=-\nabla\Phi,\ \ \Phi=-\dfrac{GM}{r}\)
Pressure force: \(\mathbf F=-\nabla P\) (fluid statics)
THEOREM The directional derivative in direction \(\hat u\) is \(D_{\hat u}f=\nabla f\cdot\hat u\), maximized when \(\hat u\parallel\nabla f\).
\(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf F\) Divergence
The divergence of a vector field \(\mathbf F\) measures the net flux out of an infinitesimal volume element — the “outflowing-ness” at a point.
\[\nabla\cdot\mathbf F=\frac{\partial F_x}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial F_y}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial F_z}{\partial z}\]
PHYSICS Maxwell's first law: \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\rho/\varepsilon_0\). Continuity equation: \(\partial\rho/\partial t+\nabla\cdot\mathbf J=0\). In fluid dynamics: \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=0\) for incompressible flow.
GAUSS' THEOREM \(\displaystyle\oint_S \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf A=\iiint_V(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F)\,dV\) — the total flux through a closed surface equals the volume integral of divergence.
Source field: \(\mathbf F=\hat r/r^2\ \Rightarrow\ \nabla\cdot\mathbf F=4\pi\,\delta^3(\mathbf r)\)
Uniform: \(\mathbf F=(x,y,z)\ \Rightarrow\ \nabla\cdot\mathbf F=3\)
Incompressible: \(\mathbf F=(-y,x,0)\ \Rightarrow\ \nabla\cdot\mathbf F=0\)
IRROTATIONAL \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F>0\): source (field lines diverge). \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F<0\): sink (field lines converge). \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F=0\): solenoidal/incompressible.
\(\nabla\!\times\!\mathbf F\) Curl (Rotation)
The curl of a vector field measures the local rotation (vorticity) — how much the field “circulates” around a point, and about which axis.
\[\nabla\times\mathbf F=\begin{vmatrix}\hat{\imath}&\hat{\jmath}&\hat{k}\\ \partial_x&\partial_y&\partial_z\\ F_x&F_y&F_z\end{vmatrix}\] \[\begin{aligned}=&\left(\frac{\partial F_z}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial F_y}{\partial z}\right)\hat{\imath}\\ +&\left(\frac{\partial F_x}{\partial z}-\frac{\partial F_z}{\partial x}\right)\hat{\jmath}\\ +&\left(\frac{\partial F_y}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial F_x}{\partial y}\right)\hat{k}\end{aligned}\]
PHYSICS Faraday's law: \(\nabla\times\mathbf E=-\partial\mathbf B/\partial t\). Ampère–Maxwell: \(\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mu_0\mathbf J+\mu_0\varepsilon_0\,\partial\mathbf E/\partial t\). Fluid vorticity: \(\boldsymbol\omega=\nabla\times\mathbf v\).
STOKES' THEOREM \(\displaystyle\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf l=\iint_S(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot d\mathbf A\) — line integral around a closed curve equals surface integral of curl.
Rigid rotation: \(\mathbf F=(-y,x,0)\ \Rightarrow\ \nabla\times\mathbf F=(0,0,2)\)
Conservative: \(\mathbf F=\nabla\varphi\ \Rightarrow\ \nabla\times\mathbf F=0\)
Vortex tube: vorticity along the \(z\)-axis
IRROTATIONAL If \(\nabla\times\mathbf F=0\) and the domain is simply connected, then \(\mathbf F=\nabla\varphi\) for some potential \(\varphi\) (conservative field).
\(\nabla^2\) Laplacian & Identities
The Laplacian \(\nabla^2 f=\nabla\cdot(\nabla f)=\dfrac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}+\dfrac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2}+\dfrac{\partial^2 f}{\partial z^2}\) appears in the wave equation, Poisson equation, and heat equation.
Key identities: \[\begin{aligned}\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf F)&=0 &&\text{(curl is solenoidal)}\\ \nabla\times(\nabla f)&=0 &&\text{(gradient is irrotational)}\\ \nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf F)&=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F)-\nabla^2\mathbf F\\ \nabla\cdot(f\mathbf F)&=f(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F)+\mathbf F\cdot(\nabla f)\end{aligned}\]
PHYSICS Poisson equation: \(\nabla^2\varphi=-\rho/\varepsilon_0\). Laplace equation: \(\nabla^2\varphi=0\) (charge-free). Schrödinger: \(-\dfrac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\psi+V\psi=E\psi\).
Numerical Methods Used
Central differences (2nd order, \(h=10^{-4}\)):
\[\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\approx\frac{f(x+h)-f(x-h)}{2h},\qquad \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}\approx\frac{f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)}{h^2}\]
Laplacian (6-point stencil):
\[\nabla^2 f\approx\frac{f(x\pm h)+f(y\pm h)+f(z\pm h)-6f(x,y,z)}{h^2}\]
Truncation error: \(O(h^2)\). For functions with large second derivatives, reduce \(h\) or increase evaluation precision.

▸ ANALYSIS TAB
Set evaluation point \((x_0,y_0,z_0)\) with sliders. All operators are computed numerically at that point using central finite differences. The Jacobian matrix shows all 9 partial derivatives \(\partial F_i/\partial x_j\).
▸ TIPS
• Start with N=6–8 for fast rendering
• For 3D view, reduce N to ≤ 6 to avoid clutter
• Use Divergence mode + RdBu colormap to see sources/sinks clearly
• Conservative fields (\(\nabla\times\mathbf F=0\)) look “orderly”; rotational fields show circular arrows
• The Coulomb preset + Gradient mode shows the Coulomb electric field
