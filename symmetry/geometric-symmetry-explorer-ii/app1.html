DEFINITION OF GEOMETRIC SYMMETRY

A geometric figure possesses symmetry under a transformation \(\hat{T}\) if and only if the transformation maps the figure onto itself — leaving it indistinguishable from its original state. The complete set of all symmetry operations forms a symmetry group \(G\) (closed under composition, satisfying the group axioms).

In crystallography, point groups describe molecular symmetry, and the 230 space groups classify all distinct periodic crystal structures in 3D. The Neumann Principle states that every physical property of a crystal must exhibit at least the symmetry of the crystal's point group.

Formal symmetry condition:  \(\hat{T}(S)=S\) where \(S\) is the shape/pattern and \(\hat{T}\) is the symmetry operator.
Group axioms:  Closure · Associativity · Identity \(\hat{I}\) · Inverse \(\hat{T}^{-1}\)
Order of \(\hat{T}\):  the smallest integer \(n\) such that \(\hat{T}^n = \hat{I}\)

REFLECTION SYMMETRY

Mirror / Line Symmetry · Groups: Cₙᵥ, Dₙ

A shape has reflection symmetry if folding it along a mirror line makes both halves coincide exactly. The line can be in any direction; not all shapes have one — a scalene triangle has none, an isosceles has 1, and an equilateral has 3. The operation is an isometry (distance-preserving) with \(\det=-1\), making it an improper transformation.

Reflection matrices across principal axes: \[ M_x=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad M_y=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix} \]
General reflection across line at angle \(\theta\) through origin: \[ M_\theta=\begin{pmatrix}\cos 2\theta & \sin 2\theta\\ \sin 2\theta & -\cos 2\theta\end{pmatrix},\quad \det M_\theta=-1 \]

ROTATIONAL SYMMETRY

Order-n Symmetry · Groups: Cₙ, Dₙ

A shape has n-fold rotational symmetry if rotating it by \(360°/n\) about a fixed center maps it onto itself. The integer \(n\) is the order. A circle has C∞ (infinite-fold) symmetry. The Crystallographic Restriction Theorem forbids 5-, 7- and higher-fold axes in periodic lattices (only orders 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 are compatible).

Rotation matrix by angle \(\theta\) (proper, \(\det = +1\)): \[ R(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta & -\sin\theta\\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta\end{pmatrix} \]
n-fold symmetry condition and orbit: \[ C_n:\;\theta=\frac{2\pi}{n},\quad R_n^{\,n}=I,\quad \{R_n^k\,S\}_{k=0}^{n-1}=S \]

TRANSLATIONAL SYMMETRY

Bravais Lattices · 14 types · 7 crystal systems

A pattern has translational symmetry if displacing it by a lattice vector \(\mathbf{T}\) leaves it invariant. A Bravais lattice is an infinite, periodic array generated by integer multiples of primitive vectors. There are exactly 14 distinct 3D Bravais lattice types in 7 crystal systems. Bloch's theorem uses this symmetry to simplify quantum-mechanical solutions in periodic potentials.

Lattice translation vector (2D): \[ \mathbf{T}=n_1\mathbf{a}_1+n_2\mathbf{a}_2,\quad n_1,n_2\in\mathbb{Z} \]
Bloch condition (quantum solid-state physics): \[ \psi_{n\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{T})=e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{T}}\,\psi_{n\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{r}) \]

SCALING (DILATION) SYMMETRY

Self-Similarity · Fractal Geometry · Scale Invariance

A shape has scaling symmetry (self-similarity) if it looks identical after magnification or contraction by factor \(s\). Fractals exhibit self-similarity at every scale. Their Hausdorff dimension \(d_H\) often exceeds the topological dimension, quantifying how detail proliferates with magnification. Power-law behavior and scale invariance underpin both critical phenomena in physics and natural pattern formation.

Scaling (dilation) transformation: \[ S_s:(x,y)\to(sx,\,sy),\quad s\in\mathbb{R}^+\setminus\{0\} \]
Hausdorff (fractal) dimension: \[ d_H=\frac{\log N}{\log(1/r)}\quad\text{(N self-similar copies at scale }r\text{)} \]
