⚙ The Mirror Equation & Lens Equation

Both mirrors and thin lenses obey the same fundamental equation relating object distance, image distance, and focal length:

1/f = 1/do + 1/di

Solving for image distance: di = f·do / (do − f)

The lateral magnification (ratio of image height to object height) is:

m = −di / do = hi / ho

A negative m means the image is inverted; positive m means upright. |m| > 1 = enlarged; |m| < 1 = diminished.

🔴 Concave Mirror (Converging)

A concave (converging) mirror has its reflecting surface on the inside of the curve. The focal length f > 0 (real focal point in front of mirror).

Sign Convention (New Cartesian)

Distances measured from the pole (vertex) of the mirror. Distances in the direction of incident light (toward mirror) are negative; reflected light direction is positive. In this simulator we use the "real-is-positive" convention for simplicity: all real objects have do > 0, real images di > 0.

Three Principal Rays

■ Parallel Ray: A ray parallel to the principal axis reflects through the focal point F.

■ Vertex Ray: A ray hitting the pole (vertex) of the mirror reflects at an equal angle on the other side of the axis.

■ Focal Ray: A ray passing through F (when object is beyond F) reflects parallel to the axis.

🔵 Convex Mirror (Diverging)

A convex (diverging) mirror has its reflecting surface on the outside of the curve. The focal length f < 0 (virtual focal point behind the mirror). The image is always virtual, upright, and diminished — regardless of object position.

di = f·do / (do − f)   (always negative for real do)

Common uses: vehicle rearview mirrors, shop security mirrors — provide wide field of view with reduced image size.

🟠 Concave Lens (Diverging)

A concave (diverging) lens is thinner at the center than at the edges. Focal length f < 0. Parallel rays diverge after passing through, appearing to come from the virtual focal point F₂ on the same side as the incoming light.

Three Principal Rays (Diverging Lens)

■ Parallel Ray: Parallel incident ray diverges after the lens, appearing to come from F₂ (on the object side).

■ Center Ray: A ray through the optical center passes straight through unchanged.

■ F₁ Ray: A ray aimed toward F₁ (on the image side) exits parallel to the axis.

🟢 Convex Lens (Converging)

A convex (converging) lens is thicker at the center. Focal length f > 0. Parallel rays converge at the real focal point F₂ on the far side.

🔬 Compound Microscope

A compound microscope uses two converging lenses to achieve high magnification of nearby objects.

Objective Lens

Short focal length. The object is placed just beyond Fobj, producing a real, enlarged, inverted intermediate image.

Eyepiece

Acts as a simple magnifier. The intermediate image is placed near Feye, producing a virtual, enlarged final image.

Mobj = −di,obj / do,obj
Meye = −di,eye / do,eye
Mtotal = Mobj × Meye

Standard approximation (tube length L, near point D = 25 cm):

Mtotal ≈ −(L / fobj) × (D / feye)

🔭 Astronomical Telescope (Keplerian)

Used to observe distant objects. Two converging lenses with the objective having a much longer focal length than the eyepiece.

Principle

The objective forms a real, inverted, diminished intermediate image of the distant object at its focal plane (Fobj). The eyepiece is positioned so that this intermediate image lies at its front focal point (Feye), producing a final image at infinity (for relaxed eye viewing).

Tube Length L = fobj + feye
Angular Magnification M = −fobj / feye

The negative sign indicates the image is inverted. Larger fobj/feye ratio → higher magnification.

Galilean Telescope

Uses a diverging eyepiece, producing an upright image but limited magnification. Tube length = fobj − |feye|. This simulator shows the Keplerian type.

🚀 Getting Started

Welcome to the Optics Lab Simulator! This tool lets you interactively explore how concave and convex mirrors, concave and convex lenses, microscopes, and telescopes form images. Use the tabs at the top to navigate between different optical elements.

💡 Tip: Open the Physics tab alongside your simulation to see the relevant formulas for each optical element.

🎮 Controls

For Mirrors & Lenses

  • Object Distance (do): Distance from the object to the mirror or lens. Drag the slider to move the object closer or farther. Minimum: 0.5 units.
  • Focal Length (|f|): The magnitude of the focal length. The sign is automatically applied based on the optic type (positive for concave mirrors/convex lenses, negative for convex mirrors/concave lenses).
  • Object Height (ho): Height of the object arrow. Affects magnification visualization.

For Microscope

  • fobj: Objective lens focal length (short = higher magnification).
  • feye: Eyepiece focal length.
  • δ (delta): How far beyond the objective focal point the object is placed. Small δ → object near Fobj → large intermediate image.

For Telescope

  • fobj: Objective focal length (long = high magnification).
  • feye: Eyepiece focal length (short = high magnification).
  • Off-axis angle α: The angle at which the distant object is off-axis. This shows how the telescope handles objects not directly on the optical axis.

🎨 Color Coding

  • Amber arrow: The object (what you're looking at).
  • Cyan arrow (solid): Real image — formed by actual convergence of rays.
  • Purple arrow (dashed): Virtual image — formed by backward extension of rays; cannot be projected on a screen.
  • Red ray: Parallel incident ray (Ray 1).
  • Green ray: Vertex/center ray (Ray 2).
  • Yellow ray: Focal ray (Ray 3 through or from F).
  • Dashed lines: Virtual extensions — the rays appear to come from these directions. Used to locate virtual images.
  • F marker: Focal point (orange cross). C marker: Center of curvature (red cross, 2× focal distance for mirrors).

📖 Reading the Results Panel

  • Image Distance di: Positive = real image (in front of mirror, other side of lens). Negative = virtual image (behind mirror, same side as object for lens). "∞" means the image is at infinity (object is at focal point).
  • Magnification m: m = −di/do. Negative = inverted. |m| > 1 = enlarged. |m| = 1 = same size. |m| < 1 = diminished.
  • Nature: Real (rays actually converge there) or Virtual (rays only appear to come from there).
  • Orientation: Upright (same orientation as object) or Inverted (flipped relative to object).
  • Size: Enlarged, Same Size, or Diminished.

🧪 Experiments to Try

  1. Concave Mirror — focal point: Slowly move do toward the focal length f. Watch the image fly to infinity, then reappear on the other side as virtual.
  2. Convex Lens — magnifier: Set do less than f. The lens acts as a magnifying glass — virtual, upright, enlarged image.
  3. Concave vs. Convex Lens: Set both to the same f and do. Notice how the concave lens always produces a virtual, upright, diminished image regardless.
  4. Microscope — magnification: Decrease δ (object closer to Fobj). The intermediate image grows, increasing total magnification.
  5. Telescope — M ratio: Increase fobj or decrease feye. Notice the angular magnification (M = fobj/feye) increases.

⚠ Notes & Limitations

  • All simulations use the paraxial (small angle) approximation — rays close to and nearly parallel to the optical axis. Real-world optics may show aberrations not captured here.
  • When the object is very close to the focal point, the image distance becomes very large. The simulation shows an "∞" indicator in this case.
  • The "unit" is an arbitrary length. You can think of 1 unit as any real distance (e.g., 10 cm), as long as it's consistent.
  • For the microscope simulation, the tube length is fixed at 12 units. You can vary fobj, feye, and δ to explore different configurations.
