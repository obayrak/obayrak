Optic Lab

Ten interactive optics labs, from first principles to quantum optics and relativity: start with Ray Optics Basics, continue with Optical Instruments, then Advanced Optics, use the Optics Workbench to build, measure and see, then Wave Optics and Light, Colour and Photonics, then Interferometry, Polarisation and Photonic Structures and Nonlinear, Quantum and Imaging Optics, then Vision and Spectroscopy, and finish with Lasers, Light Forces and Relativity. Each lab has physics presets for the classic textbook cases.

Laboratory Modules

01

Ray Optics Basics — Mirrors and Lenses

Start here. Concave and convex mirrors, converging and diverging lenses: the mirror and thin-lens equations, three principal rays, real and virtual images, and the classic cases (object beyond 2F, at 2F, at F, inside F) as presets.

Open Module →
02

Optical Instruments — Microscope and Telescope

The human eye (myopia, hyperopia, presbyopia and the glasses that correct them), the magnifier, the compound microscope (NA, resolution), Keplerian, Galilean and Newtonian telescopes, the camera (field of view, exposure, depth of field) and the projector.

Open Module →
03

Advanced Optics — Thick Lenses, Ray Tracing and Aberration

Thick lenses from the lensmaker's equation and exact Snell ray tracing, spherical and chromatic aberration (prism spectrum, colour focus, achromatic doublet), lens systems with ray-transfer (ABCD) matrices and principal planes, optical fibres (NA, single-mode V-number), the rainbow, mirages (hot roads, looming, Fata Morgana), aberration diagnostics with spot diagrams and ray fans, Gaussian laser beams and resonator stability, and gradient-index (GRIN) lenses.

Open Module →
04

Optics Workbench — Bench, Experiments and What You See

A free optical bench: combine lenses, mirrors, glass slabs, prisms, stops and screens and trace the rays. Virtual lab experiments with unknown samples (focal length by the u–v method, refractive index by Snell's law), least-squares fits, CSV export and printable worksheets. And image simulation: what a short-sighted eye or a camera actually sees.

Open Module →
05

Wave Optics — Interference, Diffraction and Polarisation

Where rays are not enough: Young's double slit (also one photon at a time), diffraction gratings and spectra, the single slit and the Airy disc that sets the resolution limit of the eye, telescope and microscope, thin-film colours of soap bubbles and coated lenses, and polarisation with Malus's law, wave plates and Brewster's angle.

Open Module →
06

Light, Colour and Photonics — Optics at Work

Fourier optics and spatial filtering (Abbe, Zernike phase contrast), recording and replaying holograms, colour vision and the CIE diagram, light sources and their efficiency, why the sky is blue and sunsets red, halos and sun dogs, how a laser works (threshold, Q-switching, mode locking) and fibre-optic communication links.

Open Module →
07

Interferometry, Polarisation and Photonic Structures

Michelson, Mach–Zehnder and Fabry–Pérot interferometers, coherence and Fourier-transform spectroscopy, polarisation calculus on the Poincaré sphere, calcite, photoelastic stress, sugar and LCD pixels, thin-film coatings and photonic crystals (butterfly colours), Fresnel's equations, evanescent waves and tunnelling, surface plasmon sensors and negative-index metamaterials.

Open Module →
08

Nonlinear, Quantum and Imaging Optics

Frequency doubling and phase matching, the Kerr effect and optical solitons, photon statistics and Hong–Ou–Mandel interference, Bell tests and BB84 quantum cryptography, MTF and pixel aliasing, noise and deconvolution, confocal and STED super-resolution microscopy, and atmospheric seeing with adaptive optics.

Open Module →
09

Vision and Spectroscopy

The eye as an optical system: refractive errors, aberrations and visual acuity with simulated eye charts, colour vision and colour-blindness simulation, contrast sensitivity, atomic spectral lines, Doppler, pressure and Voigt line broadening, the Zeeman effect, Fraunhofer lines and a Czerny–Turner grating spectrometer.

Open Module →
10

Lasers, Light Forces and Relativity

Laser resonator stability and Hermite–Gauss and Laguerre–Gauss modes, longitudinal modes, mode locking and optical frequency combs, optical tweezers in the ray-optics and Rayleigh regimes, the relativistic Doppler effect, aberration and beaming, Fizeau's moving-water experiment, and gravitational lensing with Einstein rings and microlensing.

Open Module →
