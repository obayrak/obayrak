    Examples
    What is happening
    01

    Optical bench

    Choose a light source, then add lenses, mirrors, glass slabs, thin prisms, aperture stops and screens. Every ray is traced through every element in order: lenses and mirrors with the thin-element rule, slabs with exact Snell's law. Drag any element (or the object) along the bench. The results give the final image position, magnification and whether it is real. A screen shows the picture it catches, sharp or blurred.

    02

    Experiments

    A real-lab simulation with an unknown sample. Set the object, move the screen until the image looks sharpest, and record. Readings include realistic errors. The least-squares fit of 1/v against 1/u (or sin i against sin r) gives the focal length (or refractive index) with its uncertainty. Download the data as CSV for a spreadsheet, or print a worksheet with the table, graph and report questions. Reveal the true value at the end.

    03

    What you see

    The left picture is the scene as it really is; the right one is what the eye or camera delivers. Try short sight and its correction, shallow depth of field in a portrait, the colour fringes of a simple lens and how an achromat removes them, and barrel or pincushion distortion.

    04

    Where to learn the theory

    Ray Optics Basics (module 1) covers mirrors and thin lenses, Optical Instruments (module 2) the eye, camera and telescopes, and Advanced Optics (module 3) thick lenses, aberrations, dispersion and ray-transfer matrices.
