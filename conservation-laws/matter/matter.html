Closed System — Particle Gas
A sealed container holds particles of varying masses. Particles collide elastically. No matter how they move, split, or combine — the total mass inside remains constant. Add energy (temperature) to observe behavior.

// CLOSED SYSTEM PRINCIPLE

In a closed system, no mass enters or leaves. The total mass M = Σmᵢ is an invariant of the system regardless of particle velocities, positions, or internal configurations. This holds true whether particles bounce off walls, collide with each other, or cluster together.

Mtotal = Σ mᵢ = const

Chemical Reaction — 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
Watch hydrogen and oxygen molecules combine to form water. Even as atoms rearrange and bonds form, the total mass is perfectly preserved. Lavoisier's law at the molecular level.

// CHEMICAL REACTIONS — MASS BALANCE

Chemical reactions rearrange atoms into new molecular configurations, but atoms themselves are neither created nor destroyed. The periodic table mass numbers directly reflect this conservation.

2 H₂ + O₂ → 2 H₂O

Phase Changes — Ice → Water → Steam
Heat a sample of H₂O through all three phases. Mass is conserved through every transition — ice melting, water evaporating. What changes is only the arrangement and kinetic energy of molecules.

// PHASE TRANSITIONS & MASS CONSERVATION

Phase changes are purely physical transformations. H₂O molecules are identical in all three states; only their intermolecular distances and kinetic energies change. The molecular formula — and therefore the mass — remains constant throughout.

H₂O(s) → H₂O(l) → H₂O(g)
m₁ = m₂ = m₃ = 18.015 g/mol × n

Fluid Transfer — Closed System
Two vessels connected by a valve. Transfer fluid between them in any amount, at any rate. The total mass of fluid in the system never changes — demonstrating conservation in macroscopic flow.

// MACROSCOPIC MASS CONSERVATION

The continuity equation in fluid mechanics is a direct expression of conservation of mass. For an incompressible fluid flowing between vessels, what flows out of one must flow into another: the total is invariant.

∂ρ/∂t + ∇·(ρv) = 0

Nuclear Decay — Alpha & Beta Emission
Radioactive decay transforms one nuclide into another via particle emission. Nucleon number (A) and charge (Z) are conserved exactly. A tiny mass deficit converts to energy via E=mc², the only exception to strict mass conservation.

// NUCLEAR CONSERVATION LAWS

In nuclear reactions, baryon number and lepton number are conserved exactly. Mass number A = Z + N is conserved. However, a small binding energy mass deficit (Q-value) means rest mass is not perfectly conserved — instead, the Einstein relation E=mc² converts this deficit into kinetic energy of products.

A: conserved  |  Z: conserved
Q = [Mparent − Σ Mdaughters] c²
Mass ≈ conserved (Δm/m ~ 10⁻³)
⚠ Note: Nuclear Q-values introduce minor mass-energy conversion — the only real-world deviation from strict mass conservation, governed by Special Relativity.
