Discrete Symmetries in Physics

Conservation of
Parity & its Violation

The parity transformation mirrors the universe through the origin. For decades physicists assumed nature was left-right symmetric — until Chien-Shiung Wu shattered that assumption in 1956.

P-Symmetry EM conserves P Weak violates P Wu Experiment CPT Theorem

Parity is one of the three fundamental discrete symmetries of nature. It asks a deceptively simple question: does the mirror image of an experiment obey the same laws? For two of the four fundamental forces the answer is yes — for the third, resoundingly no.

◈ What is the Parity Transformation?

The parity operator $\hat{P}$ reflects all spatial coordinates through the origin — equivalent to a point inversion in 3D space:

$$\hat{P}:\;(x,y,z)\;\longrightarrow\;(-x,-y,-z)$$Definition

This is not the same as a simple mirror reflection (which reverses only one axis). A mirror reflection combined with a 180° rotation gives the parity transformation.

The key question: is the physical world invariant under $\hat{P}$?

◈ Eigenvalues — Intrinsic Parity

Since applying parity twice returns to the original state, $\hat{P}^2 = \mathbf{1}$. Therefore the eigenvalues of $\hat{P}$ are:

$$\hat{P}\,|\psi\rangle = \eta_P\,|\psi\rangle,\quad \eta_P = \pm 1$$Parity eigenvalue

$\eta_P = +1$ is called even parity (or scalar). $\eta_P = -1$ is odd parity (or pseudoscalar). This intrinsic parity is a quantum number of particles — like charge or spin.

◈ How Vectors Transform Under Parity

Not all vectors behave the same way. This distinction is crucial for understanding parity violation:

$$\text{Polar vector:}\quad \hat{P}\,\vec{v} = -\vec{v}$$e.g. momentum, position, electric field

Position $\vec{r}$, momentum $\vec{p}$, electric field $\vec{E}$, force $\vec{F}$ — these flip sign under parity. They are true vectors.

$$\text{Axial vector:}\quad \hat{P}\,\vec{A} = +\vec{A}$$e.g. angular momentum, magnetic field

Angular momentum $\vec{L} = \vec{r}\times\vec{p}$, spin $\vec{S}$, magnetic field $\vec{B}$ — these do not flip. They are pseudovectors (axial vectors).

$$\hat{P}\,(\vec{r}\times\vec{p}) = (-\vec{r})\times(-\vec{p}) = +(\vec{r}\times\vec{p})\quad\Rightarrow\quad\vec{L}\text{ is an axial vector}$$Why L doesn't flip
Scalars & Pseudoscalars
A scalar is unchanged by $\hat P$. A pseudoscalar changes sign — like the dot product of a polar vector and an axial vector: $\vec{p}\cdot\vec{S}$, the helicity operator.
Helicity = Chirality?
Helicity is the projection of spin onto momentum. Under parity, $\vec{p}\to-\vec{p}$ but $\vec{S}\to+\vec{S}$, so helicity flips sign. Left-handed becomes right-handed.
Why Does it Matter?
If the laws of physics conserve parity, no experiment can distinguish its mirror image. The Wu experiment showed this is false for the weak force — an astonishing discovery.
02Mathematical Framework

The parity operator is Hermitian and unitary, making it an observable with real eigenvalues. Its action on quantum fields determines the parity quantum numbers of all particles.

◈ The Parity Operator — Formal Properties
$$\hat{P}^\dagger = \hat{P},\quad \hat{P}^2 = \mathbf{1} \;\Rightarrow\; \hat{P} = \hat{P}^{-1}$$Hermitian + unitary

Because $\hat{P}$ is both Hermitian and unitary, it is also its own inverse. Its spectrum is $\{+1, -1\}$ only.

$$[\hat{H}, \hat{P}] = 0 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \text{Parity conserved}$$Conservation criterion

Parity is conserved in a system if and only if the Hamiltonian commutes with $\hat{P}$. This means eigenstates of $\hat{H}$ can also be eigenstates of $\hat{P}$.

◈ Parity in Quantum Field Theory

In QFT, parity transforms quantum fields. For a Dirac spinor field $\psi(t,\vec{x})$:

$$\hat{P}\,\psi(t,\vec{x})\,\hat{P}^{-1} = \gamma^0\,\psi(t,-\vec{x})$$Spinor under parity

The $\gamma^0$ matrix mixes the upper and lower components of the Dirac spinor, turning a left-handed Weyl spinor into a right-handed one — and vice versa. This is why parity violation in weak interactions is so deep: the weak force only couples to left-handed fermions.

$$\hat{P}\,\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi\,\hat{P}^{-1} = \bar{\psi}\gamma_\mu\psi \;\;(\text{vector current — even})$$Vector current
$$\hat{P}\,\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\psi\,\hat{P}^{-1} = -\bar{\psi}\gamma_\mu\gamma^5\psi \;\;(\text{axial current — odd})$$Axial-vector current
◈ Parity of Composite Systems

For a system of particles, the total parity is the product of intrinsic parities times the orbital angular momentum factor:

$$\eta_P^{\rm total} = \eta_P^{(1)}\cdot\eta_P^{(2)}\cdot(-1)^{\ell}$$Combined parity

The $(-1)^\ell$ factor arises because the spatial wave function $Y_\ell^m(\theta,\phi)$ of orbital angular momentum $\ell$ transforms as $(-1)^\ell$ under parity:

$$\hat{P}\,Y_\ell^m(\hat{n}) = (-1)^\ell\,Y_\ell^m(\hat{n})$$

◈ The Theta-Tau Puzzle — First Sign of Trouble

In the early 1950s, two particles were observed: the $\theta^+$ decaying to $\pi^+\pi^0$ (parity +1) and the $\tau^+$ decaying to $\pi^+\pi^+\pi^-$ (parity −1). They had identical masses, charges, and lifetimes — yet apparently different parities. Lee and Yang posed the radical question:

$$\theta^+\text{ and }\tau^+\text{ are the SAME particle: }K^+$$Resolution: parity is not conserved!

Lee and Yang (1956) reviewed the experimental evidence and found: parity conservation in weak interactions had never actually been tested. They proposed specific experiments to test it. Wu's experiment provided the definitive answer within months.

03Parity in the Four Forces

Each of the four fundamental forces has a different relationship with parity symmetry. The pattern is striking — and still not fully understood at a deep theoretical level.

Gravity
Conserved

General relativity is parity-symmetric. The Einstein field equations are invariant under spatial inversion. No gravitational parity violation has ever been observed.

Electromagnetism
Conserved

Maxwell's equations are parity-invariant. $\vec{E}$ is a polar vector (flips), $\vec{B}$ is an axial vector (doesn't flip). The combination $\vec{F} = q(\vec{E}+\vec{v}\times\vec{B})$ is consistently parity-even.

Strong Force
Conserved

QCD respects parity symmetry. The strong interaction Lagrangian is parity-invariant (ignoring the strong CP problem — a small potential violation bounded to $\theta < 10^{-10}$).

Weak Force
Maximally Violated

The weak force maximally violates parity. It couples only to left-handed fermions and right-handed antifermions. The V−A structure of the weak current is the source.

◈ The V−A Theory of Weak Interactions

Fermi originally wrote the weak interaction as a pure Vector (V) coupling, analogous to electromagnetism. The discovery of parity violation required the V−A (Vector minus Axial-vector) structure, proposed by Feynman & Gell-Mann and Sudarshan & Marshak in 1957:

$$\mathcal{J}^\mu_{\rm weak} = \bar{\psi}\,\gamma^\mu\underbrace{(1-\gamma^5)}_{\text{projects left-handed}}\psi = \bar{\psi}_L\,\gamma^\mu\,\psi_L$$V−A weak current

The projection operator $\frac{1}{2}(1-\gamma^5)$ selects left-handed (negative helicity) fermions only. Under parity, left-handed ↔ right-handed, but there are no right-handed weak couplings — hence maximal parity violation.

$$\hat{P}\,\mathcal{J}^\mu_{\rm weak}\,\hat{P}^{-1} \neq \mathcal{J}^\mu_{\rm weak}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad [\hat{H}_{\rm weak},\hat{P}]\neq 0$$Parity is violated
◈ Why EM Conserves Parity

The electromagnetic interaction is mediated by photons and couples through the vector current $J^\mu_{\rm EM} = e\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$. This current contains equal V and A parts, but the photon coupling only picks up the vector part, which is parity-even:

$$\hat{P}\,(e\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi)\,\hat{P}^{-1} = e\bar\psi\gamma_\mu\psi \;\;\checkmark$$

Electromagnetic processes are entirely left-right symmetric. A mirror-image chemistry experiment gives identical results.

◈ Handedness of Nature

Left-Handed

Spin antiparallel to momentum. Couples to weak $W^\pm$ and $Z^0$.

Weakly Active ✓

Right-Handed

Spin parallel to momentum. Does NOT couple to charged weak currents.

Weakly Inactive ✗

The neutrino is the most dramatic example: only left-handed neutrinos have ever been observed. Right-handed neutrinos, if they exist at all, are sterile (undetectable by any known interaction).

04The Wu Experiment (1956)

Chien-Shiung Wu's experiment is one of the most consequential in the history of physics. In a single elegant measurement, she demonstrated that nature has a preferred handedness — a result so shocking that Lee and Yang won the Nobel Prize within a year.

1927
Dirac Equation & Spinors
Dirac unifies quantum mechanics and special relativity. His equation naturally produces left- and right-handed spinors, but at the time both were assumed equally physical.
1954–1956
The θ–τ Puzzle
The discovery that $K^+$ decays into both $2\pi$ (even parity) and $3\pi$ (odd parity) final states at the same rate suggests parity might not be conserved in weak decays.
June 1956
Lee & Yang Paper
T.D. Lee and C.N. Yang publish their landmark paper "Question of Parity Conservation in Weak Interactions," showing no existing experiment had tested parity in weak decays. They propose specific tests including the $\beta$-decay experiment.
Dec 1956 – Jan 1957
Wu's Experiment at NIST
Chien-Shiung Wu, working at the National Bureau of Standards (now NIST), uses polarized $^{60}$Co cooled to 0.01 K in a magnetic field. She observes that electrons from $\beta$-decay are emitted preferentially opposite to the nuclear spin — impossible if parity were conserved.
Oct 1957
Nobel Prize to Lee & Yang
Lee and Yang receive the Nobel Prize in Physics "for their penetrating investigation of the so-called parity laws which has led to important discoveries regarding elementary particles." Wu, who did the experimental work, was controversially not included.
1957
V−A Theory
Feynman, Gell-Mann, Sudarshan, and Marshak independently propose the V−A theory, explaining parity violation through the chiral structure of the weak interaction. Left-handed fermions only.
1964
CP Violation (Cronin & Fitch)
Even the combined CP symmetry (Charge conjugation + Parity) is violated in neutral kaon decays — an even deeper asymmetry related to the matter-antimatter imbalance of the universe.
◈ The Experiment in Detail
1
Cool $^{60}$Co to 0.01 K — thermal randomization would wash out the effect. The nuclei must be polarized along a definite axis.
2
Apply magnetic field to align the nuclear spins $\vec{J}$ along a preferred direction. The spin axis defines "up" and "down" in the experiment.
3
The $^{60}$Co nucleus undergoes $\beta^-$ decay: $n\to p + e^- + \bar\nu_e$. The electron carries away the parity-violating information.
4
Measure electron emission direction relative to the spin axis with scintillator detectors above and below the sample.
5
Result: significantly more electrons are emitted opposite to the spin direction ($\vec{J}$). If parity were conserved, emission would be symmetric. It is not.
$$^{60}{\rm Co}\;\xrightarrow{\beta^-}\;^{60}{\rm Ni}^* + e^- + \bar\nu_e$$Wu decay process
$$\langle\vec{J}\rangle\cdot\vec{p}_e < 0\quad\text{(preferred direction!)}$$Observed asymmetry

If parity were conserved, the quantity $\vec{J}\cdot\vec{p}_e$ (a pseudoscalar — polar × axial) would average to zero. The observation that it doesn't is direct proof of parity violation.

$$A = \frac{N(\uparrow\uparrow) - N(\uparrow\downarrow)}{N(\uparrow\uparrow) + N(\uparrow\downarrow)} \approx -0.7$$Asymmetry parameter

An asymmetry of −0.7 means 70% more electrons are emitted opposite to the spin direction vs parallel — a dramatically non-zero result.

The Mirror Argument: In the parity-reflected experiment, the spin direction is unchanged (axial vector) but electron momentum reverses (polar vector). The mirror experiment predicts more electrons parallel to spin — a different measurement, proving parity is violated.

06CPT Theorem

Although parity (P) is violated, nature preserves a more subtle symmetry: the combined transformation of Charge conjugation (C), Parity (P), and Time reversal (T). The CPT theorem is one of the deepest results in quantum field theory.

◈ C — Charge Conjugation
$$\hat C:\;\text{particle}\leftrightarrow\text{antiparticle}$$

Replaces each particle with its antiparticle (flipping charge, baryon number, lepton number). Weak interactions also violate C maximally — there are no left-handed antiparticles in the weak coupling.

◈ P — Parity
$$\hat P:\;\vec{r}\to -\vec{r},\;\vec{p}\to-\vec{p}$$

Spatial inversion. Left-handed ↔ right-handed. Violated maximally by the weak force, as Wu demonstrated. Electric fields flip; magnetic fields don't.

◈ T — Time Reversal
$$\hat T:\;t\to -t,\;\vec{p}\to-\vec{p},\;\vec{J}\to-\vec{J}$$

Reverses the arrow of time. The weak force also violates T individually (by CPT theorem, since CP is violated). T violation was confirmed in neutral kaon decays.

◈ The CPT Theorem
$$[\hat H,\;\hat C\hat P\hat T] = 0\quad\text{for any local, Lorentz-invariant QFT}$$Lüders–Pauli theorem

The CPT theorem states that the combined application of charge conjugation, parity, and time reversal is always a symmetry of any theory consistent with:

  • Lorentz invariance — the laws of physics are the same in all inertial frames
  • Locality — interactions occur at points in spacetime
  • Hermiticity of the Hamiltonian — probability is conserved (unitarity)

CPT is not an additional assumption — it is a theorem that follows inevitably from these foundational principles of relativistic quantum field theory.

Consequence: Any particle and its CPT-conjugate antiparticle must have identical mass and lifetime, and exactly opposite charges and magnetic moments. This has been verified to extraordinary precision (parts per billion) for electrons, protons, and antiprotons.
◈ CP Violation — Matter/Antimatter

Even the combined CP symmetry is broken — discovered in 1964 by Cronin and Fitch in neutral kaon decays. This is deeply connected to the matter-antimatter asymmetry of the universe.

$$K_L^0 \to \pi^+\pi^-\quad(\text{CP-violating,}\;\approx 0.2\%)$$Cronin-Fitch (1964)

If CP were exact, there would be equal matter and antimatter, and the universe would have annihilated itself at the Big Bang. CP violation allows a tiny excess of matter — roughly $1$ in $10^9$ — which is all we see today.

$$\frac{n_B - n_{\bar B}}{n_\gamma}\approx 6\times10^{-10}\quad\text{(from CP violation)}$$Baryon asymmetry
◈ Sakharov's Three Conditions

In 1967, Andrei Sakharov identified the three necessary conditions for the universe to develop a matter-antimatter imbalance:

①
Baryon number violation — processes that create baryons must exist
②
C and CP violation — matter and antimatter must behave differently
③
Departure from thermal equilibrium — the universe must not be in perfect thermodynamic equilibrium
Open Question: The CP violation observed in kaon and B-meson systems is too small to account for the observed baryon asymmetry. New sources of CP violation — perhaps in neutrino oscillations — are being actively searched for.
