Fundamental Conservation Laws

Conservation of
Electric Charge

In every physical process ever observed — from nuclear decays to thunderstorms — the total electric charge of an isolated system is absolutely constant. No known exception exists in the entire history of experimental physics.

Q = const Noether's Theorem U(1) Gauge Symmetry Quantized ±ne

Electric charge is one of the most fundamental properties of matter. Its conservation is not merely a rule of thumb — it is an exact law with no known exceptions, tested to extraordinary precision across every scale of nature.

◈ The Law of Charge Conservation

For any isolated system, the total electric charge $Q$ is constant in time. No process can create or destroy net charge — it can only be transferred between objects or transformed in form.

$$Q_{\rm total} = \sum_i q_i = \text{constant}$$Conservation law

Equivalently: the algebraic sum of all charges is preserved. A reaction producing a positive charge must simultaneously produce an equal negative charge.

Experimental bound: If protons were unstable and charge-violating, we would observe atmospheric ionization anomalies. The charge non-conservation rate is bounded to $\tau > 10^{21}$ years — essentially infinite.
◈ Charge Quantization

Electric charge is not continuous — it comes in discrete units. Every observed particle carries a charge that is an integer multiple of the elementary charge $e$:

$$q = ne,\quad n \in \mathbb{Z},\quad e = 1.602\times10^{-19}\;\text{C}$$Quantization

Quarks carry fractional charges ($\pm\tfrac{1}{3}e$, $\pm\tfrac{2}{3}e$), but these are always confined inside hadrons — free particles are always integer-charged. Millikan's 1909 oil-drop experiment first measured $e$ directly.

Why quantized? Dirac showed in 1931 that the mere existence of a magnetic monopole would force charge quantization. Whether or not monopoles exist, quantization is deeply embedded in quantum field theory.

◈ Charge in the Standard Model

In the Standard Model, electric charge conservation follows from the $U(1)_Y$ hypercharge gauge symmetry, combined with electroweak symmetry breaking. The conserved charge is a linear combination of weak isospin $T_3$ and hypercharge $Y$:

$$Q = T_3 + \frac{Y}{2}$$Gell-Mann–Nishijima

This relation holds for all quarks and leptons. Since both $T_3$ and $Y$ are conserved in their respective gauge theories, $Q$ is exactly conserved by construction.

02Mathematical Framework

The mathematical statement of charge conservation goes beyond a simple bookkeeping rule — it is a local law encoded in a continuity equation that holds at every point in spacetime.

◈ Charge Density & Current Density
$$\rho(\vec{r},t) = \sum_i q_i\,\delta^{(3)}(\vec{r}-\vec{r}_i(t))$$Charge density [C/m³]

$\rho$ is the electric charge per unit volume at position $\vec{r}$ and time $t$. The total charge is the volume integral:

$$Q = \int_V \rho\,dV$$Total charge
$$\vec{J}(\vec{r},t) = \rho\,\vec{v} = \sum_i q_i\,\vec{v}_i\,\delta^{(3)}(\vec{r}-\vec{r}_i)$$Current density [A/m²]

$\vec{J}$ is the electric current per unit area — the flux of charge through a surface. The current $I$ through area $A$ is:

$$I = \int_A \vec{J}\cdot d\vec{A}$$Electric current
◈ The Continuity Equation — Local Conservation

The central mathematical statement of charge conservation is the continuity equation. It says that charge cannot appear or disappear — it can only flow:

$$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla\cdot\vec{J} = 0$$Continuity equation

This is a local conservation law: charge is conserved not just globally, but at every point in space. The term $\nabla\cdot\vec{J}$ is the divergence of current — the net outflow of charge per unit volume. If charge flows out of a region ($\nabla\cdot\vec{J} > 0$), the density there decreases ($\partial\rho/\partial t < 0$).

Integral form: Integrating over a volume $V$ and applying the divergence theorem:

$$\frac{dQ}{dt} = -\oint_{\partial V} \vec{J}\cdot d\vec{A}$$Gauss's theorem form

The rate of change of charge inside $V$ equals the inward flux of current through its boundary $\partial V$. Charge inside changes only by flowing through the surface.

For isolated systems: If no current crosses the boundary ($\vec{J} = 0$ on $\partial V$), then:

$$\frac{dQ}{dt} = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Q = \text{const}$$Global conservation

Global conservation is a consequence of the local law, but not vice versa. The local continuity equation is the deeper statement — it forbids "teleportation" of charge.

◈ Connection to Gauss's Law

In electrostatics, Gauss's law relates the charge density to the electric field $\vec{E}$:

$$\nabla\cdot\vec{E} = \frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}$$Gauss's law (differential)

Taking the divergence of Ampère's law (with displacement current) and combining with Gauss's law recovers the continuity equation identically. The continuity equation is built into Maxwell's equations — it is not an independent assumption.

$$\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\vec{B}) = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \nabla\cdot\vec{J} + \frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} = 0 \;\checkmark$$From Maxwell equations
◈ Four-Current in Relativity

In special relativity, charge density $\rho$ and current density $\vec{J}$ unite into a single 4-vector:

$$J^\mu = (c\rho,\;\vec{J}) = (c\rho,\; J_x,\; J_y,\; J_z)$$4-current

The continuity equation becomes the manifestly covariant statement:

$$\partial_\mu J^\mu = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} + \nabla\cdot\vec{J} = 0$$Covariant continuity

This form is automatically Lorentz invariant — charge conservation holds in every inertial reference frame. The total charge $Q = \int J^0 dV/c$ transforms as the time component of a 4-vector under boosts, but its value is frame-independent for an isolated system.

◈ Conserved Noether Current

In quantum field theory, the electromagnetic current $J^\mu$ arises as the Noether current of the global $U(1)$ phase symmetry $\psi \to e^{i\alpha}\psi$. For a complex scalar field $\phi$:

$$J^\mu = -i\,(\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi - \phi\,\partial^\mu\phi^*)$$Scalar Noether current

For a Dirac spinor field $\psi$ (electrons, quarks):

$$J^\mu_{\rm em} = \bar\psi\,\gamma^\mu\,\psi$$Dirac current

The conservation $\partial_\mu J^\mu = 0$ follows directly from the equations of motion (Euler-Lagrange equations) whenever the Lagrangian is invariant under $\psi\to e^{i\alpha}\psi$. The conserved charge is:

$$Q = \int J^0\,d^3x = \int \bar\psi\gamma^0\psi\,d^3x = \int \psi^\dagger\psi\,d^3x$$Conserved Noether charge

◈ Beta Decay — A Detailed Example

Neutron beta decay is one of the clearest demonstrations of charge conservation at the nuclear level. A neutron (charge 0) decays via the weak force:

$$n \;\to\; p^+ + e^- + \bar\nu_e$$β⁻ decay

Charge accounting: $0 = (+1) + (-1) + (0)$. The proton carries the newly "created" positive charge, and the electron carries the compensating negative charge. The antineutrino is neutral. Total: 0 → 0. ✓

At the quark level: a down quark ($-\tfrac{1}{3}e$) converts to an up quark ($+\tfrac{2}{3}e$), emitting a virtual $W^-$ boson (charge $-1$), which decays to $e^- + \bar\nu_e$. Charge is conserved at every vertex.

$$d \;\to\; u + W^- \;\to\; u + e^- + \bar\nu_e$$Quark-level process
At each QFT vertex: charge in = charge out. The conservation is vertex-by-vertex, not just globally — this is the local nature of the law.
◈ Pair Production & Annihilation

Pair production: A photon (neutral) converts to an electron–positron pair. Total charge: $0 \to -1 + 1 = 0$. The universe creates charge — but always in equal and opposite pairs.

$$\gamma + \gamma \;\to\; e^- + e^+\quad(Q: 0+0 = -1+1 = 0)$$

Pair annihilation: An electron and positron annihilate to photons. Charge $-1+1 = 0$ before, $0+0 = 0$ after. The charges cancel rather than disappear — annihilation is charge-neutral.

$$e^- + e^+ \;\to\; \gamma + \gamma\quad(Q: -1+1 = 0+0 = 0)$$

These processes also illustrate that "creation" and "destruction" of charged particles always preserves total charge — particles are made or destroyed only in charge-neutral combinations.

05Noether's Theorem & Gauge Symmetry

The deepest explanation of charge conservation is Noether's theorem (1918): every continuous symmetry of the action functional implies a conserved quantity. For electromagnetism, the relevant symmetry is gauge invariance.

◈ Noether's Theorem

If the action $S = \int \mathcal{L}\,d^4x$ is invariant under a continuous one-parameter group of transformations $\phi\to\phi + \epsilon\,\delta\phi$, then there exists a conserved current $J^\mu$ with $\partial_\mu J^\mu = 0$.

$$J^\mu = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\delta\phi - F^\mu$$Noether current

The conserved charge is $Q = \int J^0\,d^3x$. Noether's theorem connects symmetry and conservation at the deepest level: charge conservation is phase symmetry.

The symmetry: The Dirac Lagrangian for a free electron,

$$\mathcal{L} = \bar\psi(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\psi$$

is invariant under the global $U(1)$ phase transformation:

$$\psi(x) \;\to\; e^{i\alpha}\psi(x),\quad \alpha\in\mathbb{R}\;\text{(constant)}$$Global U(1)

This holds because $\bar\psi = \psi^\dagger\gamma^0 \to e^{-i\alpha}\bar\psi$, so $\bar\psi\psi \to e^{-i\alpha}e^{i\alpha}\bar\psi\psi = \bar\psi\psi$. The phase cancels.

The consequence: Applying Noether's theorem with $\delta\psi = i\psi$, the conserved current is:

$$J^\mu = \bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi \quad\Rightarrow\quad \partial_\mu J^\mu = 0$$Conserved EM current

The conserved Noether charge is then:

$$Q = \int J^0\,d^3x = \int\psi^\dagger\psi\,d^3x$$

Interpreting $\psi^\dagger\psi$ as probability density, $Q$ counts the number of particles minus antiparticles — the net charge, in units of $e$.

◈ From Global to Local: Gauge Invariance

If we promote the global symmetry $\alpha = \text{const}$ to a local symmetry $\alpha = \alpha(x)$, the Lagrangian is no longer invariant because $\partial_\mu\psi \to e^{i\alpha}(\partial_\mu + i\partial_\mu\alpha)\psi$. To fix this, we must introduce a gauge field $A_\mu$:

$$\partial_\mu \;\longrightarrow\; D_\mu = \partial_\mu - ieA_\mu$$Covariant derivative

Under the local $U(1)$ transformation $\psi \to e^{i\alpha(x)}\psi$, the gauge field transforms as $A_\mu \to A_\mu + \frac{1}{e}\partial_\mu\alpha$. The full gauge-invariant Lagrangian is:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\rm QED} = \bar\psi(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu - m)\psi - \frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$$QED Lagrangian

The field $A_\mu$ is the electromagnetic field. The photon — the quantum of the electromagnetic field — is a consequence of demanding local gauge invariance. Requiring local $U(1)$ symmetry generates electromagnetism.

Summary of the logical chain: Local $U(1)$ gauge invariance → photon field $A_\mu$ → Maxwell's equations → continuity equation → charge conservation. The electromagnetic force and charge conservation have the same origin: gauge symmetry.

◈ Is Charge Conservation Exact?

Within the Standard Model, charge conservation is exact — a mathematical consequence of $U(1)_{\rm EM}$ gauge invariance. But could it be violated beyond the SM?

①
Millicharged particles: Some extensions predict particles with charge $\epsilon e$ for tiny $\epsilon$. These would appear as missing energy. Severely constrained.
②
Photon mass: A non-zero photon mass $m_\gamma$ would break gauge invariance and potentially allow charge non-conservation. Bound: $m_\gamma < 10^{-18}$ eV.
③
Grand Unified Theories: GUTs predict baryon number violation (proton decay) but typically preserve $B-L$, which protects electric charge. No charge violation predicted in most GUT models.
Current bound: Experimental searches for processes like $e^- \to \nu + \gamma$ set limits on charge-non-conserving amplitudes of $\lesssim 10^{-26}$ relative to normal EM amplitudes. No violation has ever been detected.
◈ Applications of Charge Conservation
Kirchhoff's Laws
Engineering

KCL: the sum of currents entering a circuit node equals the sum leaving. This is the continuity equation applied to lumped circuit elements.

Particle Physics
Detector Design

Charge tracking in detectors allows reconstruction of particle trajectories and identification. Conservation is used to find "missing" neutral particles.

Electrolysis
Chemistry

Faraday's laws of electrolysis follow directly from charge conservation: the mass deposited is proportional to the charge transferred.

Cosmology
Cosmology

The universe appears electrically neutral on cosmological scales. Charge conservation and neutrality are deeply connected to the structure of matter.
