Conservation of Angular Momentum

⛸️ Figure Skater — Moment of Inertia & Spin Rate

A spinning figure skater is the archetypal demonstration of angular momentum conservation. When the skater pulls their arms inward, the moment of inertia $I$ decreases, and since no external torque acts (the ice is nearly frictionless), the angular velocity $\omega$ must increase to keep $L = I\omega$ constant.

$$L = I\,\omega = \text{const}\qquad\text{(no external torque)}$$
$$I_1\,\omega_1 = I_2\,\omega_2\;\Longrightarrow\;\omega_2 = \omega_1\frac{I_1}{I_2}$$

Key Parameters

$L$Angular momentum — conserved throughout the spinkg·m²/s
$I$Moment of inertia $I = I_{\rm core} + 2m_{\rm arm}r^2$ for point-mass armskg·m²
$\omega$Angular velocity — increases as $I$ decreasesrad/s
$r$Arm extension radius from the spin axism
$KE$Rotational kinetic energy $= \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2 = \frac{L^2}{2I}$ — increases as arms pull inJ
Why does KE increase?
The skater does positive work pulling arms inward against the centrifugal tendency. Angular momentum is conserved but kinetic energy $= L^2/(2I)$ grows as $I$ shrinks — the skater's muscular effort supplies the extra energy.
Real-world limits
In practice, ice friction and air resistance provide small external torques that slowly dissipate $L$. The conservation law is an idealization valid over the short timescale of a spin — and it is extraordinarily accurate.
∂ Derivation of the Skater Spin-Up Formula
Model the skater as a cylindrical core plus two point-mass arms
$$I(r) = I_{\rm core} + 2\,m_{\rm arm}\,r^2$$

$I_{\rm core}$ is the moment of inertia of the body trunk. The two arms of mass $m_{\rm arm}$ each sit at radius $r$ from the spin axis.

Write the conservation law for angular momentum
$$L = I(r)\,\omega(r) = \bigl(I_{\rm core}+2m_{\rm arm}r^2\bigr)\,\omega(r) = \text{const}$$
Solve for $\omega$ as a function of arm extension $r$
$$\omega(r) = \frac{L}{I_{\rm core}+2m_{\rm arm}r^2}$$
Compute spin-rate ratio between two arm positions
$$\frac{\omega_2}{\omega_1} = \frac{I_1}{I_2} = \frac{I_{\rm core}+2m_{\rm arm}r_1^2}{I_{\rm core}+2m_{\rm arm}r_2^2}$$
Rotational KE as a function of $r$
$$KE(r)=\frac{L^2}{2(I_{\rm core}+2m_{\rm arm}r^2)}$$
Torque argument: why $L$ is conserved
$$\tau_{\rm ext} = \frac{dL}{dt};\quad\tau_{\rm ice}\approx 0\;\Longrightarrow\;\frac{dL}{dt}\approx 0\;\Longrightarrow\;L=\text{const}\quad\checkmark$$
∫ Worked Cases & Real Numbers

① Arms fully extended → fully retracted  $r_1=0.8$ m → $r_2=0.1$ m

$$\frac{I_1}{I_2}=\frac{2.5+2(0.4)(0.64)}{2.5+2(0.4)(0.01)}=\frac{3.012}{2.508}\approx 1.20$$

② Negligible core — pure arm extension  $I_{\rm core}\to 0$

$$\frac{\omega_2}{\omega_1}=\left(\frac{r_1}{r_2}\right)^2\qquad\Longrightarrow\quad r_1=0.8,\;r_2=0.1:\;\frac{\omega_2}{\omega_1}=64$$

🌍 Orbital Mechanics — Kepler's Second Law

A planet orbiting under a central gravitational force conserves angular momentum because gravity always acts along the radial direction $\hat{r}$ — producing zero torque about the Sun. This directly implies Kepler's Second Law: a planet sweeps equal areas in equal times.

$$L = m\,r\,v_\perp = m\,r^2\,\dot\theta = \text{const}$$
$$\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{1}{2}r^2\dot\theta = \frac{L}{2m} = \text{const}\qquad\text{(Kepler II)}$$

Key Parameters

$a$Semi-major axis of the ellipsem (AU)
$e$Eccentricity — $0$ = circular, $0{<}e{<}1$ = ellipsedimensionless
$r(\theta)$Orbital radius: $r = a(1-e^2)/(1+e\cos\theta)$m
$v_\perp$Tangential velocity component $= r\dot\theta$ — larger at perihelionm/s
$T$Orbital period — related to $a$ by Kepler III: $T^2\propto a^3$s
Central force → zero torque
Torque $\tau = \vec r \times \vec F$. For gravity, $\vec F \parallel \hat r$, so $\vec r \times \vec F = 0$ at every instant. Therefore $dL/dt = \tau = 0$, and $L$ is conserved for all orbital shapes.
Speed variation along the orbit
At perihelion (closest approach), $r$ is small so $v_\perp = L/(mr)$ is large. At aphelion (farthest point), $r$ is large so the planet moves slowly. The product $r\,v_\perp$ stays constant throughout.
∂ Derivation of Orbital Angular Momentum Conservation
Write angular momentum in polar coordinates
$$\vec L = m\,r\hat r\times(\dot r\hat r+r\dot\theta\hat\theta)=m\,r^2\dot\theta\,\hat z$$
Show that gravity exerts zero torque about the focus
$$\vec F_g = -\frac{GMm}{r^2}\hat r\;\Longrightarrow\;\tau=\vec r\times\vec F_g = 0\quad(\hat r\times\hat r=0)$$
Apply Newton's second law in the $\hat\theta$ direction
$$m(r\ddot\theta+2\dot r\dot\theta)=0\;\Longrightarrow\;\frac{d}{dt}(r^2\dot\theta)=0\;\Longrightarrow\;r^2\dot\theta=h=\text{const}$$
Derive Kepler's Second Law from $h = $ const
$$\frac{dA}{dt}=\tfrac{1}{2}r^2\dot\theta=\tfrac{h}{2}=\frac{L}{2m}=\text{const}\quad\checkmark$$
Derive the orbit shape
$$r=\frac{h^2/(GM)}{1+e\cos\theta}$$
Orbital speed at any point
$$v_\perp = r\dot\theta = h/r = \frac{L}{mr}\;\Longrightarrow\; v_{\rm peri}=\frac{L}{mr_{\rm min}}\quad\checkmark$$
∫ Orbital Speed & Area Sweep — Key Results

① Speed ratio perihelion/aphelion  $e > 0$

$$\frac{v_{\rm peri}}{v_{\rm aph}} = \frac{r_{\rm aph}}{r_{\rm peri}} = \frac{1+e}{1-e}$$

💫 Collapsing Star — Neutron Star & Pulsar Formation

When a massive star exhausts its nuclear fuel, its core collapses catastrophically from a radius of ~500,000 km to ~10 km — a factor of 50,000. With no external torque acting on the infalling material, angular momentum is conserved. The resulting neutron star spins hundreds of times per second.

$$L = I\,\omega = \tfrac{2}{5}MR^2\,\omega = \text{const}$$
$$\omega_f = \omega_i\,\frac{R_i^2}{R_f^2}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{collapse by }50{,}000\times\text{ multiplies }\omega\text{ by }2.5\times10^9$$

Key Parameters

$R_i$Initial stellar radius — the pre-collapse core radiuskm
$R_f$Final neutron star radius — typically $\sim 10$ kmkm
$I$Moment of inertia of uniform sphere: $I = \frac{2}{5}MR^2$kg·m²
$\omega$Angular velocity — scales as $1/R^2$ during collapserad/s
$P$Rotation period $= 2\pi/\omega$ — milliseconds for fast pulsarss
The $R^2$ scaling law
For a uniform sphere $I \propto R^2$, so $\omega \propto 1/R^2$. Halving the radius increases spin rate by 4×. A collapse by 10,000× in radius multiplies spin rate by $10^8$ — a slow-rotating star becomes a millisecond pulsar.
Galactic disk formation — same physics
A diffuse gas cloud 100 light-years across collapses to form a galaxy. The same $L$-conservation law flattens it into a rapidly spinning disk — explaining why spiral galaxies have their characteristic flat shape.
∂ Derivation of Spin-Up During Gravitational Collapse
Moment of inertia of a uniform sphere
$$I = \frac{2}{5}MR^2$$
Conservation law for the collapsing star
$$L_i = L_f\;\Longrightarrow\;\frac{2}{5}MR_i^2\,\omega_i = \frac{2}{5}MR_f^2\,\omega_f$$
Solve for final angular velocity
$$\boxed{\omega_f = \omega_i\left(\frac{R_i}{R_f}\right)^2}$$
Express as rotation period
$$P_f = P_i\left(\frac{R_f}{R_i}\right)^2$$
Kinetic energy ratio
$$\frac{KE_f}{KE_i}=\left(\frac{R_i}{R_f}\right)^2$$
∫ Real Stellar Collapse Numbers

① Typical neutron star formation  $R_i=500{,}000$ km → $R_f=10$ km

$$\frac{\omega_f}{\omega_i}=\left(\frac{500{,}000}{10}\right)^2 = 2.5\times10^9$$

🌀 Gyroscope — Precession & Gyroscopic Stability

A spinning gyroscope resists toppling under gravity. Instead of falling, its spin axis sweeps out a slow cone — precession. This happens because the gravitational torque is always perpendicular to the spin angular momentum $L$, so it changes $L$'s direction but never its magnitude.

$$\vec L = I\,\omega_s\,\hat n\qquad\text{(spin angular momentum along the axis } \hat n\text{)}$$
$$\vec\tau = \vec d\times\vec F_g\;\Longrightarrow\;\tau = m\,g\,d\sin\theta$$
$$\Omega_{\rm prec} = \frac{\tau}{L\sin\theta} = \frac{m\,g\,d}{I\,\omega_s}$$

Key Parameters

$L$Spin angular momentum — constant magnitude, precessing directionkg·m²/s
$\tau$Gravitational torque about the pivot — always $\perp$ to $L$N·m
$\Omega$Precession angular velocity — rate the axis sweeps around verticalrad/s
$\omega_s$Spin rate of the flywheel about its own axisrad/s
$\theta$Tilt angle of the spin axis from verticaldeg
$d$Distance from pivot to center of massm
Why doesn't it fall?
Torque is always perpendicular to $L$, so $dL$ only rotates $L$'s direction — never adds to its magnitude in the direction of gravity. The axis traces a cone instead of collapsing.
Fast-top approximation
$\Omega=mgd/(I\omega_s)$ holds when spin dominates ($I\omega_s$ large). Real gyroscopes also nutate — a small wobble in $\theta$ superimposed on precession — which damps out faster at higher spin rates. That's gyroscopic stability.
∂ Derivation of Steady Precession
Torque about the pivot from gravity
$$\vec\tau=\vec d\times\vec F_g,\quad \vec F_g=-mg\hat y\;\Longrightarrow\;\tau = mgd\sin\theta\;\;(\perp\text{ to both }\vec d\text{ and }\hat y)$$
Torque equals rate of change of $L$
$$\frac{d\vec L}{dt}=\vec\tau,\qquad \vec\tau\perp\vec L\;\Longrightarrow\;|\vec L|=\text{const, only direction changes}$$
$L$ sweeps a cone of half-angle $\theta$
$$|d\vec L| = L\sin\theta\,d\phi$$

where $\phi$ is the precession (azimuthal) angle around the vertical.

Equate the two expressions for $dL$
$$\tau\,dt = L\sin\theta\,d\phi\;\Longrightarrow\;\Omega=\frac{d\phi}{dt}=\frac{\tau}{L\sin\theta}$$
Substitute $\tau=mgd\sin\theta$ and $L=I\omega_s$
$$\boxed{\Omega = \frac{mgd\sin\theta}{I\omega_s\sin\theta} = \frac{mgd}{I\omega_s}}$$

The $\sin\theta$ cancels — precession rate is independent of tilt angle.

Stability condition
$$\Omega \ll \omega_s\;\Longrightarrow\;\frac{mgd}{I\omega_s^2}\ll 1\quad\text{(fast spin → slow, stable precession)}$$

⚛️ Quantum Spin — Larmor Precession & Conservation of $|\vec S|$

Spin is intrinsic angular momentum with no classical counterpart, yet it obeys the same conservation law: absent an external torque, $|\vec S|$ is fixed. Placed in a magnetic field $\vec B = B\hat z$, the spin experiences a torque from the interaction $H=-\gamma\vec S\cdot\vec B$ that precesses $\langle\vec S\rangle$ around $\hat z$ at the Larmor frequency — exactly like the gyroscope, but with $S_z$ quantized into $2s{+}1$ discrete outcomes.

$$|\vec S|^2 = \hbar^2 s(s+1) = \text{const}\qquad\text{(magnitude conserved)}$$
$$\omega_L = \gamma B\qquad\text{(Larmor precession frequency)}$$

Key Parameters

$s$Spin quantum number — sets $|\vec S|=\hbar\sqrt{s(s+1)}$dimensionless
$m_s$Spin projection quantum number, $m_s = -s,\dots,+s$ in integer stepsdimensionless
$\gamma$Gyromagnetic ratio — coupling between spin and $\vec B$rad/(s·T)
$B$Applied magnetic field along $\hat z$T
$\omega_L$Larmor frequency $=\gamma B$ — rate $\langle S_x\rangle,\langle S_y\rangle$ rotaterad/s
Why $S_z$ stays fixed
With $\vec B\parallel\hat z$, $[H,S_z]=0$, so $S_z$ is a conserved quantity — only the transverse components precess. This is the quantum analogue of the gyroscope's constant tilt angle $\theta$.
Quantization vs. the classical cone
The precessing $\langle\vec S\rangle$ traces a smooth classical cone, but any single measurement of $S_z$ only ever returns one of the $2s{+}1$ discrete values $m_s\hbar$ — the cone describes the expectation value of an ensemble, not a single trajectory.
∂ Derivation of Larmor Precession from the Heisenberg Equation
Interaction Hamiltonian for spin in a field
$$H = -\gamma\,\vec S\cdot\vec B = -\gamma B\,S_z$$
Heisenberg equation of motion for each spin operator
$$\frac{dS_i}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar}[H,S_i] = -\frac{i\gamma B}{\hbar}[S_z,S_i]$$
Use the spin commutation relations $[S_i,S_j]=i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}S_k$
$$\frac{dS_x}{dt}=\gamma B\,S_y,\qquad \frac{dS_y}{dt}=-\gamma B\,S_x,\qquad \frac{dS_z}{dt}=0$$
Solve the coupled equations — rotation in the $xy$-plane
$$S_x(t)=S_x(0)\cos\omega_L t+S_y(0)\sin\omega_L t,\quad \omega_L=\gamma B$$
Show $S_z$ and $|\vec S|^2$ are conserved
$$S_z(t)=S_z(0)=\text{const}\qquad|\vec S|^2=S_x^2+S_y^2+S_z^2=\text{const}\quad\checkmark$$

$[H,S^2]=0$ since $S^2$ commutes with every component of $\vec S$.

Zeeman splitting of the $2s{+}1$ sublevels
$$E_{m_s} = -\gamma\hbar B\,m_s\;\Longrightarrow\;\Delta E = \gamma\hbar B\quad\text{between adjacent }m_s$$
∫ Worked Cases & Real Systems

① Spin-1/2 precession  $s=\tfrac12$, $B=1$ T

$$|\vec S|=\hbar\sqrt{\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac32}=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\hbar\qquad m_s=\pm\tfrac12\;\Longrightarrow\;\Delta E=\gamma\hbar B$$

🎯 Total Angular Momentum — Spin-Orbit Coupling & Fine Structure

When spin-orbit coupling $H_{SO}=\zeta(r)\,\vec L\cdot\vec S$ is added to the Hamiltonian, neither $\vec L$ nor $\vec S$ is separately conserved — but the total angular momentum $\vec J=\vec L+\vec S$ is, because rotations mix $L$ and $S$ together and the full Hamiltonian is rotationally invariant. Classically, $\vec L$ and $\vec S$ precess around the fixed $\vec J$ direction while their sum stays constant, and the total energy (including the fine-structure shift) is conserved throughout.

$$\vec J=\vec L+\vec S,\qquad [H,\vec J]=0\;\Longrightarrow\;\vec J=\text{const}$$
$$[H,\vec L]\neq0,\quad[H,\vec S]\neq0\qquad\text{(individually NOT conserved)}$$

Key Parameters

$l$Orbital angular momentum quantum numberdimensionless
$s$Spin quantum number (electron: $s=1/2$)dimensionless
$j$Total angular momentum quantum number, $j=l+s$ or $j=l-s$dimensionless
$\zeta$Spin-orbit coupling constant — sets fine-structure energy scaleeV
$E_{SO}$Spin-orbit energy shift $=\frac{\zeta}{2}[j(j+1)-l(l+1)-s(s+1)]$eV
Why $\vec J$ survives when $\vec L,\vec S$ don't
$H_{SO}=\zeta\,\vec L\cdot\vec S$ commutes with $J^2$ and $J_z$ because $\vec L\cdot\vec S=\frac12(J^2-L^2-S^2)$ — a function of the conserved operators $J^2,L^2,S^2$. But $[\vec L,\vec L\cdot\vec S]\neq0$, so $\vec L$ precesses; likewise $\vec S$.
Fine structure — an energy fingerprint
Each $l$ level splits into $j=l+\tfrac12$ and $j=l-\tfrac12$ sublevels of slightly different energy. This is the origin of the sodium D-line doublet and hydrogen's fine structure, both direct evidence of $J$-conservation with individually precessing $L,S$.
∂ Derivation of J-Conservation and the Vector Model
Spin-orbit Hamiltonian
$$H = H_0 + \zeta(r)\,\vec L\cdot\vec S$$
Rewrite $\vec L\cdot\vec S$ using $J^2=L^2+S^2+2\vec L\cdot\vec S$
$$\vec L\cdot\vec S = \tfrac12\bigl(J^2-L^2-S^2\bigr)$$
Since $[J^2,L^2]=[J^2,S^2]=0$, confirm $J^2$ and $J_z$ commute with $H$
$$[H,J^2]=0,\quad[H,J_z]=0\;\Longrightarrow\;j,\,m_j\text{ are good quantum numbers}$$
Classical torque equations — $L$ and $S$ exert equal/opposite torque on each other
$$\frac{d\vec L}{dt}=\frac{\zeta}{\hbar}\,\vec L\times\vec S,\qquad \frac{d\vec S}{dt}=-\frac{\zeta}{\hbar}\,\vec L\times\vec S=\frac{\zeta}{\hbar}\,\vec S\times\vec L$$
Sum vanishes — $J$ is fixed; each vector precesses about $\vec J$
$$\frac{d\vec J}{dt}=\frac{d\vec L}{dt}+\frac{d\vec S}{dt}=0\qquad\checkmark$$

Using $\vec L\times\vec S=\vec L\times(\vec J-\vec L)=\vec L\times\vec J$, both $\vec L$ and $\vec S$ precess about $\hat J$ at the same rate $\Omega=\zeta J/\hbar$.

Landé vector-model angles (law of cosines in the $L,S,J$ triangle)
$$\cos\theta_{LJ}=\frac{j(j+1)+l(l+1)-s(s+1)}{2\sqrt{j(j+1)}\sqrt{l(l+1)}}\qquad\cos\theta_{SJ}=\frac{j(j+1)+s(s+1)-l(l+1)}{2\sqrt{j(j+1)}\sqrt{s(s+1)}}$$
Fine-structure energy shift
$$\boxed{E_{SO}=\frac{\zeta}{2}\bigl[j(j+1)-l(l+1)-s(s+1)\bigr]}$$
∫ Worked Cases & Real Spectra

① Hydrogen 2p state  $l=1,\,s=\tfrac12,\,j=\tfrac32\text{ or }\tfrac12$

$$E_{SO}(j{=}3/2)=\zeta,\qquad E_{SO}(j{=}1/2)=-\zeta\qquad\Delta E_{\rm fine}=\tfrac{3}{2}\zeta\approx4.5\times10^{-5}\text{ eV}$$
