01 — The Principle

What is Angular Momentum?

Angular momentum is the rotational analogue of linear momentum. Just as a moving object resists changes to its velocity, a rotating object resists changes to its spin. It is a vector quantity — it has both magnitude and direction.

Conservation Law: In a system where no external torque acts, the total angular momentum remains constant. This is not a rule we imposed — it flows directly from the rotational symmetry of space itself (Noether's theorem).

L = r × p
Angular momentum as cross product of radius and linear momentum
L = I · ω
For rotating rigid bodies: moment of inertia × angular velocity
τ = dL/dt
Torque equals rate of change — zero torque means L is conserved
I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂
When moment of inertia changes, angular velocity adjusts accordingly

The Key Variables

L — Angular Momentum (kg·m²/s)  ·  I — Moment of Inertia (kg·m²)  ·  ω — Angular Velocity (rad/s)  ·  r — Radius vector from axis  ·  p — Linear momentum (mv)

03 — The Mathematics

Why L is Conserved

The conservation law follows directly from Newton's second law applied to rotation. Here is the logical chain:

1
Define angular momentum for a particle: L = r × p = r × mv
2
Differentiate with respect to time: dL/dt = (dr/dt × mv) + (r × m·dv/dt)
3
The first term v × mv = m(v × v) = 0 since any vector crossed with itself is zero.
4
The second term gives r × F = τ (torque), so: dL/dt = τ
5
Therefore, if net external torque τ = 0, then dL/dt = 0, meaning L = constant. ∎

Moment of Inertia — The Key to the Skater

For a point mass: I = mr². For an extended body, we integrate over all mass elements: I = ∫r² dm. When mass redistributes closer to the axis of rotation, I decreases. Since L = Iω is fixed, ω must increase — spin speeds up.

04 — Orbital Mechanics

Kepler's Second Law

A planet sweeps equal areas in equal times — a direct consequence of angular momentum conservation. Gravity acts through the Sun (central force), so torque is zero and L is conserved. The planet speeds up near perihelion and slows at aphelion.

05 — In the Real World

Nature's Spinning Wonders

⛸️

Figure Skater

Pulling arms inward reduces moment of inertia, dramatically increasing spin rate. The quintessential demonstration of L conservation.

🌍

Forming Planets

A slowly rotating gas cloud collapses under gravity. As it shrinks, I decreases enormously, creating a rapidly spinning planetary disk.

🌀

Neutron Stars

A dying star (radius ~700,000 km) collapses to ~10 km. The result: a pulsar spinning hundreds of times per second.

🪀

Gyroscopes

A spinning gyroscope resists changes in orientation because altering its axis would require changing L — which needs a torque.

🏊

Diving & Gymnastics

Tuck position = small I = fast spin. Open position = large I = slow spin. Athletes control rotation precisely with body position.

🔭

Galaxy Formation

The spiral arms of our Milky Way exist because the original gas cloud that collapsed had angular momentum — it couldn't all fall to the center.

06 — Deeper Insight

Symmetry & Noether's Theorem

In 1915, Emmy Noether proved one of the most profound results in physics: every continuous symmetry of nature corresponds to a conserved quantity.

The conservation of angular momentum arises from rotational symmetry — the fact that the laws of physics are the same regardless of which direction you face. Rotating your lab by any angle does not change the outcome of any experiment. This symmetry directly guarantees that angular momentum is conserved.

Similarly: translational symmetry → conservation of linear momentum; time symmetry → conservation of energy. Angular momentum conservation is not just a rule — it is a deep truth about the geometry of the universe.

Quantum Angular Momentum

In quantum mechanics, angular momentum is quantized. Orbital angular momentum comes in integer multiples of ℏ (reduced Planck constant), while spin angular momentum can be half-integer. The same conservation law holds — spin is conserved in particle interactions, governing atomic transitions, nuclear reactions, and the structure of matter itself.
