🔗 Pendulum — Energy Conservation with Friction

A pendulum of length $L$ and mass $m$ continuously converts energy between kinetic and gravitational potential forms. Viscous friction irreversibly converts mechanical energy into heat (thermal energy), yet the total energy remains perfectly constant.

$$E_{\rm total} = \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}mL^2\omega^2}_{KE} + \underbrace{mgL(1-\cos\theta)}_{PE} + \underbrace{Q_{\rm thermal}}_{\text{friction}} = E_0 = \text{const}$$

Energy Components

KEKinetic $\frac{1}{2}mL^2\omega^2$ — bob velocityJ
PEGravitational $mgL(1-\cos\theta)$ — height above equilibriumJ
$Q$Thermal $= E_0 - KE - PE$ — heat from frictionJ
$E_0$Initial energy $= mgL(1-\cos\theta_0)$ (starts at rest)J

Equation of Motion

$$\ddot{\theta} + b\,\dot{\theta} + \frac{g}{L}\sin\theta = 0$$

$b$ is the viscous damping coefficient (s⁻¹), equivalent to $b = c/(mL^2)$ where $c$ is the physical rotational friction constant.

⚠️ Time-reversal asymmetry: The damping term $b\dot\theta$ breaks time-reversal symmetry. Energy flows only from mechanical → thermal, never back. This is a fundamental thermodynamic arrow of time.
💡 Energy oscillations: Without friction, KE and PE oscillate perfectly anti-phase — when $\theta=0$ all energy is kinetic; at $\theta=\theta_0$ all energy is potential. Friction shrinks this oscillation while the thermal reservoir grows.
∂ Energy Derivation — Work-Energy Theorem
Rotational Newton's 2nd Law (torque = $I\alpha$)

Two torques act on the bob: gravity restoring torque $(-mgL\sin\theta)$ and friction torque $(-c\dot\theta = -bmL^2\dot\theta)$:

$$mL^2\ddot{\theta} = -mgL\sin\theta - b\,mL^2\,\dot{\theta}$$

Divide by $mL^2$:  $\ddot\theta + b\dot\theta + \dfrac{g}{L}\sin\theta = 0$

Multiply both sides by $\dot\theta$ — extract power
$$mL^2\dot\theta\,\ddot\theta\;+\; mgL\sin\theta\cdot\dot\theta\;+\; b\,mL^2\dot\theta^2 = 0$$
Identify each term as a time derivative
$$\frac{d}{dt}\!\underbrace{\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}mL^2\dot\theta^2\Bigr)}_{KE}\;+\;\frac{d}{dt}\!\underbrace{\Bigl(mgL(1-\cos\theta)\Bigr)}_{PE}\;=\;-\underbrace{b\,mL^2\omega^2}_{\dot{Q}\geq 0}$$

The left side is $\dot{ME} = \frac{d}{dt}(KE + PE)$ and the right side is the dissipation power.

Define thermal energy generated by friction
$$\dot{Q} = b\,mL^2\omega^2 \geq 0 \implies Q(t) = \int_0^t b\,mL^2\,\omega^2(\tau)\,d\tau$$

Heat is generated at rate $\propto \omega^2$. Since $\dot Q \geq 0$ always, thermal energy only ever increases.

Total energy conservation — the fundamental result
$$\frac{d}{dt}\!\bigl(KE + PE + Q\bigr) = 0 \implies E_{\rm total} = E_0 = mgL(1-\cos\theta_0) = \text{const}$$

In practice: $Q(t) = E_0 - KE(t) - PE(t)$. This is computed exactly from the RK4 trajectory.

Rate of mechanical energy loss
$$\frac{dME}{dt} = -b\,mL^2\omega^2 \leq 0$$

Mechanical energy decreases fastest when $\omega$ is largest (at equilibrium). The system "leaks" energy fastest near $\theta = 0$.

∫ Analytical Energy Expressions

For small angles ($\theta \ll 1$, so $\sin\theta \approx \theta$), the EOM linearises and gives exact closed-form solutions.

Underdamped ($\gamma = b/2 < \omega_0 = \sqrt{g/L}$)

$$\theta(t) = \theta_0\,e^{-\gamma t}\cos(\omega_d t),\quad \omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}$$
$$ME(t) \approx E_0\,e^{-bt}$$

Mechanical energy decays exponentially with time constant $\tau_E = 1/b$.

$$Q(t) \approx E_0\!\left(1 - e^{-bt}\right)$$

Thermal energy grows from 0 to $E_0$ as $t\to\infty$.

Critical Damping ($b = 2\omega_0$)

$$\theta(t) = \theta_0(1+\omega_0 t)\,e^{-\omega_0 t}\quad\text{(fastest return, no oscillation)}$$

Overdamped ($\gamma > \omega_0$)

$$\theta(t) = \theta_0\frac{r_2 e^{r_1 t} - r_1 e^{r_2 t}}{r_2 - r_1},\quad r_{1,2} = -\gamma \pm \sqrt{\gamma^2-\omega_0^2}$$

Large-Angle Period (Exact)

$$T = 4\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}\,K\!\left(\sin\frac{\theta_0}{2}\right), \quad K(k)=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{d\varphi}{\sqrt{1-k^2\sin^2\varphi}}$$

🔗 Coupled Pendulums — Spring-Linked Energy Exchange

Two pendulums (lengths $L_1,L_2$, masses $m_1,m_2$) hang from separate pivots and swing in the same vertical plane. A horizontal spring of constant $k$ connects the two bobs. Gravity supplies the restoring torque for each pendulum individually, while the spring couples their motion — energy sloshes back and forth between the bobs exactly as it does between the two masses in the Three-Springs system, but here the "springs to the wall" are replaced by gravity.

$$E_{\rm total}=\underbrace{\tfrac12 m_1L_1^2\dot\theta_1^2+\tfrac12 m_2L_2^2\dot\theta_2^2}_{KE}+\underbrace{m_1gL_1(1-\cos\theta_1)+m_2gL_2(1-\cos\theta_2)+\tfrac12k(L_1\sin\theta_1-L_2\sin\theta_2)^2}_{PE}+\underbrace{Q_{\rm thermal}}_{\text{friction}}=E_0=\text{const}$$

Energy Components

$KE_i$Kinetic of bob $i$: $\tfrac12m_iL_i^2\dot\theta_i^2$J
$PE_i$Gravitational: $m_igL_i(1-\cos\theta_i)$J
$PE_k$Spring: $\tfrac12k(L_1\sin\theta_1-L_2\sin\theta_2)^2$J
$Q$Thermal $=E_0-KE-PE$ — heat from frictionJ

Equations of Motion

$$\ddot\theta_1=-\frac{g}{L_1}\sin\theta_1-\frac{k\cos\theta_1}{m_1L_1}\bigl(L_1\sin\theta_1-L_2\sin\theta_2\bigr)-b\dot\theta_1$$ $$\ddot\theta_2=-\frac{g}{L_2}\sin\theta_2+\frac{k\cos\theta_2}{m_2L_2}\bigl(L_1\sin\theta_1-L_2\sin\theta_2\bigr)-b\dot\theta_2$$

The spring is modelled as horizontal, connecting the bobs' horizontal positions $x_i=L_i\sin\theta_i$. Damping $b$ (s⁻¹) acts on each pendulum, identical in form to the single-pendulum friction term.

∂ Energy Derivation — Lagrangian / Work-Energy Theorem
Equations of motion (torque balance, including spring + friction)
$$m_1L_1^2\ddot\theta_1=-m_1gL_1\sin\theta_1-kL_1\cos\theta_1\bigl(L_1\sin\theta_1-L_2\sin\theta_2\bigr)-bm_1L_1^2\dot\theta_1$$ $$m_2L_2^2\ddot\theta_2=-m_2gL_2\sin\theta_2+kL_2\cos\theta_2\bigl(L_1\sin\theta_1-L_2\sin\theta_2\bigr)-bm_2L_2^2\dot\theta_2$$
Multiply each equation by its own $\dot\theta_i$ and add — extract power balance
$$m_1L_1^2\dot\theta_1\ddot\theta_1+m_1gL_1\sin\theta_1\,\dot\theta_1+m_2L_2^2\dot\theta_2\ddot\theta_2+m_2gL_2\sin\theta_2\,\dot\theta_2$$ $$+\,k\bigl(L_1\sin\theta_1-L_2\sin\theta_2\bigr)\bigl(L_1\cos\theta_1\dot\theta_1-L_2\cos\theta_2\dot\theta_2\bigr)=-b\bigl(m_1L_1^2\dot\theta_1^2+m_2L_2^2\dot\theta_2^2\bigr)$$
Identify each term as a time derivative
$$\frac{d}{dt}\Bigl[\underbrace{\tfrac12m_1L_1^2\dot\theta_1^2+\tfrac12m_2L_2^2\dot\theta_2^2}_{KE}+\underbrace{m_1gL_1(1-\cos\theta_1)+m_2gL_2(1-\cos\theta_2)}_{PE_{\rm grav}}+\underbrace{\tfrac12k(L_1\sin\theta_1-L_2\sin\theta_2)^2}_{PE_{\rm spring}}\Bigr]=-\underbrace{b\bigl(m_1L_1^2\dot\theta_1^2+m_2L_2^2\dot\theta_2^2\bigr)}_{\dot Q\geq0}$$

The spring term on the left is exactly $\tfrac{d}{dt}\bigl[\tfrac12(L_1\sin\theta_1-L_2\sin\theta_2)^2\bigr]$ — its chain-rule expansion is the coupling term that appears in Step 2.

Thermal energy generated by friction
$$\dot Q=b\bigl(m_1L_1^2\dot\theta_1^2+m_2L_2^2\dot\theta_2^2\bigr)\geq0 \implies Q(t)=\int_0^t\dot Q\,d\tau$$
Total energy conservation — the fundamental result
$$\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(KE+PE_{\rm grav}+PE_{\rm spring}+Q\bigr)=0 \implies E_{\rm total}=E_0=\text{const}$$

In practice: $Q(t)=E_0-KE(t)-PE_{\rm grav}(t)-PE_{\rm spring}(t)$, computed exactly from the RK4 trajectory.

Small-angle linearisation → normal modes

For $\theta_1,\theta_2\ll1$, the EOM linearise to $\mathbf M\ddot{\boldsymbol\theta}+\mathbf K\boldsymbol\theta=0$ with $\mathbf M={\rm diag}(m_1L_1^2,\,m_2L_2^2)$ and:

$$\mathbf K=\begin{pmatrix}m_1gL_1+kL_1^2 & -kL_1L_2\\-kL_1L_2 & m_2gL_2+kL_2^2\end{pmatrix}$$

Solving $\det(\mathbf K-\omega^2\mathbf M)=0$ with $a=\dfrac{g}{L_1}+\dfrac{k}{m_1}$, $d=\dfrac{g}{L_2}+\dfrac{k}{m_2}$:

$$\omega_\pm^2=\frac{a+d}{2}\pm\sqrt{\frac{(a-d)^2}{4}+\frac{k^2}{m_1m_2}},\qquad T_{\rm beat}=\frac{2\pi}{\omega_+-\omega_-}$$

$$\omega_-^2=\frac{g}{L},\qquad \omega_+^2=\frac{g}{L}+\frac{2k}{m}$$

With Damping

🌀 Double Pendulum — Chaos with Exact Energy Conservation

A second pendulum hangs from the bob of the first — mass $m_1$ on a rod of length $L_1$, with mass $m_2$ on a second rod of length $L_2$ attached at $m_1$. Unlike every other system in this app, its motion has no closed-form solution: for large release angles the trajectory is chaotic, meaning two runs that start a fraction of a degree apart diverge completely within seconds. Yet at every instant, exactly as with the single pendulum, total energy is perfectly conserved.

$$E_{\rm total}=\underbrace{\tfrac12(m_1+m_2)L_1^2\dot\theta_1^2+\tfrac12m_2L_2^2\dot\theta_2^2+m_2L_1L_2\dot\theta_1\dot\theta_2\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)}_{KE}+\underbrace{(m_1+m_2)gL_1(1-\cos\theta_1)+m_2gL_2(1-\cos\theta_2)}_{PE}+\underbrace{Q_{\rm thermal}}_{\text{friction}}=E_0=\text{const}$$

$PE$Gravitational, each bob measured from its own lowest pointJ
$Q$Thermal $=E_0-KE-PE$ — heat from friction, if anyJ

🖼 Mechanical Model
∂ Derivation — Lagrangian Mechanics
Bob positions and velocities (pivot at origin)
$$x_1=L_1\sin\theta_1,\;\; y_1=L_1\cos\theta_1$$$$x_2=L_1\sin\theta_1+L_2\sin\theta_2,\;\; y_2=L_1\cos\theta_1+L_2\cos\theta_2$$

Differentiating gives $v_1^2=L_1^2\dot\theta_1^2$ and $v_2^2=L_1^2\dot\theta_1^2+L_2^2\dot\theta_2^2+2L_1L_2\dot\theta_1\dot\theta_2\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)$ — the cross term is what couples the two rods.

Lagrangian $\mathcal L=T-V$
$$\mathcal L=\tfrac12(m_1+m_2)L_1^2\dot\theta_1^2+\tfrac12m_2L_2^2\dot\theta_2^2+m_2L_1L_2\dot\theta_1\dot\theta_2\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)-\bigl[(m_1+m_2)gL_1(1-\cos\theta_1)+m_2gL_2(1-\cos\theta_2)\bigr]$$
Euler–Lagrange equations $\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\dot\theta_i}-\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\theta_i}=0$ — the standard result
$$\ddot\theta_1=\frac{-g(2m_1+m_2)\sin\theta_1-m_2g\sin(\theta_1-2\theta_2)-2\sin(\theta_1-\theta_2)\,m_2\bigl(\dot\theta_2^2L_2+\dot\theta_1^2L_1\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)\bigr)}{L_1\bigl(2m_1+m_2-m_2\cos(2\theta_1-2\theta_2)\bigr)}$$ $$\ddot\theta_2=\frac{2\sin(\theta_1-\theta_2)\bigl(\dot\theta_1^2L_1(m_1+m_2)+g(m_1+m_2)\cos\theta_1+\dot\theta_2^2L_2m_2\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)\bigr)}{L_2\bigl(2m_1+m_2-m_2\cos(2\theta_1-2\theta_2)\bigr)}$$

These two coupled, nonlinear ODEs have no general analytic solution — every trajectory in the Simulation tab comes from RK4 integrating them directly, exactly like every other system here.

Optional friction — same ad hoc treatment as the single pendulum
$$\ddot\theta_1\to\ddot\theta_1-b\dot\theta_1,\qquad \ddot\theta_2\to\ddot\theta_2-b\dot\theta_2$$

A linear angular-damping term is subtracted from each acceleration, same convention as the single-pendulum tab. Because the two rods are coupled, an exact closed-form expression for the dissipation rate $\dot Q$ is unwieldy, so thermal energy is tracked the same way as in every tab: $Q(t)=E_0-KE(t)-PE(t)$, guaranteed non-negative by construction.

Total energy conservation still holds exactly
$$\frac{d}{dt}(KE+PE+Q)=0\;\Longrightarrow\;E_{\rm total}=E_0=(m_1+m_2)gL_1(1-\cos\theta_{10})+m_2gL_2(1-\cos\theta_{20})=\text{const}$$

Chaos affects the path the system takes through $(\theta_1,\theta_2,\dot\theta_1,\dot\theta_2)$-space — it never affects this identity.

∫ Small-Angle Normal Modes (the only closed form that exists)

For $\theta_1,\theta_2\ll1$, linearising $\sin\theta_i\approx\theta_i$ and $\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)\approx1$ reduces the exact equations to a linear, solvable system:

$$(m_1+m_2)L_1\ddot\theta_1+m_2L_2\ddot\theta_2+(m_1+m_2)g\theta_1=0$$$$L_1\ddot\theta_1+L_2\ddot\theta_2+g\theta_2=0$$

This is a standard $\mathbf M\ddot{\boldsymbol\theta}+\mathbf K\boldsymbol\theta=0$ eigenvalue problem, giving two normal-mode frequencies:

$$m_1L_1L_2\,\omega^4-(m_1+m_2)g(L_1+L_2)\,\omega^2+(m_1+m_2)g^2=0$$

Equal Masses & Lengths ($m_1=m_2=m$, $L_1=L_2=L$)

$$\omega_-^2=(2-\sqrt2)\frac{g}{L}\approx0.586\frac{g}{L}\quad\text{(in-phase-ish)},\qquad\omega_+^2=(2+\sqrt2)\frac{g}{L}\approx3.414\frac{g}{L}\quad\text{(out-of-phase-ish)}$$

For small releases the motion is a superposition of these two modes — quasi-periodic and fully predictable, same flavour as the Coupled Pendulums tab.

⚛️ Charged Particle — Lorentz Force Energy Conservation

A charged particle ($q$, $m$) moves through uniform electric ($\mathbf{E}$) and magnetic ($\mathbf{B}=B\hat z$) fields. The electric field accelerates the charge and does work on it, exactly like gravity or a spring. The magnetic field only ever bends the path — it is always perpendicular to $\mathbf v$, so it does zero work at every instant. An optional resistive drag $-b\mathbf v$ (collisions with a background medium) dissipates energy as heat, exactly as friction does in the mechanical systems.

∂ Derivation — Power Balance from the Lorentz Force
Newton's 2nd law with the Lorentz force and drag
$$m\dot{\mathbf v}=q\mathbf E+q\mathbf v\times\mathbf B-b\mathbf v$$
Dot with $\mathbf v$ — extract the power balance
$$m\mathbf v\cdot\dot{\mathbf v}=q\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf v+q\,\mathbf v\cdot(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)-b v^2$$

The magnetic term vanishes identically: $\mathbf v\cdot(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)=0$ for any $\mathbf v,\mathbf B$ — a vector dotted with something perpendicular to it.

Identify kinetic energy rate
$$\frac{d}{dt}\!\underbrace{\Bigl(\tfrac12mv^2\Bigr)}_{KE}=q\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf v-\underbrace{bv^2}_{\dot Q\geq0}$$
Electric potential energy (uniform field, $\mathbf E=-\nabla\phi$, $\phi=-\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf r$)

Combine — total energy conservation
$$\frac{d}{dt}(KE+U)=-bv^2\leq0\;\Longrightarrow\;\frac{d}{dt}(KE+U+Q)=0\;\Longrightarrow\;E_{\rm total}=E_0=\text{const}$$

Undamped case ($b=0$): decompose into drift + gyration

Let $\mathbf v_d=(\mathbf E\times\mathbf B)/B^2$. One can check directly that $\mathbf E+\mathbf v_d\times\mathbf B=0$. Writing $\mathbf u=\mathbf v-\mathbf v_d$:

$$m\dot{\mathbf u}=q\mathbf E+q(\mathbf u+\mathbf v_d)\times\mathbf B=q\mathbf u\times\mathbf B\quad(\text{pure gyration at }\omega_c=qB/m)$$

The velocity is therefore an exact sum of a constant drift $\mathbf v_d$ and a circular gyration of frequency $\omega_c$ — the cycloid seen in the diagram above.

∫ Analytical Trajectories

Using the complex velocity $w=v_x+iv_y$ and $E_c=E_x+iE_y$, the full Lorentz-plus-drag equation of motion is a single linear ODE:

$$\dot w=\frac{qE_c}{m}-\lambda w,\qquad \lambda=\frac{b+iqB}{m}$$

which integrates exactly for any $b\geq0$ — no small-angle or perturbative approximation is needed anywhere in this system.

General Exact Solution

$$w(t)=w_{ss}+(w_0-w_{ss})\,e^{-\lambda t},\qquad w_{ss}=\frac{qE_c}{b+iqB}$$

$w_{ss}$ is the terminal (steady-state) velocity. Its real/imaginary parts give the closed-form terminal drift:

$$v_{x,ss}=\frac{q(bE_x+qBE_y)}{b^2+q^2B^2},\qquad v_{y,ss}=\frac{q(bE_y-qBE_x)}{b^2+q^2B^2}$$

$KE(t)=KE_0$ exactly — energy is trivially conserved since the magnetic force alone does no work at all.

🧲 Falling Magnet Through a Coil — Induction Braking

A magnet dropped through a fixed conducting coil induces an EMF as the flux through the coil changes — Faraday's law. The induced current then feels a Lorentz-force reaction from the magnet's own field, and by Lenz's law that reaction always opposes the motion. Unlike ordinary air drag, this braking coefficient is not constant: it depends sharply on how close the magnet is to the coil, vanishing far away and, surprisingly, exactly at the coil's centre plane.

$$E_{\rm total}=\underbrace{\tfrac12mv^2}_{KE}+\underbrace{mgz}_{PE}+\underbrace{Q}_{\text{Joule heat in }R}=E_0=mgz_0=\text{const}$$

🖼 Mechanical Model
∂ Derivation — Faraday, Ohm, and Energy Balance
Axial flux from a dipole magnet through a coil of radius $a$
$$\Phi(z)=\frac{\Phi_0\,a^3}{(a^2+z^2)^{3/2}}$$

$\Phi_0$ lumps the magnet's dipole strength and coil turns into one flux scale. $\Phi(z)$ is maximal and flat at $z=0$, falling off for $|z|\gg a$.

Faraday's law — induced EMF
$$\varepsilon=-\frac{d\Phi}{dt}=-\frac{d\Phi}{dz}\dot z=-\frac{d\Phi}{dz}\,v,\qquad \frac{d\Phi}{dz}=-\frac{3\Phi_0a^3z}{(a^2+z^2)^{5/2}}$$
Ohm's law (self-inductance neglected — resistance-dominated coil)
$$I=\frac{\varepsilon}{R}=-\frac{1}{R}\frac{d\Phi}{dz}\,v$$
Reaction force on the magnet
$$F_{\rm ind}=I\frac{d\Phi}{dz}=-\frac{1}{R}\!\left(\frac{d\Phi}{dz}\right)^{\!2}v=-k(z)\,v,\qquad k(z)=\frac{1}{R}\!\left(\frac{d\Phi}{dz}\right)^{\!2}\geq0$$

Squaring $d\Phi/dz$ guarantees $F_{\rm ind}$ always opposes $v$ — Lenz's law falls straight out of the algebra, it doesn't need to be imposed separately.

Newton's 2nd law, then multiply by $v$ — extract power
$$m\dot v=-mg-k(z)v\;\;\xrightarrow{\times v}\;\;mv\dot v=-mgv-k(z)v^2$$
Identify energy rates
$$\frac{d}{dt}\!\underbrace{\Bigl(\tfrac12mv^2\Bigr)}_{KE}+\frac{d}{dt}\!\underbrace{(mgz)}_{PE}=-\underbrace{k(z)v^2}_{\dot Q=I^2R\geq0}$$

The dissipated power $k(z)v^2$ is identically $I^2R$ — mechanical loss and Joule heating are the same quantity written two ways.

Total energy conservation — the fundamental result
$$\frac{d}{dt}(KE+PE+Q)=0\;\Longrightarrow\;E_{\rm total}=E_0=mgz_0=\text{const}$$

In practice: $Q(t)=E_0-KE(t)-PE(t)$, computed exactly from the RK4 trajectory, exactly as in every other tab.

🧭 Electric Dipole — Torque, Not Force

🍎 Falling Object — Gravitational Energy Conservation

An object dropped from height $H$ converts gravitational potential energy into kinetic energy. Air drag (friction) dissipates energy as heat. The total energy — kinetic + potential + work done against drag — remains exactly constant throughout the fall.

$$E_{\rm total} = \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2}_{KE} + \underbrace{mg(H-y)}_{PE} + \underbrace{W_{\rm drag}}_{\text{thermal}} = mgH = E_0 = \text{const}$$

where $y$ is the distance fallen (downward positive), so $H-y$ is the remaining height above ground.

∂ Energy Derivation — Power Balance
Newton's 2nd law (downward positive, $y$ = distance fallen)
$$m\dot v = mg - bv, \quad v = \dot y$$
Multiply both sides by $v$ — extract power equation
$$mv\dot v = mgv - bv^2$$
Identify kinetic energy rate and gravitational work rate

Note that $mv\dot v = \frac{d}{dt}(\frac{1}{2}mv^2) = \dot{KE}$ and $mgv = mg\dot y$.

Also $PE = mg(H-y)$, so $\dot{PE} = -mg\dot y = -mgv$.

$$\frac{d(KE)}{dt} = -\frac{d(PE)}{dt} - \underbrace{bv^2}_{\dot W_d \geq 0}$$
Rearrange — rate of total energy change
$$\frac{d}{dt}(KE + PE) = -bv^2 \leq 0$$

Mechanical energy $ME = KE + PE$ decreases at rate $bv^2$ — dissipated as heat by drag.

Define work done against drag
$$\dot W_d = bv^2 \geq 0 \implies W_d(t) = \int_0^t bv^2(\tau)\,d\tau = E_0 - KE(t) - PE(t)$$
Total energy conservation — the fundamental result
$$\frac{d}{dt}(KE + PE + W_d) = 0 \implies KE + PE + W_d = mgH = E_0 = \text{const}$$
At terminal velocity: power balance
$$v = v_T = \frac{mg}{b}: \quad \underbrace{mgv_T}_{\text{gravity power}} = \underbrace{bv_T^2}_{\text{drag power}} \implies \dot{KE} = 0$$
∫ Analytical Energy Expressions

Starting from rest ($v_0 = 0$) at height $H$, the exact solution is:

$$v(t) = v_T\!\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \quad y(t) = v_T\!\left[t - \tau(1-e^{-t/\tau})\right]$$

where $v_T = mg/b$ (terminal velocity) and $\tau = m/b$ (time constant).

🏀 Bouncing Ball — Energy Lost in Discrete Steps

A ball dropped from height $H$ converts gravitational PE into KE during free fall, exactly like the Falling Object system. The difference is how energy dissipates: instead of continuous drag, energy is lost only in an instantaneous burst at each contact with the ground, governed by the coefficient of restitution $e$. Between bounces, motion is perfectly conservative.

$$E_{\rm total}=\underbrace{\tfrac12mv^2}_{KE}+\underbrace{mgy}_{PE}+\underbrace{Q}_{\text{impact heat}}=E_0=mgH=\text{const}$$

⚠️ Event-driven, not continuous: Unlike $\dot Q=bv^2$ in the friction-based tabs, here $\dot Q=0$ during flight and $Q$ jumps by a finite amount only at the instant of contact — a delta-function in dissipation power, not a smooth rate.
💡 Finite time, infinite bounces: For $e<1$ the ball bounces infinitely many times, yet comes to complete rest in finite total time — a discrete-mechanics cousin of Zeno's paradox. See the Derivation tab for the closed form.

Optional: Air Friction (Quadratic Drag)

With air friction enabled, the ball also loses energy continuously during flight, not just at impact. Drag force opposes velocity and scales with $v^2$ (realistic for a ball at typical speeds, unlike the linear drag used in the Falling Object tab):

$$F_{\rm drag}=-k_d m\,v|v|,\qquad \dot v = -g - k_d v|v|$$

Now two dissipation channels act simultaneously: continuous drag heat $Q_{\rm air}$ during flight, and discrete impact heat $Q_{\rm impact}$ at each bounce.

$$E_{\rm total}=KE+PE+Q_{\rm air}+Q_{\rm impact}=E_0=mgH=\text{const}$$
$k_d$Drag coefficient (lumps $\tfrac12\rho C_dA/m$ into one constant)m⁻¹
⚠️ Effect on the bounce sequence: Drag removes energy before each impact, so every successive apex height is lower than the pure-restitution prediction $e^{2n}H$ — the closed-form geometric decay and finite-time formulas from the Derivation tab are exact only when $k_d=0$.
∂ Energy Derivation — Newton's Restitution Law
Newton's experimental law of impact

At the instant the ball touches the ground, the rebound speed is a fixed fraction of the approach speed:

$$v_{\rm after}=-e\,v_{\rm before},\qquad 0\le e\le1$$
Energy lost in a single collision
$$\Delta E=KE_{\rm before}-KE_{\rm after}=\tfrac12mv_{\rm before}^2-\tfrac12mv_{\rm after}^2=(1-e^2)\,KE_{\rm before}\ge0$$
Between bounces: pure free fall, no dissipation
$$\ddot y=-g\;\implies\;\frac{d}{dt}\Bigl(\tfrac12m\dot y^2+mgy\Bigr)=0$$

Mechanical energy is exactly constant in flight — it only steps down at the instant of contact.

Geometric decay of bounce height

Free flight returns the ball to the ground at the same speed it left with, so $v_{\rm impact,\,n+1}=v_{\rm after,\,n}$. Chaining the restitution law:

$$v_{\rm after,\,n}=e^{\,n}\sqrt{2gH},\qquad h_n=\frac{v_{\rm after,\,n}^2}{2g}=e^{\,2n}H$$
Time between successive bounces
$$T_n=\frac{2v_{\rm after,\,n}}{g}=2e^{\,n}\sqrt{\frac{2H}{g}}$$
Closed form: total time to rest (sum the geometric series)
$$t_\infty=\sqrt{\frac{2H}{g}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}T_n=\sqrt{\frac{2H}{g}}\Bigl(1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{\,n}\Bigr)=\sqrt{\frac{2H}{g}}\cdot\frac{1+e}{1-e}$$

Finite for any $e<1$, despite the infinite number of bounces — and $t_\infty\to\infty$ exactly as $e\to1$, the conservative limit.

With air friction: equation of motion during flight

Taking $y$ as height (positive up) and $v=\dot y$, drag always opposes the velocity:

$$m\dot v = -mg - k_d m\,v|v| \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \dot v = -g - k_d v|v|$$

This has no elementary closed-form solution for the full rise-and-fall trajectory (unlike the no-drag case), so $y(t),v(t)$ are obtained numerically via RK4 between each pair of bounces.

Power balance with drag — extract the dissipation rate
$$mv\dot v = -mgv - k_d m\,v^2|v|$$

Since $mv\dot v=\dot{KE}$ and $mgv=-\dot{PE}$ (as $PE=mgy$):

$$\frac{d}{dt}(KE+PE)=-\underbrace{k_d m|v|^3}_{\dot Q_{\rm air}\geq 0}$$
Total energy with both dissipation channels
$$Q_{\rm air}(t)=\int_0^t k_d m\,|v(\tau)|^3\,d\tau,\qquad Q_{\rm impact}=\sum_n (1-e^2)\,KE_{\rm before,\,n}$$
$$KE+PE+Q_{\rm air}+Q_{\rm impact}=E_0=mgH=\text{const}$$

$Q_{\rm air}$ grows continuously between bounces; $Q_{\rm impact}$ jumps in discrete steps at each contact — the same event/continuous split seen in the Theory tab, now combined.

∫ Analytical Trajectory & Bounce Sequence

Each flight segment between bounce $k$ and bounce $k+1$ is an exact parabola, with $(y_0,v_0)$ reset at every impact:

$$y(t)=y_0+v_0(t-t_0)-\tfrac12g(t-t_0)^2,\qquad v(t)=v_0-g(t-t_0)$$

🎳 Rolling Object — Rotational & Translational KE Split

When a rigid body rolls without slipping down an incline, the initial gravitational PE splits into two kinetic channels: translational KE of the centre of mass and rotational KE about the centre of mass. The fraction going to rotation is set entirely by the dimensionless moment-of-inertia parameter $\beta = I/(mR^2)$ — not by mass, radius, or incline angle. This is why a hollow cylinder always loses to a solid sphere in a race, no matter what.

$$E_{\rm total}=\underbrace{mgH}_{E_0}=\underbrace{\tfrac12mv^2}_{KE_{\rm trans}}+\underbrace{\tfrac12I\omega^2}_{KE_{\rm rot}}+\underbrace{mgH_{\rm rem}}_{PE}+\underbrace{W_{\rm fr}}_{\rm rolling\;friction}=E_0=\rm const$$

Rolling Constraint & KE Split

No-slip rolling demands $v = \omega R$, so $I\omega^2 = \beta mR^2(v/R)^2 = \beta mv^2$:

$$KE_{\rm total}=\tfrac12mv^2(1+\beta),\quad \frac{KE_{\rm rot}}{KE_{\rm total}}=\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}$$

Lower $\beta$ → more energy in translation → faster descent. Mass $m$ and radius $R$ cancel entirely — the race is determined only by shape.

$$a=\frac{g(\sin\theta-\mu_r\cos\theta)}{1+\beta},\qquad v_f=\sqrt{\frac{2gH}{1+\beta}}$$

∂ Derivation — Energy Method
Newton's 2nd law (translation + rotation)
$$ma = mg\sin\theta - f - \mu_r mg\cos\theta$$$$I\alpha = fR \quad\Rightarrow\quad f = \frac{I\alpha}{R}$$

$f$ is the static friction force (does no work in pure rolling). $\mu_r mg\cos\theta$ is rolling resistance (does work → heat). $I\alpha = I\dot\omega$.

Apply rolling constraint $v = \omega R \Rightarrow a = \alpha R$
$$ma = mg\sin\theta - \frac{I a}{R^2} - \mu_r mg\cos\theta$$$$\Rightarrow\quad a\!\left(m+\frac{I}{R^2}\right) = mg(\sin\theta - \mu_r\cos\theta)$$
Substitute $I = \beta mR^2$; $\beta$ absorbs shape information
$$a = \frac{g(\sin\theta-\mu_r\cos\theta)}{1+\beta}$$

This is constant (uniform acceleration down the slope). The smaller $\beta$, the larger $a$ — lighter rotational inertia means more acceleration.

Energy accounting — multiply EOM by $v = \dot s$
$$\frac{d}{dt}\!\underbrace{\Bigl(\tfrac12mv^2\Bigr)}_{KE_{\rm trans}}+\frac{d}{dt}\!\underbrace{\Bigl(\tfrac12I\omega^2\Bigr)}_{KE_{\rm rot}}+\frac{d}{dt}\!\underbrace{\Bigl(mg(H-s\sin\theta)\Bigr)}_{PE}=-\underbrace{\mu_r mg\cos\theta\cdot v}_{\dot W_{\rm fr}\geq 0}$$

Note: static friction $f$ does no work in pure rolling — it transfers energy between translational and rotational channels without dissipation.

Define rolling friction work and integrate
$$W_{\rm fr}(t)=\mu_r mg\cos\theta\cdot s(t)\geq 0,\qquad\frac{d}{dt}(KE_{\rm trans}+KE_{\rm rot}+PE+W_{\rm fr})=0$$$$\therefore\quad E_{\rm total}=E_0=mgH=\rm const$$
KE split — fixed by $\beta$ alone, independent of $m,R,\theta,H$
$$\frac{KE_{\rm rot}}{KE_{\rm trans}+KE_{\rm rot}}=\frac{\tfrac12\beta mv^2}{\tfrac12(1+\beta)mv^2}=\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}\quad\forall\,t$$

This ratio is constant throughout the motion — from the first instant until the bottom. The split is established immediately by the shape and never changes.

🎢 Roller Coaster — Track Energy Conservation & the Loop-the-Loop

A cart released from rest at height $H$ on a frictionless track converts gravitational PE into KE as it descends, and back into PE as it climbs — the total is exactly conserved regardless of the track's shape. This path independence is what makes energy methods so powerful: you never need to know the track's twists and turns, only the height at each point.

$$E_{\rm total}=\underbrace{\tfrac12mv^2}_{KE}+\underbrace{mgy}_{PE}+\underbrace{Q_{\rm fr}}_{\text{track friction}}=E_0=mgH=\text{const}$$

Loop-the-Loop Safety Condition

At the top of a vertical circular loop, gravity supplies the centripetal force. If the cart is too slow, it falls away from the track before reaching the top — the normal force $N$ would have to go negative, which the track can't provide.

$$N=\frac{mv_{\rm top}^2}{R}-mg\;\geq\;0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_{\rm top}^2\geq gR$$
ConditionOutcome
$H\geq 2.5R$Completes the loop, $N\geq0$ throughout

💡 Path independence: Because gravity is a conservative force, $v(x)=\sqrt{2g(H-y(x))}$ depends only on the current height $y(x)$ — a steep drop and a gentle drop to the same height give the cart the exact same speed there. Only friction (a non-conservative force) makes the result depend on the path actually taken.
⚠️ Why 2.5R, not 2R: Reaching the top with $v_{\rm top}=0$ only needs $H=2R$ — but at that speed the track can't supply any centripetal force at all, and the cart would already have fallen off well before the top. The extra $0.5R$ is exactly the energy needed to maintain $v_{\rm top}^2=gR$, the minimum non-zero speed circular motion requires at the apex.
🖼 Mechanical Model
∂ Derivation — Energy Method & the Loop Condition
Energy conservation between release and any track point (frictionless)
$$mgH=\tfrac12mv(x)^2+mgy(x)\;\Longrightarrow\;v(x)=\sqrt{2g\bigl(H-y(x)\bigr)}$$

No reference to the track's shape appears — only the height $y(x)$. This is the path-independence result.

Newton's 2nd law, radial direction, at the top of a loop of radius $R$
$$N+mg=\frac{mv_{\rm top}^2}{R}\quad\Rightarrow\quad N=\frac{mv_{\rm top}^2}{R}-mg$$

Both $N$ (track push) and $mg$ point toward the loop's centre at the top, so they add to supply the centripetal force.

Minimum speed: track can only push, never pull ($N\geq0$)
$$N\geq0\;\Longrightarrow\;v_{\rm top}^2\geq gR$$
Combine with energy conservation from release height $H$ to loop top (height $2R$)
$$mgH=\tfrac12mv_{\rm top}^2+mg(2R)\;\Longrightarrow\;v_{\rm top}^2=2g(H-2R)$$
Substitute the minimum-speed condition — solve for $H_{\min}$
$$2g(H-2R)\geq gR\;\Longrightarrow\;H\geq\frac{5}{2}R=2.5R$$

The classic result: a cart needs to start at least $2.5R$ above the track's base to safely complete a loop of radius $R$, independent of mass — mass cancels exactly as it does in the Rolling Object system.

With track friction: energy accounting along arc length $s$
$$\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(KE+PE\bigr)=-\underbrace{\mu\,mg\cos\phi(s)\,v}_{\dot Q_{\rm fr}\geq0}$$

where $\phi(s)$ is the track's local slope angle. Unlike the frictionless case, this integral does depend on the path taken — friction is non-conservative. (Numerical arc-length integration — Step 2.)
