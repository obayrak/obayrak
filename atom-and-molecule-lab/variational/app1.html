The Variational Principle

The variational method is one of the most powerful approximation techniques in quantum mechanics. Given any normalizable trial wavefunction \(\psi\), the expectation value of the Hamiltonian provides an upper bound to the true ground-state energy.

\[E[\psi] \;=\; \frac{\langle\psi|\hat{H}|\psi\rangle}{\langle\psi|\psi\rangle} \;=\; \frac{\displaystyle\int \psi^*(x)\,\hat{H}\,\psi(x)\,dx}{\displaystyle\int |\psi(x)|^2\,dx} \;\geq\; E_0\]

where \(E_0\) is the exact ground-state energy. Equality holds if and only if \(\psi = \psi_0\) (the exact eigenstate).

By parameterizing the trial function \(\psi(\mathbf{r};\alpha,\beta,\ldots)\) and minimizing \(E[\psi]\) over the variational parameters, we obtain the best approximation within the chosen family of functions.

Hamiltonian & Energy Decomposition

In natural units \(\hbar = m = 1\), the time-independent Schrödinger equation reads:

\[\hat{H}\psi = E\psi\qquad\text{where}\qquad \hat{H} = -\frac{1}{2}\nabla^2 + V(\mathbf{r})\]

The total energy decomposes into kinetic and potential contributions:

\[E[\psi] = \underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\frac{\displaystyle\int|\nabla\psi|^2\,dx}{\displaystyle\int|\psi|^2\,dx}}_{\langle T\rangle\;\text{(kinetic)}} + \underbrace{\frac{\displaystyle\int V(x)|\psi|^2\,dx}{\displaystyle\int|\psi|^2\,dx}}_{\langle V\rangle\;\text{(potential)}}\]

The kinetic energy integral is obtained via integration by parts: \(\displaystyle-\int\psi^*\psi''\,dx = \int|\psi'|^2\,dx\), and is evaluated numerically using 4th-order central finite differences.

Kinetic Energy

Computed using 4th-order central finite differences for \(\psi'(x)\), then Simpson's rule quadrature of \(|\psi'|^2\). This avoids numerical noise from double differentiation.

Potential Energy

Direct numerical quadrature of \(V(x)|\psi(x)|^2\) on the grid. For singular potentials (hydrogen \(V\sim -1/r\)), the grid starts at small positive \(r\) to avoid the singularity.

Radial Hydrogen Atom

For the 3D hydrogen atom with \(l=0\), we work with the reduced radial wavefunction \(u(r) = rR(r)\). The effective Schrödinger equation becomes 1D:

\[-\frac{1}{2}u''(r) - \frac{1}{r}u(r) = E\,u(r)\,,\qquad u(0)=0\,,\quad u\to 0\text{ as }r\to\infty\]

For the trial function \(\psi(r)=e^{-\alpha r}\), so \(u(r)=re^{-\alpha r}\), the variational energy is analytically:

\[E(\alpha) = \frac{\alpha^2}{2} - \alpha \quad\Longrightarrow\quad E_{\min} = -\frac{1}{2}\;\text{ at }\alpha=1\]

This exactly recovers the ground-state energy \(E_0 = -\frac{1}{2}\) a.u., since the hydrogenic exponential is the exact solution!

Numerical Methods

Nelder-Mead Simplex

A derivative-free optimization algorithm. Starting from a simplex of \(n+1\) points in \(n\)-dimensional parameter space, each iteration performs reflection, expansion, contraction, or shrinking to move the simplex toward the minimum. Converges reliably even for non-smooth objectives.

Gradient Descent

Uses numerical finite differences to estimate \(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} E[\psi]\) and iterates: \(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k+1} = \boldsymbol{\alpha}_k - \eta_k\nabla E\), with adaptive step-size via backtracking line search.

Golden Section Search

For single-parameter problems. Exploits unimodality to bracket the minimum, reducing the interval by the golden ratio \(\phi = (\sqrt{5}+1)/2 \approx 1.618\) at each step. Guaranteed convergence for unimodal functions.

Convergence Criterion

\[\text{Converged when: }\quad \max_i\left|E\bigl(\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(i)}\bigr) - E\bigl(\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(i-1)}\bigr)\right| < \varepsilon \quad\text{or}\quad \|\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(i+1)}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(i)}\| < \varepsilon\]

Example: Harmonic Oscillator

With \(V(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^2\) and trial \(\psi=e^{-\alpha x^2}\), using Gaussian integral identities \(\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-2\alpha x^2}dx = \sqrt{\pi/(2\alpha)}\):

\[E(\alpha) = \underbrace{\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{\langle T\rangle} + \underbrace{\frac{1}{8\alpha}}_{\langle V\rangle} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{dE}{d\alpha} = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{8\alpha^2} = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \alpha^* = \frac{1}{2}\]
\[E_{\min} = E\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{2} = E_0\quad\checkmark\]

The Gaussian trial is exact for the harmonic oscillator, so the variational method achieves the true ground-state energy. For other potentials (anharmonic, double well, etc.), the best variational energy lies strictly above \(E_0\), demonstrating the variational bound.

Orthogonal Basis Expansion & Secular Equation

A more systematic approach expands the trial wavefunction in a set of \(N\) orthonormal basis functions \(\{\varphi_i\}\):

\[\psi = \sum_{i=1}^N c_i\,\varphi_i(x)\,,\qquad \langle\varphi_i|\varphi_j\rangle = \delta_{ij}\]

Substituting into the variational energy and minimizing over coefficients \(\{c_i\}\) yields the generalized eigenvalue problem (secular equation):

\[\mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{c} = E\,\mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{c}\]

where \(H_{ij} = \langle\varphi_i|\hat{H}|\varphi_j\rangle\) is the Hamiltonian matrix and \(S_{ij} = \langle\varphi_i|\varphi_j\rangle\) is the overlap matrix. For an orthonormal basis, \(\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{I}\) and this reduces to a standard eigenvalue problem.

The N eigenvalues \(E_1 \le E_2 \le \cdots \le E_N\) provide upper bounds to the true energies \(\varepsilon_1 \le \varepsilon_2 \le \cdots \le \varepsilon_N\) by the Hylleraas-Undheim theorem: \(E_k \ge \varepsilon_k\). The lowest eigenvalue is the variational estimate of the ground state.

Basis Sets Available

HO Eigenstates

\(\varphi_n(x) = H_n(\sqrt{\omega}x)\,e^{-\omega x^2/2}\,/\sqrt{2^n n!\sqrt{\pi/\omega}}\) — the exact eigenfunctions of the harmonic oscillator. Excellent for states near a quadratic minimum.

Fourier Sine Series

\(\varphi_n(x) = \sqrt{2/L}\,\sin(n\pi x/L)\) — the ISW eigenfunctions, complete on \([0,L]\). Efficient for localized potentials on a finite domain.

Hermite-Gaussian

\(\varphi_n(x) = H_n(\alpha x)\,e^{-\alpha^2 x^2/2}\) — unnormalized Hermite-Gaussian functions with tunable width \(\alpha\). Flexible basis for anharmonic systems.

Legendre-Gaussian

\(\varphi_n(x) = P_n(x/L)\,e^{-\omega x^2/2}\) — Legendre polynomials modulated by a Gaussian envelope. Useful for capturing polynomial corrections near the origin.

Numerical Procedure

The matrix elements \(H_{ij}\) are computed numerically on the grid using Simpson's rule: the kinetic part via \(\frac{1}{2}\int\varphi_i'\,\varphi_j'\,dx\) (integration by parts) and the potential part via \(\int V(x)\,\varphi_i\varphi_j\,dx\). For a non-orthogonal basis, the overlap matrix \(S_{ij} = \int\varphi_i\varphi_j\,dx\) is also computed and the generalized eigenproblem solved via Cholesky factorization.
