Level 1 · Rigid rotor + harmonic oscillator

The simplest diatomic model treats the bond as a rigid stick for rotation and as an ideal spring for vibration. Every number below comes from isotopic masses and NIST constants, and the table checks it against NIST data.

Theory, assumptions & limits

Rigid rotor

Two masses at fixed separation \(r_e\) have reduced mass \(\mu = \dfrac{m_1 m_2}{m_1+m_2}\) and moment of inertia \(I=\mu r_e^2\). The levels are

\[E_J = B\,J(J+1),\qquad B=\frac{h}{8\pi^2 c\,\mu r_e^2}\ \ [\mathrm{cm^{-1}}]\]

Pure-rotation absorption lines (\(\Delta J=+1\)) lie at \(\tilde\nu = 2B(J+1)\). Thermal populations are \(P_J \propto g_{ns}(2J+1)e^{-BJ(J+1)hc/k_BT}\), peaking at \(J_{\max}\approx\sqrt{k_BT/2hcB}-\tfrac12\).

Harmonic oscillator

\[G(v) = \omega_e\,(v+\tfrac12),\qquad k = \mu\,(2\pi c\,\omega_e)^2\]

Selection rule \(\Delta v=\pm1\), so every transition falls at \(\omega_e\). The zero-point energy is \(\omega_e/2\).

Homonuclear nuclear-spin statistics

For identical nuclei, the weight \(g_{ns}\) alternates between even and odd \(J\): 1:3 for H₂ (ortho/para), 6:3 for ¹⁴N₂, 15:21 for ¹²⁷I₂. In ¹⁶O₂ (X³Σg⁻), only odd rotational levels exist. Homonuclear molecules have no pure-rotation absorption spectrum and are seen in Raman.

Where it breaks down

  • Real bonds stretch when the molecule rotates (centrifugal distortion), and the vibrationally averaged \(B_v=B_e-\alpha_e(v+\tfrac12)\) is smaller than \(B_e\).
  • Real potentials are anharmonic, so the levels get closer together as \(v\) rises and the molecule can dissociate (Level 2).
  • Open-shell molecules (NO X²Π, O₂ X³Σg⁻) have spin–orbit and spin–rotation structure that is ignored here.

Level 2 · Anharmonicity & the Morse potential

A real bond softens as it stretches and eventually breaks. The Morse potential captures this with one extra parameter. Here it is built from the NIST constants ωₑ and ωₑxₑ, used to predict the dissociation energy, and checked against an independent numerical Schrödinger solution.

Theory, assumptions & limits

Morse potential

\[V(r)=D_e\left(1-e^{-a(r-r_e)}\right)^2,\qquad a=\omega_e\sqrt{\frac{2\pi^2 c\,\mu}{D_e h}}\]

Its Schrödinger equation is exactly solvable:

\[G(v)=\omega_e(v+\tfrac12)-\omega_e x_e(v+\tfrac12)^2,\qquad \omega_e x_e=\frac{\omega_e^2}{4D_e}\]

so \(D_e=\omega_e^2/4\omega_e x_e\) and \(D_0=D_e-G(0)\). The number of bound levels is \(v_{\max}=\lfloor \omega_e/2\omega_e x_e-\tfrac12\rfloor\).

Birge–Sponer extrapolation

Spacings \(\Delta G_{v+1/2}=\omega_e-2\omega_e x_e(v+1)\) fall linearly with \(v\). The area under the \(\Delta G\) line out to zero gives \(D_0\). Real molecules curve below the line near dissociation, so linear Birge–Sponer, like Morse, usually overestimates \(D_0\).

Reverse prediction

Given the measured \(D_0\) and \(\omega_e\), solving \(D_e^2-(D_0+\omega_e/2)D_e+\omega_e^2/16=0\) gives a Morse \(D_e\) and hence a predicted \(\omega_e x_e\), compared with NIST.

Numerical check

The radial equation \(-\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}\psi''+V\psi=E\psi\) is discretised by second-order finite differences on 8000 points. The lowest eigenvalues of the tridiagonal matrix are found by Sturm-sequence bisection and compared with the exact Morse levels.

RKR: the potential from experiment

The Rydberg–Klein–Rees method inverts measured \(G(v)\) and \(B_v\) into turning points without assuming a shape:

\[f=\sqrt{\tfrac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}}\int_{-1/2}^{v}\frac{dv'}{\sqrt{G(v)-G(v')}},\quad g=\sqrt{\tfrac{2\mu}{\hbar^2}}\int_{-1/2}^{v}\frac{B_{v'}\,dv'}{\sqrt{G(v)-G(v')}},\quad r_\pm=\sqrt{f^2+f/g}\pm f\]

The singular integrals are evaluated with the substitution \(v'=v-(v+\tfrac12)t^2\) and 64-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature. The exact quantum levels of an RKR curve lie above G(v) by the small Dunham correction \(Y_{00}\approx B_e/4+\alpha_e\omega_e/12B_e+(\alpha_e\omega_e)^2/144B_e^3-\omega_e x_e/4\), which is included in the round-trip check. They use every higher-order Dunham constant in the WebBook (ωₑyₑ, ωₑzₑ, γₑ, …). The curve is valid only as far as those constants are (v ≤ 37 for CO).

Where it breaks down

  • One parameter cannot set both the curvature near \(r_e\) and the long-range tail (\(-C_6/r^6\), \(-C_5/r^5\)). Morse therefore misses \(D_0\) by a few percent for light hydrides and by tens of percent for heavy, weakly bound molecules like I₂.
  • Higher-order terms (\(\omega_e y_e\), …) and rotation–vibration coupling are ignored (Level 4).

Level 3 · Numerical Schrödinger solver

Solve the 1D radial equation for any potential and look at the wavefunctions. Three potentials built from different NIST inputs are compared on two measured quantities, the fundamental and B₀. The convergence plot shows the solver's second-order accuracy.

Theory, assumptions & limits

Discretisation

\[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}\frac{\psi_{i+1}-2\psi_i+\psi_{i-1}}{h^2}+V_i\psi_i=E\psi_i\]

This gives a symmetric tridiagonal matrix. Eigenvalues come from Sturm-sequence bisection, which counts sign changes to locate each level. Eigenvectors come from inverse iteration (Thomas algorithm). The global error scales as \(O(h^2)\).

Potentials

  • Harmonic: \(\tfrac12 k(r-r_e)^2\), from NIST \(\omega_e\).
  • Morse: from NIST \(\omega_e,\ \omega_e x_e\).
  • Lennard-Jones: \(D_e[(r_e/r)^{12}-2(r_e/r)^6]\) with the well depth \(D_e = D_0 + G(0)\) set from measured values. It is a van der Waals form, so its curvature is wrong for a covalent bond.
  • Custom: any JavaScript expression in r, De, re, a, we.

Rotational constant from the wavefunction

\[B_0=\frac{h}{8\pi^2c\mu}\,\langle\psi_0|r^{-2}|\psi_0\rangle\]

This is compared with NIST \(B_0=B_e-\alpha_e/2\). It tests the shape of the potential, beyond its curvature.

Limits

  • Uniform grid with Dirichlet walls: high levels near dissociation feel the box edge.
  • Rotationless (J = 0). Centrifugal terms enter in Level 4.

Level 4 · Rovibrational spectroscopy

Combine vibration and rotation with centrifugal distortion and rotation–vibration coupling. The simulated spectrum uses Boltzmann populations, Hönl–London or Placzek–Teller factors and nuclear-spin statistics at the toolbar temperature.

Theory, assumptions & limits

Term values (Dunham-style, low order)

\[E(v,J)=\omega_e(v+\tfrac12)-\omega_e x_e(v+\tfrac12)^2+B_vJ(J+1)-D_eJ^2(J+1)^2\]

\[B_v=B_e-\alpha_e(v+\tfrac12)\]

Raman (Stokes, Δv = +1)

Q (\(\Delta J=0\)), O (\(-2\)) and S (\(+2\)) branches with Placzek–Teller factors \(b_{J,J'}\). Homonuclear molecules have no IR spectrum, and their Raman lines show nuclear-spin intensity alternation.

Isotopes

With \(\rho=\sqrt{\mu/\mu'}\): \(\omega_e'=\rho\omega_e\), \(\omega_e x_e'=\rho^2\omega_e x_e\), \(B_e'=\rho^2B_e\), \(\alpha_e'=\rho^3\alpha_e\), \(D_e'=\rho^4D_e\).

From lines back to constants

For each measured line, \(\tilde\nu=\nu_0+B_1x'-B_0x-D_1x'^2+D_0x^2+H(x'^3-x^3)\) with \(x=J(J+1)\), which is linear in the parameters. A QR least-squares fit to the 69 NIST CO lines gives \(B_0, B_1\), hence \(B_e=(3B_0-B_1)/2\), \(\alpha_e\) and \(r_e\). These are then compared with the WebBook table.

Limits

  • Higher Dunham terms (\(\omega_e y_e\), \(\gamma_e\)) are omitted. The band origin is off by \(\sim\tfrac{13}{4}\omega_e y_e\), so compare with the literature band origins.
  • NO (²Π) has a Q branch and Λ-doubling, which are ignored. O₂ is treated without spin–rotation splitting.

Level 5 · Electronic structure of diatomics

H₂⁺ LCAO · H₂ Hartree–Fock/STO-3G · MO theory · Franck–Condon

Want more? The quantum chemistry track extends this to any molecule from H to F, with excited states and 3D orbitals. So far the potential came from spectroscopy. Here it is computed from electrons and nuclei. The simplest molecule, H₂⁺, is solved by LCAO. H₂ gets a real ab initio calculation with contracted Gaussians, Boys-function integrals and Hartree–Fock, plus full CI. Qualitative MO theory explains bond orders and magnetism.

Theory, assumptions & limits

H₂⁺ LCAO

\(\psi_\pm=(1s_A\pm1s_B)/\sqrt{2(1\pm S)}\) with Slater orbitals \(\propto e^{-\zeta r}\). In atomic units, using \(w=\zeta R\):

\[E_\pm=\frac{\zeta^2(T_{aa}\pm T_{ab})+\zeta(V_{aa}\pm V_{ab})}{1\pm S}+\frac1R\]

\(S=e^{-w}(1+w+w^2/3)\), \(T_{aa}=\tfrac12\), \(T_{ab}=\tfrac12e^{-w}(1+w-w^2/3)\), \(V_{aa}=-1-\tfrac1w[1-(1+w)e^{-2w}]\), \(V_{ab}=-2(1+w)e^{-w}\). Optimising \(\zeta\) at each \(R\) (Finkelstein–Horowitz) lowers the energy toward the exact solution.

H₂ ab initio

STO-3G basis (\(\zeta=1.24\)): each 1s is a sum of three Gaussians. Overlap, kinetic, nuclear-attraction and two-electron integrals are evaluated analytically with the Boys function \(F_0\). In this minimal basis, symmetry fixes the RHF orbital \(\sigma_g\), so SCF converges in one step: \(E_{RHF}=2h_{gg}+J_{gg}+1/R\). Full CI mixes in \(\sigma_u^2\) and repairs the dissociation limit.

Second-row MO diagram

Bond order \(=(n_b-n_{ab})/2\). s–p mixing puts \(\pi_{2p}\) below \(\sigma_{2p}\) for Li₂–N₂. The order flips for O₂–Ne₂. Hund's rule in the degenerate \(\pi^*\) makes O₂ paramagnetic.

Franck–Condon factors

\(q_{v'0}=|\langle\psi_{v'}|\psi_0\rangle|^2\), computed from numerical Morse eigenfunctions of two displaced states. \(\sum_{v'}q_{v'0}=1\) checks completeness.

Limits

  • A minimal basis overbinds H₂ (basis-set imbalance) and RHF dissociates to H⁺ + H⁻ mixtures.
  • The MO diagram is qualitative. C₂ and B₂ ground states really need multireference treatment.

Level 6 · Polyatomic molecules

Normal modes (Cartesian Hessian) · rotational constants · Hückel π theory

With more than two atoms, vibration becomes a set of coupled oscillators. A valence force field gives the potential, the mass-weighted Hessian gives normal modes, and the inertia tensor gives the rotational constants. All are compared with NIST CCCBDB experimental data.

Theory, assumptions & limits

Valence force field

\[V=\tfrac12\sum f_r\Delta r_i^2+\sum_{i

\(f\) in mdyn/Å and mdyn·Å/rad². The Cartesian Hessian comes from central finite differences of the exact internal coordinates (angles via atan2, stable at 180°).

Normal modes

Diagonalise \(\mathbf F=\mathbf M^{-1/2}\mathbf H\mathbf M^{-1/2}\) (Jacobi rotations). Translations and rotations give near-zero eigenvalues and are removed (\(3N-6\), or \(3N-5\) if linear). \(\tilde\nu=1302.8\sqrt{\lambda}\) cm⁻¹ for \(\lambda\) in mdyn Å⁻¹ u⁻¹.

Rotation

Principal moments from the inertia tensor give \(A\ge B\ge C\). The molecule is linear, a symmetric top (prolate or oblate) or an asymmetric top (Ray's \(\kappa\)).

Hückel

\(H_{ii}=\alpha\), \(H_{ij}=\beta\) for bonded p-orbitals, so \(E_k=\alpha+x_k\beta\) where \(x_k\) are eigenvalues of the adjacency matrix. One β is fitted to polyene UV absorption to test the model.

Limits

  • Computed frequencies are harmonic. Experimental fundamentals include anharmonicity, typically 2–5% lower for X–H stretches, and Fermi resonance (CO₂ ν₁).
  • Default force constants are approximate textbook-style values. Edit them to explore.
  • Hückel ignores electron repulsion and bond alternation, so its polyene gaps shrink too fast.

Level 7 · Many-body systems

Lennard-Jones molecular dynamics · statistical thermodynamics

The last level adds many molecules and temperature. Argon atoms interact through a Lennard-Jones potential and are integrated with velocity-Verlet in a Web Worker. Separately, the spectroscopic constants from Levels 1–4 feed partition functions that predict heat capacities and entropies, compared with NIST thermochemistry.

Theory, assumptions & limits

Molecular dynamics

\(u(r)=4\varepsilon[(\sigma/r)^{12}-(\sigma/r)^6]\), truncated and shifted at \(2.5\sigma\), periodic box, minimum image. Velocity-Verlet with \(\Delta t\) in reduced units \(\tau=\sigma\sqrt{m/\varepsilon}\). Thermostats are Berendsen (rescaling) or Langevin (exact Ornstein–Uhlenbeck velocity update). Argon parameters are σ = 3.405 Å and ε/k = 119.8 K.

Observables: \(T=2K/3(N-1)k\), virial pressure with tail correction, \(g(r)\) from pair histograms, and \(D=\lim \langle|\Delta \mathbf r|^2\rangle/6t\) (Einstein relation).

Statistical thermodynamics (ideal gas, 1 bar)

\[q=q_{tr}\,q_{el}\sum_{v,J}\frac{g_{ns}(J)}{(2I+1)^2}(2J+1)e^{-E(v,J)/kT}\]

The rovibrational sum is explicit, with no high-T approximation. It includes anharmonicity, \(\alpha_e\), centrifugal distortion and ortho/para weights. \(U=RT^2\partial\ln q/\partial T\), \(C_p=\partial H/\partial T\), \(S=R\ln(q/N)+U/T+R\). Compared with NIST Shomate fits.

Second virial coefficient

\(B_2(T)=-2\pi N_A\int_0^\infty\left(e^{-u(r)/kT}-1\right)r^2dr\) tests the pair potential directly. The NIST value comes from \(Z-1=B_2\rho\) at 0.1 MPa, using the Tegeler–Span–Wagner reference equation of state (valid to 700 K).

Limits

  • MD is classical. Small N gives finite-size effects in D. Run long enough for the MSD to become linear.
  • The thermo sum uses natural constants of the main isotopologue, so isotope mixing entropy is ignored (≪ 0.1 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹). Excited electronic states are included only for O₂ and NO.

QC 1 · Hartree–Fock self-consistent field

General RHF, STO-3G, McMurchie–Davidson integrals, DIIS

A real ab initio program running in your browser. It evaluates Gaussian integrals (overlap, kinetic, nuclear attraction, electron repulsion, dipole) for s and p functions, iterates the Roothaan equations with DIIS acceleration, and reports geometry, dipole moment and Koopmans ionisation energy against NIST CCCBDB experiment.

Theory, assumptions & limits

Roothaan–Hall equations

\[\mathbf{FC}=\mathbf{SC}\boldsymbol\varepsilon,\qquad F_{\mu\nu}=H_{\mu\nu}+\sum_{\lambda\sigma}P_{\lambda\sigma}\left[(\mu\nu|\lambda\sigma)-\tfrac12(\mu\lambda|\nu\sigma)\right]\]

Orthogonalisation uses canonical \(\mathbf X=\mathbf U\mathbf s^{-1/2}\). The initial guess is the core Hamiltonian. Convergence requires \(|\Delta E|<10^{-11}\) Eh and \(\mathrm{rms}(\Delta P)<10^{-9}\).

Integrals (McMurchie–Davidson)

Gaussian products are expanded in Hermite Gaussians with coefficients \(E^{ij}_t\). Coulomb-type integrals reduce to \(R_{tuv}\), which are built from Boys functions \(F_n(T)\). Those come from a series with downward recursion, or the asymptotic form with upward recursion for \(T>33\).

DIIS

Pulay's direct inversion in the iterative subspace extrapolates \(\mathbf F=\sum c_k\mathbf F_k\) to minimise \(\|\sum c_k\mathbf e_k\|\) with \(\mathbf e=\mathbf{FPS}-\mathbf{SPF}\) and \(\sum c_k=1\).

Properties

Dipole \(\boldsymbol\mu=\sum_A Z_A\mathbf R_A-\sum P_{\mu\nu}\langle\mu|\mathbf r|\nu\rangle\). Mulliken charges \(q_A=Z_A-\sum_{\mu\in A}(\mathbf{PS})_{\mu\mu}\). Koopmans: \(IE\approx-\varepsilon_{HOMO}\).

Limits

  • STO-3G is a minimal basis: angles too small, dipoles off by 10–40%. 6-31G adds split valence, and 6-31G* adds d polarisation on heavy atoms, which fixes most of the H₂O angle. MP2 (frozen core, canonical, \(E^{(2)}=\sum\frac{(ia|jb)[2(ia|jb)-(ib|ja)]}{\varepsilon_i+\varepsilon_j-\varepsilon_a-\varepsilon_b}\)) adds the leading electron correlation.
  • RHF has no electron correlation, so dissociation is wrong for every bond.
  • Koopmans IEs neglect orbital relaxation and correlation, which partly cancel.
One atom per line, "El x y z" in Å (H–F, STO-3G). Even electron count required (RHF).

QC 2 · Excited states & potential curves

CIS · full CI (2e) · Franck–Condon I₂ B←X

Electronic excitations from configuration interaction, potential curves showing where states cross or dissociate wrongly, and a complete visible absorption band of iodine built from NIST constants.

Theory, assumptions & limits

CIS

Single excitations from the RHF determinant. Singlets: \(A_{ia,jb}=\delta_{ij}\delta_{ab}(\varepsilon_a-\varepsilon_i)+2(ia|jb)-(ij|ab)\). Triplets drop the exchange-like \(2(ia|jb)\). Oscillator strengths: \(f=\tfrac23\Delta E\,|\sqrt2\sum c_{ia}\langle i|\boldsymbol\mu|a\rangle|^2\).

Full CI for two electrons

For H₂ and HeH⁺ in a minimal basis, the three singlet configurations \(\sigma_g^2,\ \sigma_u^2,\ \sigma_g\sigma_u\) and the triplet \(\sigma_g\sigma_u\) are diagonalised exactly. This repairs RHF dissociation and gives the X, EF, B and b states.

I₂ B←X

Both states are Morse oscillators from NIST \(T_e,\omega_e,\omega_ex_e,r_e\). Vibrational wavefunctions come from the numerical solver, Franck–Condon factors from their overlap, and v″ = 0–3 are Boltzmann-weighted at the toolbar temperature with 14 cm⁻¹ broadening for unresolved rotation.

Limits

  • CIS overestimates valence excitation energies by roughly 1–3 eV, and STO-3G has no diffuse functions, so Rydberg states are missing.
  • RHF and CIS curves dissociate incorrectly for covalent bonds.

QC 3 · Vibrations & IR spectra from first principles

Parallel finite-difference Hessian · dipole derivatives · scale factors

No force constants are fitted here. The geometry is optimised, then every displaced geometry for the full Cartesian Hessian (1 + 6N + 18N² − 6N single points) runs as an independent job on the worker pool. Diagonalising the mass-weighted Hessian gives harmonic frequencies, and dipole derivatives give IR intensities. Results are compared with NIST experimental fundamentals.

Theory, assumptions & limits

\[H_{ij}=\frac{E(+i,+j)-E(+i,-j)-E(-i,+j)+E(-i,-j)}{4h^2},\qquad H_{ii}=\frac{E(+i)-2E_0+E(-i)}{h^2},\quad h=0.005\,a_0\]

The eigenvalues of \(M^{-1/2}HM^{-1/2}\) give \(\omega_k\). Six (or five, if linear) near-zero modes are rigid translations and rotations. IR intensity: \(I_k=42.256\,|\partial\boldsymbol\mu/\partial Q_k|^2\) km/mol, with μ in D, Q in Å·u^½.

Harmonic frequencies exceed the measured anharmonic fundamentals. One least-squares scale factor per molecule is reported, alongside the standard literature factors (HF/6-31G* 0.8953, MP2/6-31G* 0.9427).

Cost

  • H₂O needs 163 single points, NH₃ 289, CH₄ 451. 6-31G* with MP2 takes minutes on one thread but scales with the worker count. Results are cached.

Level 8 · Many-body: phases, transport & quantum gases

Parallel MD on the worker pool · Green–Kubo · Maxwell construction · Wigner–Kirkwood

Ensembles of independent simulations run in parallel on as many worker threads as you choose in the toolbar. Every result is checked against NIST fluid data: the argon coexistence curve, liquid viscosity and thermal conductivity, the 200 K supercritical isotherm, polyatomic heat capacities, and quantum effects in neon's second virial coefficient.

Theory, assumptions & limits

Parallel ensembles

Each state point or replica is an independent NVT-equilibrated LJ simulation (N = 108–500, truncated and shifted at 2.5σ, virial pressure with tail correction). The pool distributes jobs across worker threads, so the wall time scales roughly as jobs ÷ threads.

Potentials & cutoffs

Lennard-Jones, or Tang–Toennies with ab initio dispersion (C₆, C₈, C₁₀ and recursion), damping, and A, b fixed by the Aziz well (99.553 cm⁻¹ at 3.757 Å). It runs through a tabulated-potential mode in the WebAssembly kernel. Cutoffs of 2.5–4.5σ with numerical tail corrections for energy and pressure.

Gibbs-ensemble MC

Two periodic boxes at fixed total N, V and T exchange volume and particles, so coexisting liquid and vapour are sampled directly with no interface. The acceptance rules are Panagiotopoulos's.

Maxwell construction

Below T_c, a small periodic box shows a van der Waals-like loop in P(ρ). A quintic is fitted, and the tie line is chosen so that \(\int_{v_l}^{v_g}(P-P_\sigma)\,dv=0\) (equal chemical potentials).

Green–Kubo transport

\[\eta=\frac{V}{k_BT}\int_0^\infty\langle P_{xy}(0)P_{xy}(t)\rangle dt,\qquad \lambda=\frac{1}{3Vk_BT^2}\int_0^\infty\langle\mathbf J(0)\cdot\mathbf J(t)\rangle dt\]

NVE production after NVT equilibration, averaged over xy, xz, yz and replicas. The plateau is read over 1.5–3 ps.

Polyatomic ideal gases

Rigid rotor (CCCBDB A, B, C and symmetry number σ) plus harmonic oscillators, using either the NIST experimental fundamentals or the Level 6 force-field modes.

Quantum corrections

\[\Delta B_2=\frac{N_Ah^2}{24\pi m (kT)^3}\int_0^\infty e^{-u/kT}\left(\frac{du}{dr}\right)^2r^2dr\]

This matters when the de Boer parameter \(\Lambda^*=h/\sigma\sqrt{m\varepsilon}\) is large (Ne ≈ 0.59, Ar ≈ 0.19).

Limits

  • A truncated LJ potential lowers T_c relative to real argon, so coexistence densities will differ. That difference is the honest result.
  • Pair vs effective potentials: the accurate Tang–Toennies pair potential reproduces gas-phase B₂(T) to ~1%, but it makes the liquid ~10% too dense. Real argon liquid also feels the repulsive three-body Axilrod–Teller dispersion. Lennard-Jones, fitted to condensed-phase data, absorbs that effect, which is why it looks better for liquids and worse for gases.
  • Gibbs-ensemble vapour densities are noisy at low T, where few swaps are accepted. Use more cycles or particles.
  • Green–Kubo integrals are noisy. Use more replicas (and threads) for tighter error bars.
  • Harmonic vibrations underestimate Cp at high T. Fermi resonance and anharmonicity are ignored.

Reading the results

Every results table compares a computed value with a reference. The reference is usually NIST data (Chemistry WebBook, CCCBDB, fluid tables, the Triatomic Spectral Database). Otherwise it is an exact theoretical result or a literature benchmark, and the table says which. The signed percentage error is

\[ \delta = 100\,\frac{x_\text{calc}-x_\text{ref}}{|x_\text{ref}|}\,\% ,\qquad \text{✓ } |\delta|\lt 1\%,\quad \text{! } 1\%\le|\delta|\le 5\%,\quad \text{✕ } |\delta|\gt 5\% .\]
  • ⚠ on a reference means it was not taken from a fetched NIST table, for example a literature value entered from memory. Hover to see why. Treat these as provisional.
  • “n/a” means there is no meaningful reference, for example Mulliken charges, which depend on the basis set.
  • Error bars (±) on stochastic results are one standard error from block averaging (Numerics).
  • A colour grade judges agreement, not the method. A large error is often the expected physics: Koopmans ionisation energies, harmonic frequencies and an unscaled minimal basis are all supposed to miss.
In this app · Stochastic runs use seeded mulberry32 generators, so the same seed reproduces the same numbers. Expensive jobs are cached in IndexedDB under a deep, key-sorted hash of all inputs, including geometries. The Error dashboard collects every table; the Model report summarises them.

Units, constants and conventions

The SI defining constants are exact (CODATA 2018): \(h = 6.62607015\times10^{-34}\) J s, \(c = 299\,792\,458\) m s⁻¹, \(k_B = 1.380649\times10^{-23}\) J K⁻¹, \(N_A = 6.02214076\times10^{23}\) mol⁻¹, \(e = 1.602176634\times10^{-19}\) C. Derived conversions:

\[ 1\ \text{eV} = \frac{e}{hc}\approx 8065.544\ \text{cm}^{-1},\qquad \frac{k_B}{hc}\approx 0.695035\ \text{cm}^{-1}\,\text{K}^{-1},\qquad 1\,E_h = 27.211386\ \text{eV},\qquad a_0 = 0.529177\ \text{Å}. \]

Spectroscopy works in wavenumbers (cm⁻¹), and term values \(G(v)\) and \(F(J)\) are energies divided by \(hc\). Quantum chemistry uses atomic units (\(\hbar=m_e=e=4\pi\varepsilon_0=1\)): energies in hartree, lengths in bohr. Simulations of argon use Lennard-Jones reduced units: length \(\sigma = 3.405\) Å, energy \(\varepsilon/k_B = 119.8\) K, mass \(m = 39.948\) u, time \(\tau = \sigma\sqrt{m/\varepsilon}\approx 2.16\) ps, density \(\rho^* = \rho\sigma^3\), temperature \(T^* = k_BT/\varepsilon\). The Ising model uses \(J = k_B = 1\).

Reduced masses use isotopic atomic masses (AME): \(\mu = m_1m_2/(m_1+m_2)\). That is why isotopologue predictions (H³⁵Cl vs H³⁷Cl, D₂) follow from one set of Born–Oppenheimer constants.

1 · Rigid rotor and harmonic oscillator

Rotation

Two point masses at fixed separation \(r_e\) have moment of inertia \(I = \mu r_e^2\). The Schrödinger equation on the sphere gives the spherical harmonics, with energies

\[ F(J) = B\,J(J+1),\qquad B = \frac{h}{8\pi^2 c\,\mu r_e^2}\ (\text{cm}^{-1}),\qquad g_J = 2J+1 . \]

A permanent dipole gives electric-dipole selection rules \(\Delta J=\pm1\), so pure-rotation lines fall at \(\tilde\nu = 2B(J+1)\), evenly spaced by \(2B\). Thermal populations follow Boltzmann with degeneracy,

\[ \frac{N_J}{N} = \frac{(2J+1)\,e^{-hcBJ(J+1)/k_BT}}{q_\text{rot}},\qquad q_\text{rot}\approx\frac{k_BT}{\sigma hcB}\ (T\gg hcB/k_B),\qquad J_\text{max}\approx\sqrt{\frac{k_BT}{2hcB}}-\tfrac12 , \]

where \(\sigma\) is the symmetry number (2 for homonuclear molecules). For homonuclear molecules, nuclear-spin statistics weight odd and even \(J\) differently: odd:even 3:1 for H₂, 1:2 for D₂ and ¹⁴N₂.

Vibration

Expanding the potential to second order about \(r_e\), \(V\approx\tfrac12 k(r-r_e)^2\), gives

\[ G(v) = \omega_e\,(v+\tfrac12),\qquad \omega_e = \frac{1}{2\pi c}\sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu}},\qquad \psi_v \propto H_v(\sqrt{\alpha}x)\,e^{-\alpha x^2/2},\ \ \alpha = \mu\omega/\hbar . \]

The dipole selection rule is \(\Delta v=\pm1\), so every transition falls at \(\omega_e\). The zero-point energy \(\omega_e/2\) is why \(D_0 = D_e - \text{ZPE}\).

In this app · \(B_e\), \(\omega_e\) and \(r_e\) come from the NIST WebBook diatomic tables. The table recomputes \(B_e\) from \(r_e\) and isotopic masses, which checks that mass, geometry and B agree.
Limits · The rigid rotor misses centrifugal stretching (\(D J^2(J+1)^2\)) and the vibration dependence of \(B\) (\(\alpha_e\)). The harmonic oscillator misses anharmonicity, so its fundamental is too high by \(2\omega_ex_e\). Level 2 fixes both.

2 · Anharmonicity, Morse, Dunham and RKR

Morse oscillator

\[ V(r) = D_e\left[1-e^{-a(r-r_e)}\right]^2,\qquad a = \omega_e\sqrt{\frac{2\pi^2 c\,\mu}{D_e h}},\qquad G(v)=\omega_e(v+\tfrac12)-\omega_ex_e(v+\tfrac12)^2,\ \ \omega_ex_e=\frac{\omega_e^2}{4D_e}. \]

The Morse oscillator is exactly solvable and has a finite number of bound levels, \(v_\text{max} = \lfloor \omega_e/(2\omega_ex_e) - \tfrac12\rfloor\). The level spacing \(\Delta G_{v+1/2} = \omega_e - 2\omega_ex_e(v+1)\) falls linearly. Extrapolating it to zero is the Birge–Sponer estimate of \(D_0\), exact for Morse: \(D_e = \omega_e^2/4\omega_ex_e\), \(D_0 = D_e - (\omega_e/2 - \omega_ex_e/4)\). Real molecules have long-range tails that Morse lacks, so Birge–Sponer usually overestimates \(D_0\).

Dunham expansion

The general rovibrational term value is a double series in \((v+\tfrac12)\) and \(J(J+1)\):

\[ E(v,J) = \sum_{k,l} Y_{kl}\,(v+\tfrac12)^k\,[J(J+1)]^l,\quad Y_{10}\approx\omega_e,\ Y_{20}\approx-\omega_ex_e,\ Y_{01}\approx B_e,\ Y_{11}\approx-\alpha_e,\ Y_{02}\approx -D_e . \]

Hence \(B_v = B_e - \alpha_e(v+\tfrac12)+\gamma_e(v+\tfrac12)^2\). The term \(Y_{00} = B_e/4 + \alpha_e\omega_e/12B_e + (\alpha_e\omega_e)^2/144B_e^3 - \omega_ex_e/4\) is a small zero-point correction. Within Born–Oppenheimer, \(Y_{kl}\propto\mu^{-(k/2+l)}\), which is how isotopologue constants are predicted.

RKR inversion

The Rydberg–Klein–Rees method inverts measured \(G(v)\) and \(B_v\) into the classical turning points of the potential, with no assumed functional form:

\[ r_\pm(v) = \sqrt{\frac{f}{g}+f^2}\pm f,\qquad f = \frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2\mu}}\int_{v_\text{min}}^{v}\frac{dv'}{\sqrt{G(v)-G(v')}},\qquad g = \frac{\sqrt{2\mu}}{\hbar}\int_{v_\text{min}}^{v}\frac{B_{v'}\,dv'}{\sqrt{G(v)-G(v')}} . \]
In this app · \(G(v)\) and \(B_v\) use every higher-order constant the WebBook lists. The integrable \(1/\sqrt{\cdot}\) singularity at the upper limit needs care in the quadrature. Turning points are joined with a cubic spline of \(u=\pm\sqrt{V}\), not of \(V\), because \(\sqrt V\) is nearly linear in \(r\) near the minimum. The Morse curve is shown against RKR at the same NIST energies.
Checked by · Round trip: the Numerov eigenvalues of the RKR curve reproduce \(G(v)+Y_{00}\). Morse reproduces \(\omega_e\) and \(\omega_ex_e\) exactly, so any residual is the higher Dunham terms.

3 · Numerical solution of the radial Schrödinger equation

For a diatomic with rotation \(J\), the nuclear motion obeys

\[ -\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}\frac{d^2\chi}{dr^2} + \left[V(r) + \frac{\hbar^2J(J+1)}{2\mu r^2}\right]\chi = E\,\chi,\qquad \chi(r_\text{min})=\chi(r_\text{max})=0 . \]

Finite differences + Sturm bisection

On a uniform grid \(r_i\) with spacing \(h\), \(\chi''\approx(\chi_{i+1}-2\chi_i+\chi_{i-1})/h^2\) turns the equation into a symmetric tridiagonal eigenproblem. Its eigenvalues are counted below any trial energy by the Sturm sequence (number of sign changes of the leading principal minors), and each level is bracketed and bisected individually. The error is \(O(h^2)\).

Numerov shooting

Numerov's method integrates \(\chi'' = k(r)\chi\) with \(k = 2\mu(V-E)/\hbar^2\) to \(O(h^6)\) per step:

\[ \left(1-\tfrac{h^2}{12}k_{i+1}\right)\chi_{i+1} = 2\left(1+\tfrac{5h^2}{12}k_i\right)\chi_i - \left(1-\tfrac{h^2}{12}k_{i-1}\right)\chi_{i-1}. \]

Integrating outward and counting nodes tells whether a trial \(E\) lies above or below the \(v\)-th level (\(v\) nodes). Bisection on \(E\) converges to the eigenvalue.

Expectation values

The vibrationally averaged rotational constant follows from the numerical wavefunction, \(B_v = \frac{\hbar}{4\pi c\mu}\langle \chi_v|r^{-2}|\chi_v\rangle\). This is why \(B_0\lt B_e\) without fitting \(\alpha_e\).

In this app · 8000 grid points with Dirichlet boundaries for the finite-difference solver. Wavefunctions are normalised by Simpson's rule. Any \(V(r)\) works: harmonic, Morse, RKR spline, or the ab initio curves from Q2.
Checked by · Self-tests: H₂ harmonic levels match \(\omega_e(v+\tfrac12)\) within 2×10⁻⁴ %, H₂ and I₂ Morse levels match the analytic \(G(v)\) within 10⁻²–10⁻³ %.

4 · Rovibrational and Raman spectra

A vibrational band \(v''\to v'\) with \(\Delta J=\pm1\) splits into the R branch (\(J'=J''+1\)) and the P branch (\(J'=J''-1\)). Using \(x=J(J+1)\):

\[ \tilde\nu = \nu_0 + F_{v'}(J') - F_{v''}(J''),\qquad F_v(J) = B_vx - D_vx^2 + H_vx^3 . \]

Because \(B_1\lt B_0\), R lines converge (band head at high J) and P lines spread apart. The Q branch (\(\Delta J=0\)) appears only when the molecule has electronic angular momentum, as NO does. Its lines spread by \((B_1-B_0)J(J+1)\).

Intensities

\[ I \propto \tilde\nu\, S_J\, \frac{g_{J''}\,e^{-hcE''/k_BT}}{q}\left(1-e^{-hc\tilde\nu/k_BT}\right) ,\]

with Hönl–London factors \(S_J\) (\(J''+1\) for R, \(J''\) for P), the Boltzmann population of the lower state, and stimulated-emission correction. Raman scattering obeys \(\Delta J = 0,\pm2\) (polarisability is a rank-2 tensor), giving O, Q and S branches and allowing homonuclear molecules such as N₂ and H₂.

Fitting lines

Given assigned P and R lines, linear least squares on \(\nu = \nu_0 + B_1x' - B_0x - D_1x'^2 + D_0x^2 + H(x'^3-x^3)\) returns \(\nu_0, B_0, B_1, D_0, D_1, H\). Then \(\alpha_e \approx B_0-B_1\), \(B_e\approx B_0+\alpha_e/2\) and \(r_e=\sqrt{h/8\pi^2c\mu B_e}\). The measured lines alone fix the bond length.

In this app · The CO fundamental fit uses 69 NIST lines and six parameters. Its reference is the band origin from the NIST Dunham expansion. The I₂ visible band (Level 5) adds Franck–Condon factors.

5 · Electronic structure of diatomics

H₂⁺ by LCAO

With \(\psi_\pm = (1s_A\pm1s_B)/\sqrt{2(1\pm S)}\) and a 1s exponent \(\zeta\), the energy is

\[ E_\pm(R) = \frac{H_{AA}\pm H_{AB}}{1\pm S} + \frac1R ,\]

with closed-form overlap, Coulomb and resonance integrals. Optimising \(\zeta\) at every \(R\) by golden-section search improves the bond (variationally, \(\zeta\to\) ~1.24 at \(R_e\)). The antibonding \(E_-\) is repulsive at all \(R\).

H₂: Hartree–Fock and the dissociation problem

RHF puts both electrons in \(\sigma_g\). At large \(R\) that wavefunction is 50 % ionic (H⁺H⁻), so the RHF curve dissociates far too high. A 2×2 CI that mixes \(\sigma_g^2\) and \(\sigma_u^2\) fixes this exactly in a minimal basis. It is the full CI for two electrons in two orbitals (MP2, CIS, full CI).

Franck–Condon factors (I₂ B←X)

For a vertical electronic transition, the vibrational band intensities are the squared overlaps of the two states' vibrational wavefunctions:

\[ q_{v'v''} = \left|\int\chi_{v'}^{(B)}(r)\,\chi_{v''}^{(X)}(r)\,dr\right|^2 ,\qquad \sum_{v'}q_{v'v''} = 1\ \ (\text{bound + continuum}) . \]
In this app · Both I₂ states are Morse oscillators built from NIST \(T_e,\omega_e,\omega_ex_e,r_e\). Wavefunctions come from the finite-difference solver on one shared grid (3500 points, 2.25–7.0 Å). Lines are weighted by thermal populations of X-state levels and broadened. The B state's 0.36 Å longer bond pushes the strongest bands to high \(v'\).
Limits · Morse overestimates the B-state well depth (wrong long-range tail). Intensity missing above \(v'=74\) goes to the dissociation continuum, which is not modelled.

6 · Polyatomic molecules

Moments of inertia and rotor classes

The inertia tensor \(I_{\alpha\beta} = \sum_A m_A(r_A^2\delta_{\alpha\beta} - r_{A\alpha}r_{A\beta})\) (centre of mass at the origin) is diagonalised to \(I_a\le I_b\le I_c\), giving \(A,B,C = h/8\pi^2cI\). Linear, symmetric-top, spherical-top and asymmetric-top molecules follow. Ray's parameter \(\kappa = (2B-A-C)/(A-C)\) measures asymmetry (−1 prolate, +1 oblate).

Normal modes

In mass-weighted coordinates \(q_i = \sqrt{m_i}\,x_i\), the harmonic Hamiltonian separates into independent oscillators. The eigenvalues \(\lambda_k\) of the mass-weighted Hessian give the frequencies:

\[ \mathbf{H}^{(m)}_{ij} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{m_im_j}}\frac{\partial^2V}{\partial x_i\partial x_j},\qquad \mathbf{H}^{(m)}\mathbf{L}_k = \lambda_k\mathbf{L}_k,\qquad \tilde\nu_k = \frac{\sqrt{\lambda_k}}{2\pi c}. \]

Six eigenvalues (five for linear molecules) are zero: translations and rotations. Level 6 uses an empirical valence force field; Q3 uses ab initio Hessians (Q3).

Hückel theory

For conjugated π systems, each carbon contributes one p orbital. Then \(H_{ii}=\alpha\), \(H_{ij}=\beta\) for bonded neighbours, \(S=\mathbf 1\). The orbital energies are \(\alpha + x_k\beta\), where the \(x_k\) are the adjacency-matrix eigenvalues; for a linear polyene of \(n\) carbons, \(x_k = 2\cos(k\pi/(n+1))\). The HOMO–LUMO gap \(\Delta x|\beta|\) predicts \(\lambda_\text{max} = hc/(\Delta x|\beta|)\). One \(|\beta|\) is fitted by least squares to four polyenes.

Asymmetric top: H₂O rotational spectrum

The rigid asymmetric rotor \(H = AJ_a^2+BJ_b^2+CJ_c^2\) is diagonalised in the symmetric-top basis \(|J,K\rangle\) (representation Iʳ: \(z=a\)), where

\[ H = \tfrac12(B+C)\mathbf J^2 + \left[A-\tfrac12(B+C)\right]J_z^2 + \tfrac14(B-C)\left(J_+^2+J_-^2\right). \]

Levels are labelled \(J_{K_aK_c}\) by energy order. Water's light hydrogens make centrifugal distortion large, so the app uses Watson's A-reduced Hamiltonian:

\[ \begin{aligned} H = H_\text{rigid} &- \Delta_J\mathbf J^4 - \Delta_{JK}\mathbf J^2J_z^2 - \Delta_KJ_z^4 - 2\delta_J\mathbf J^2(J_x^2-J_y^2) - \delta_K[J_z^2,\,J_x^2-J_y^2]_+\\ &+ H_J\mathbf J^6 + H_{JK}\mathbf J^4J_z^2 + H_{KJ}\mathbf J^2J_z^4 + H_KJ_z^6 + 2h_J\mathbf J^4(J_x^2-J_y^2)+h_{JK}\mathbf J^2[J_z^2,\cdot]_+ + h_K[J_z^4,\cdot]_+\\ &+ L_{JK}\mathbf J^4J_z^4 + L_{KKJ}\mathbf J^2J_z^6 + L_KJ_z^8 + \ell_K[J_z^6,\cdot]_+ + P_KJ_z^{10} + p_K[J_z^8,\cdot]_+ \end{aligned} \]

with \([X,Y]_+ = XY+YX\), “·” standing for \(J_x^2-J_y^2\), and \(\langle K+2|J_x^2-J_y^2|K\rangle = \tfrac12\sqrt{[x-K(K+1)][x-(K+1)(K+2)]}\). Line strengths use b-type selection rules (\(\Delta K_a,\Delta K_c\) odd, since H₂O's dipole lies along b) and ortho:para = 3:1 spin weights.

Checked by · With the full NIST constant set, all 16 benchmark lines (22 GHz–1.67 THz) agree within 0.0011 %. The rigid rotor misses by up to ~100 %. The convergence chart shows each order gaining one to two decades. Self-test: \(E(1_{10})-E(1_{01}) = A-C\) exactly.

Statistical thermodynamics

For an ideal gas the molecular partition function factorises, \(q = q_\text{tr}\,q_\text{rot}\,q_\text{vib}\,q_\text{el}\), and

\[ \frac{q_\text{tr}}{V} = \left(\frac{2\pi mk_BT}{h^2}\right)^{3/2},\quad q_\text{vib} = \prod_k\frac{1}{1-e^{-\theta_k/T}},\quad \theta_k=\frac{hc\tilde\nu_k}{k_B},\quad q_\text{rot}^\text{nonlinear} = \frac{\sqrt\pi}{\sigma}\sqrt{\frac{T^3}{\theta_A\theta_B\theta_C}} . \]

The translational entropy is the Sackur–Tetrode equation, \(S_\text{tr} = R\left[\ln\!\left(\frac{q_\text{tr}}{N_A}\frac{k_BT}{p}\right)+\tfrac52\right]\). Heat capacity: \(C_p = \tfrac52R\) (translation + \(pV\)), plus \(R\) (linear) or \(\tfrac32R\) (nonlinear) rotation, plus an Einstein function \(R\,u^2e^u/(e^u-1)^2\), \(u=\theta_k/T\), per mode. For diatomics the app also sums rovibrational levels explicitly from the Dunham expansion, with no harmonic or rigid-rotor assumption.

In this app · References are the NIST WebBook Shomate fits, \(C_p^\circ = A+Bt+Ct^2+Dt^3+E/t^2\) with \(t=T/1000\), and the matching \(H^\circ-H^\circ_{298}\) and \(S^\circ\) expressions.
Limits · Harmonic modes underestimate high-T heat capacities: anharmonic levels crowd together, raising \(C_p\). Low-lying electronic states are included only where listed.

7 · Molecular dynamics

Newton's equations for \(N\) atoms in a periodic cubic box (minimum-image convention) are integrated with velocity Verlet, which is time-reversible and symplectic, so energy does not drift systematically:

\[ \mathbf v(t+\tfrac{\Delta t}2) = \mathbf v(t)+\tfrac{\Delta t}{2m}\mathbf F(t),\quad \mathbf r(t+\Delta t) = \mathbf r(t)+\Delta t\,\mathbf v(t+\tfrac{\Delta t}2),\quad \mathbf v(t+\Delta t) = \mathbf v(t+\tfrac{\Delta t}2)+\tfrac{\Delta t}{2m}\mathbf F(t+\Delta t). \]

Interactions, cut-offs and tail corrections

The pair potential is truncated at \(r_c\) (tabulated potentials are also shifted to zero there) and corrected for the neglected tail assuming \(g(r)=1\) beyond \(r_c\):

\[ \frac{U_\text{tail}}{N} = 2\pi\rho\int_{r_c}^\infty u(r)\,r^2dr,\qquad P_\text{tail} = -\frac{2\pi}{3}\rho^2\int_{r_c}^\infty r\,u'(r)\,r^2dr. \]

Measured quantities

\[ T = \frac{2K}{3(N-1)k_B},\qquad P = \rho k_BT + \frac{1}{3V}\sum_{i\lt j}\mathbf r_{ij}\cdot\mathbf F_{ij} + P_\text{tail},\qquad g(r) = \frac{\langle n(r,r+dr)\rangle}{4\pi r^2dr\,\rho},\qquad D = \lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\langle|\Delta\mathbf r|^2\rangle}{6t}. \]
In this app · Equilibration with velocity rescaling (NVT), production in NVE. Default \(\Delta t^*=0.005\) (≈ 11 fs for argon). Cell lists (cells ≥ \(r_c\)) make the force loop \(O(N)\). The force kernel runs in a WebAssembly module, written in C, compiled with zig, and embedded as base64. For non-LJ potentials it reads tabulated \(u\) and \(F/r\) on a uniform grid in \(r^2\). MSD slope over the last 70 % of the window.
Checked by · Self-test: LJ NVE, N = 108, Δt* = 0.002, 10⁴ steps, relative energy drift below 0.1 %. WASM and JS kernels give identical trajectories to round-off.
Limits · Near-zero liquid pressures are small differences of large terms, so expect large relative scatter. Small N means finite-size effects in \(D\) (∝ \(N^{-1/3}\)).

7 · Pair potentials and virial coefficients

Lennard-Jones

\[ u_\text{LJ}(r) = 4\varepsilon\left[\left(\frac\sigma r\right)^{12}-\left(\frac\sigma r\right)^6\right] \]

This is an effective potential: its σ and ε, fitted to bulk data, silently absorb many-body and quantum effects. That is why it works well for liquids yet differs from the true two-body potential.

Tang–Toennies (accurate argon pair potential)

\[ u_\text{TT}(R) = A\,e^{-bR} - \sum_{n=3}^{8} f_{2n}(bR)\,\frac{C_{2n}}{R^{2n}},\qquad f_{2n}(x) = 1-e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{2n}\frac{x^k}{k!},\qquad C_{2n} = \left(\frac{C_{2n-2}}{C_{2n-4}}\right)^3C_{2n-6}. \]

The damping functions \(f_{2n}\) switch the dispersion off at short range. \(C_6, C_8, C_{10}\) are literature values; the higher terms follow the recursion. \(A\) and \(b\) are solved so that the well reproduces Aziz's argon minimum (99.553 cm⁻¹ at 3.757 Å).

In this app · For \(x \lt 2n+1\), \(f_{2n}\) is evaluated from its tail \(e^{-x}\sum_{k\gt 2n}x^k/k!\). The direct form cancels to round-off at short range and once returned \(-10^{16}\) at \(r \lt 0.02\sigma\), a bug found by Widom insertion and fixed (self-test).

Second virial coefficient

The low-density equation of state \(Z = pV_m/RT = 1 + B_2(T)/V_m + \dots\) has

\[ B_2(T) = -2\pi N_A\int_0^\infty\left(e^{-u(r)/k_BT}-1\right)r^2dr,\qquad \Delta B_2^\text{qu} = \frac{N_Ah^2}{24\pi m(k_BT)^3}\int_0^\infty e^{-u/k_BT}\left(u'\right)^2r^2dr \ \ (\text{Wigner–Kirkwood}). \]

The Boyle temperature is the root of \(B_2(T)=0\). The quantum correction matters for neon, where it is fitted alongside σ and ε. The NIST reference is \(B_2 = (Z-1)/\rho\) at 0.1 MPa from the reference equation of state; the third-virial contamination is below 0.05 cm³/mol.

Checked by · Tang–Toennies \(B_2\) agrees with NIST to 0.6–2 % with no fitting to \(B_2\).

8 · Phase equilibria: isotherms, Gibbs ensemble, NpT

Isotherms and the Maxwell construction

Below \(T_c\), a finite-size simulated isotherm \(P(\rho)\) shows a van der Waals-like loop. Coexistence requires equal \(T\), \(P\) and \(\mu\); equal \(\mu\) is equivalent to equal areas:

\[ \int_{v_l}^{v_g}P(v)\,dv = P_\text{sat}\,(v_g-v_l). \]

The app fits the isotherm (forced through \(P=0\) at \(\rho=0\)), locates the spinodals (the vapour one comes first along \(\rho\)), scans for a sign change of the area difference and bisects \(P_\text{sat}\).

Gibbs-ensemble Monte Carlo (Panagiotopoulos)

Two periodic boxes, without an interface, exchange volume and particles at fixed total \(N, V, T\). Three move types, each with a Metropolis acceptance probability \(\min(1,e^{\,\cdot})\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{displacement:}&\quad -\beta\Delta U \\ \text{volume } (\ln\tfrac{V_1}{V_2}\text{ random walk}):&\quad -\beta\Delta U_\text{tot} + (N_1+1)\ln\tfrac{V_1'}{V_1} + (N_2+1)\ln\tfrac{V_2'}{V_2}\\ \text{transfer } 2\to1:&\quad \ln\frac{N_2V_1}{(N_1+1)V_2} - \beta(\Delta U_1+\Delta U_2). \end{aligned} \]

At equilibrium one box holds liquid and the other vapour, and their densities are the coexistence densities.

NpT Monte Carlo

One box at fixed \(N,P,T\), with displacement moves and \(\ln V\) moves accepted with \(-\beta[\Delta U + P\Delta V] + (N+1)\ln(V'/V)\). Running at the NIST \(p_\text{sat}\) gives the model's liquid density at coexistence. The liquid is nearly incompressible, so this matches the coexistence density to high accuracy, and converges far faster than GEMC particle swaps into a dense liquid.

In this app · Step sizes auto-tune to ~40–45 % acceptance during equilibration. GEMC boxes may not shrink below 2× the cut-off (prevents a near-empty box collapsing on its own images). Densities are block-averaged (10 blocks); the liquid is whichever box is denser at each sample.
Limits · GEMC near \(T_c\) and at high liquid density converges slowly (swap acceptance ≪ 1 %). Use N = 256 and ≥ 6000 cycles, or the NpT card.

8 · Three-body (Axilrod–Teller–Muto) forces

Pair potentials are not the whole story. At third order in perturbation theory, three mutually induced dipoles give the triple-dipole energy:

\[ u_3 = C_9\,\frac{1+3\cos\gamma_1\cos\gamma_2\cos\gamma_3}{(r_{12}\,r_{13}\,r_{23})^3}, \]

with \(\gamma_i\) the interior angles of the triangle. It is repulsive for near-equilateral triangles (common in dense liquids) and attractive for near-linear ones. Net effect in liquid argon: about +0.2 kJ/mol per atom (a few % of the cohesive energy), which lowers the liquid density by ~7 % compared with an exact pair potential alone.

In this app · \(C_9 = 518\) a.u. ⚠ (literature range ≈ 516–522 a.u.), adjustable. All triplets with every side between 0.8σ and 2.5σ are summed. The bare term diverges at contact, where pair repulsion dominates anyway. For a moving particle, the code builds its neighbour list within 2.5σ and loops over neighbour pairs; box totals count each triplet once at its lowest index.
Checked by · NpT liquid density at the NIST \(p_\text{sat}\), 90–130 K: pair-only ≈ +8 %, pair + three-body within about ±2 % (mean +0.5 %).
Limits · No three-body tail correction beyond 2.5σ (≈ 5 % of \(\langle U_3\rangle\)). Higher multipole (DDQ, …) and three-body exchange terms are omitted.

8 · Chemical potential by Widom test-particle insertion

The excess chemical potential is the free-energy cost of adding one particle. Inserting a “ghost” at random and averaging its Boltzmann factor gives (NpT form):

\[ \beta\mu_\text{ex} = -\ln\frac{\langle V\,e^{-\beta\Delta U}\rangle}{\langle V\rangle}, \]

where \(\Delta U\) is the ghost's pair energy within \(r_c\), the change in tail correction \(2\pi(2N+1)I/V\), and every triplet it would form. The ghost is never kept.

A reference from NIST data alone

At coexistence \(\mu_L=\mu_V\). Writing \(\mu = k_BT\ln(\rho\Lambda^3)+\mu_\text{ex}\) and treating the vapour to second-virial order (\(\ln\varphi \approx 2(Z-1)-\ln Z\)):

\[ \beta\mu_\text{ex}^{(L)} = \ln\frac{\rho_V}{\rho_L} + 2\,(Z_V-1),\qquad Z_V = \frac{p_\text{sat}}{\rho_V k_BT}, \]

all from the NIST saturation table.

In this app · 256 insertions per cycle. The error is from block means of \(\langle Ve^{-\beta\Delta U}\rangle\) by the delta method, \(\sigma(\ln A) = \sigma_A/A\).
Limits · In the densest liquid (90 K) almost every insertion overlaps, so the average is dominated by rare cavities and is both noisy and biased. Above ~120 K the second-virial vapour correction is approximate.

8 · Transport coefficients (Green–Kubo)

Linear-response theory relates each transport coefficient to the time integral of an equilibrium autocorrelation function:

\[ D = \frac13\int_0^\infty\langle\mathbf v(0)\cdot\mathbf v(t)\rangle dt,\qquad \eta = \frac{V}{k_BT}\int_0^\infty\langle P_{xy}(0)P_{xy}(t)\rangle dt,\qquad \lambda = \frac{1}{3Vk_BT^2}\int_0^\infty\langle\mathbf J_q(0)\cdot\mathbf J_q(t)\rangle dt, \]

with \(P_{xy} = \sum_i m v_{ix}v_{iy} + \sum_{i\lt j}x_{ij}F_{ij,y}\) and heat current \(\mathbf J_q = \sum_i e_i\mathbf v_i + \tfrac12\sum_{i\ne j}(\mathbf F_{ij}\cdot\mathbf v_i)\mathbf r_{ij}\).

In this app · Correlations are averaged over the three off-diagonal stress components and independent replicas run in parallel. The running integral is read as the mean of its plateau between 1.5 and 3 ps. References: NIST viscosity and thermal-conductivity correlations for argon.
Limits · Long-time tails and noise make the plateau choice matter; error bars come from replica scatter.

8 · The 2D Ising model

\(H = -J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}s_is_j\), \(s_i=\pm1\) on an \(L\times L\) periodic square lattice. Via \(n_i = (1+s_i)/2\) it is a lattice gas: occupied and empty sites with nearest-neighbour attraction. Its critical point is in the same universality class as a 2D liquid–vapour critical point (argon belongs to the 3D Ising class).

Exact results (Onsager, Yang)

\[ T_c = \frac{2}{\ln(1+\sqrt2)} = 2.269185\ldots,\qquad m(T\lt T_c) = \left[1-\sinh^{-4}(2K)\right]^{1/8},\qquad K = \frac{J}{k_BT}, \]
\[ \frac{u}{J} = -\coth 2K\left[1+\frac2\pi\left(2\tanh^22K-1\right)K_1(\kappa)\right],\qquad \kappa = \frac{2\sinh2K}{\cosh^22K}, \]

with \(K_1\) the complete elliptic integral of the first kind, computed as \(\pi/[2\,\mathrm{AGM}(1,\sqrt{1-\kappa^2})]\). At \(T_c\), \(u=-\sqrt2\).

Algorithms

Metropolis: flip one spin with probability \(\min(1,e^{-\beta\Delta E})\), \(\Delta E\in\{0,4,8\}J\). Wolff: grow a cluster from a random seed, adding aligned neighbours with probability \(p = 1-e^{-2\beta J}\), then flip it whole. This satisfies detailed balance with acceptance 1 and flips correlated domains at once.

Finite-size scaling and dynamics

\[ U_4 = 1-\frac{\langle m^4\rangle}{3\langle m^2\rangle^2},\qquad \chi = \beta N(\langle m^2\rangle-\langle|m|\rangle^2),\qquad C = \beta^2N(\langle e^2\rangle-\langle e\rangle^2),\qquad \tau_\text{int} = \tfrac12+\sum_{t\ge1}\rho(t)\propto L^z . \]

\(U_4\) is scale-invariant at \(T_c\), so curves for different \(L\) cross there at a universal value \(U^*\approx0.611\). The integrated autocorrelation time is summed with Sokal's self-consistent window (stop when \(t\ge6\tau\)). The dynamic exponent is \(z\approx2.17\) for Metropolis and \(z \lt 0.3\) for Wolff.

In this app · A Wolff “sweep” is a fixed number of clusters ≈ \(N/\langle c\rangle\), set during equilibration. Stopping once \(N\) spins have flipped ties the measurement times to cluster sizes and biases averages by several error bars; the app did this at first and the bug was found and fixed. 20 blocks per run for error bars; Binder crossings by linear interpolation on the T grid.
Checked by · Self-test: Onsager \(u\to-\sqrt2\) at \(T_c\). Run results: \(T_c\) from L = 32/64 within 0.08 %; \(u\) and \(m\) at L = 64 within 0.1 % of exact; Wolff and Metropolis agree within error.

Q1 · Gaussian basis sets and molecular integrals

Molecular orbitals are expanded in contracted Cartesian Gaussians centred on atoms:

\[ \phi_\mu(\mathbf r) = \sum_k d_{k}\,N_k\,x_A^{l}y_A^{m}z_A^{n}\,e^{-\alpha_k r_A^2},\qquad N_k = \left(\frac{2\alpha_k}{\pi}\right)^{3/4}\frac{(4\alpha_k)^{(l+m+n)/2}}{\sqrt{(2l-1)!!(2m-1)!!(2n-1)!!}} . \]

STO-3G fits each Slater orbital with three Gaussians (minimal basis). 6-31G is split-valence: the core is 6 Gaussians, and the valence is split into an inner 3-Gaussian and an outer 1-Gaussian function. 6-31G* adds a set of six Cartesian d functions on non-hydrogen atoms (polarisation).

McMurchie–Davidson integrals

The product of two Gaussians is a Gaussian at \(\mathbf P = (a\mathbf A+b\mathbf B)/p\), \(p=a+b\) (Gaussian product theorem). It is expanded in Hermite Gaussians with coefficients from recursions:

\[ E^{i+1,j}_t = \frac{1}{2p}E^{ij}_{t-1} + X_{PA}E^{ij}_t + (t+1)E^{ij}_{t+1},\qquad E^{00}_0 = e^{-\frac{ab}{p}X_{AB}^2}. \]

Overlap, kinetic and dipole integrals follow directly from them. Coulomb-type integrals need the Hermite Coulomb integrals \(R_{tuv}\), built recursively from the Boys function:

\[ F_n(T) = \int_0^1 t^{2n}e^{-Tt^2}dt,\qquad (\mu\nu|\lambda\sigma) = \frac{2\pi^{5/2}}{pq\sqrt{p+q}}\sum_{tuv}E^{\mu\nu}_{tuv}\sum_{\tau\nu\phi}(-1)^{\tau+\nu+\phi}E^{\lambda\sigma}_{\tau\nu\phi}\,R_{t+\tau,u+\nu,v+\phi}(\alpha,\mathbf R_{PQ}) . \]

\(F_n\) uses a series with downward recursion for small \(T\) and the asymptotic form with upward recursion for large \(T\). Schwarz screening, \(|(\mu\nu|\lambda\sigma)|\le\sqrt{(\mu\nu|\mu\nu)(\lambda\sigma|\lambda\sigma)}\), skips negligible quartets (threshold 10⁻¹²). The 8-fold permutational symmetry means only unique quartets are computed.

In this app · Two-electron integrals run in a WebAssembly kernel (C, McMurchie–Davidson, its own exp() to 2×10⁻¹⁶, growable heap). There is an identical JavaScript fallback. Basis exponents and contraction coefficients are the standard published STO-3G and 6-31G(*) values; energies are validated against literature (H₂O/6-31G* −76.0107 Eh, CH₄ −40.1952 Eh).
Checked by · Self-test: WASM vs JS integrals for H₂O/6-31G* (130 321 integrals) agree to 2×10⁻¹⁶ with the same MP2 energy.

Q1 · Restricted Hartree–Fock (Roothaan–Hall)

For a closed-shell determinant of \(n_\text{occ}=N/2\) doubly occupied orbitals \(\psi_i = \sum_\mu C_{\mu i}\phi_\mu\), minimising the energy gives the Roothaan–Hall equations:

\[ \mathbf F\mathbf C = \mathbf S\mathbf C\boldsymbol\varepsilon,\qquad F_{\mu\nu} = H_{\mu\nu} + \sum_{\lambda\sigma}P_{\lambda\sigma}\left[(\mu\nu|\lambda\sigma) - \tfrac12(\mu\lambda|\nu\sigma)\right],\qquad P_{\lambda\sigma} = 2\sum_{i}^{occ}C_{\lambda i}C_{\sigma i}, \]
\[ E_\text{RHF} = \tfrac12\sum_{\mu\nu}P_{\mu\nu}(H_{\mu\nu}+F_{\mu\nu}) + V_{nn},\qquad H_{\mu\nu} = T_{\mu\nu}+V^\text{nuc}_{\mu\nu}. \]

Canonical orthogonalisation \(\mathbf X = \mathbf U\mathbf s^{-1/2}\) turns this into an ordinary eigenproblem, \(\mathbf F' = \mathbf X^\dagger\mathbf F\mathbf X\).

Convergence: guesses and DIIS

Two starting guesses are tried, the core Hamiltonian and the generalised Wolfsberg–Helmholz \(H_{\mu\nu}=1.75\,S_{\mu\nu}(H_{\mu\mu}+H_{\nu\nu})/2\). The lower converged energy is kept, which avoids wrong SCF solutions (N₂ once converged to one). The two guesses share one set of integrals. DIIS (Pulay) extrapolates the Fock matrix from the last 8 iterations to minimise the commutator error \(\mathbf e = \mathbf X^\dagger(\mathbf{FPS}-\mathbf{SPF})\mathbf X\):

\[ \mathbf F_\text{DIIS} = \sum_k c_k\mathbf F_k,\qquad \min\Big\|\sum_k c_k\mathbf e_k\Big\|\ \ \text{s.t.}\ \sum_kc_k=1 . \]

Convergence: \(|\Delta E|\lt10^{-11}\) Eh and RMS \(\Delta P\lt10^{-9}\).

Properties

\[ \boldsymbol\mu = \sum_AZ_A\mathbf R_A - \sum_{\mu\nu}P_{\mu\nu}\langle\mu|\mathbf r|\nu\rangle,\qquad q_A = Z_A - \sum_{\mu\in A}(\mathbf{PS})_{\mu\mu}\ (\text{Mulliken}),\qquad IE_\text{Koopmans} = -\varepsilon_\text{HOMO}. \]
Limits · HF misses electron correlation, about 1 % of the total energy but often most of a reaction or binding energy. RHF cannot dissociate bonds correctly. Koopmans ionisation energies benefit from a cancellation: missing orbital relaxation lowers them, missing correlation raises them.

Q1 · Open shells: unrestricted Hartree–Fock and ΔSCF

UHF gives α and β electrons separate orbitals (Pople–Nesbet equations), so radicals, cations and triplets are possible:

\[ F^\alpha_{\mu\nu} = H_{\mu\nu} + \sum_{\lambda\sigma}\left[P^T_{\lambda\sigma}(\mu\nu|\lambda\sigma) - P^\alpha_{\lambda\sigma}(\mu\lambda|\nu\sigma)\right],\qquad P^T = P^\alpha+P^\beta,\qquad n_\alpha-n_\beta = 2S , \]
\[ E_\text{UHF} = \tfrac12\sum_{\mu\nu}\left[P^T_{\mu\nu}H_{\mu\nu} + P^\alpha_{\mu\nu}F^\alpha_{\mu\nu} + P^\beta_{\mu\nu}F^\beta_{\mu\nu}\right] + V_{nn}. \]

A UHF determinant is not a pure spin state. Its spin contamination is

\[ \langle\hat S^2\rangle = S_z(S_z+1) + n_\beta - \sum_{i\in\alpha}\sum_{j\in\beta}\left|(\mathbf C_\alpha^\dagger\mathbf S\mathbf C_\beta)_{ij}\right|^2 . \]

For a doublet the exact value is 0.75; the tables flag values above 0.8.

ΔSCF and ΔMP2 ionisation energies

The vertical ionisation energy is the energy difference at the neutral geometry, \(IE = E(\text{M}^+,\text{UHF}) - E(\text{M},\text{RHF})\), with the same for MP2/UMP2. ΔSCF includes orbital relaxation of the cation but not the correlation difference. The neutral has one more electron pair and therefore more correlation energy, so ΔSCF ionisation energies are typically ~1 eV too low. ΔMP2 recovers most of that.

In this app · DIIS on the stacked α and β commutator errors with shared coefficients. The core-Hamiltonian guess is used, with optional α/β symmetry breaking by HOMO–LUMO rotation. One pool job per molecule computes the neutral RHF + MP2 and the doublet cation UHF + UMP2.
Checked by · Self-tests: closed-shell UHF reproduces RHF to 10⁻¹⁴ Eh; H atom/STO-3G = −0.466582 Eh; OH doublet ⟨S²⟩ ≈ 0.755.

Q1–Q2 · Electron correlation and excited states

Møller–Plesset second order (MP2)

Treating the difference between the true Hamiltonian and the Fock operator as a perturbation, the first correction to the HF energy is at second order:

\[ E^{(2)}_\text{RMP2} = \sum_{ij}^{occ}\sum_{ab}^{vir}\frac{(ia|jb)\left[2(ia|jb)-(ib|ja)\right]}{\varepsilon_i+\varepsilon_j-\varepsilon_a-\varepsilon_b} ,\]
\[ E^{(2)}_\text{UMP2} = \sum_{i\lt j,\,a\lt b}^{\alpha\alpha}\frac{\left[(ia|jb)-(ib|ja)\right]^2}{\Delta_{ijab}} + \sum_{i\lt j,\,a\lt b}^{\beta\beta}\frac{\left[(ia|jb)-(ib|ja)\right]^2}{\Delta_{ijab}} + \sum_{i\in\alpha,\,j\in\beta}\sum_{a\in\alpha,\,b\in\beta}\frac{(ia|jb)^2}{\Delta_{ijab}} . \]

MO integrals \((ia|jb)\) come from four successive quarter-transformations of the AO integrals, \(O(n^5)\), restricted to the occupied and virtual blocks. The frozen-core approximation leaves the 1s orbitals of Li–F uncorrelated.

Configuration interaction singles (CIS)

Excited states are expanded in single excitations \(|\Phi_i^a\rangle\). Spin adaptation gives

\[ A^\text{singlet}_{ia,jb} = \delta_{ij}\delta_{ab}(\varepsilon_a-\varepsilon_i) + 2(ia|jb) - (ij|ab),\qquad A^\text{triplet}_{ia,jb} = \delta_{ij}\delta_{ab}(\varepsilon_a-\varepsilon_i) - (ij|ab), \]

with oscillator strengths \(f = \tfrac23\Delta E\,|\langle0|\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}|S\rangle|^2\), transition dipole \(\sqrt2\sum_{ia}c_{ia}\langle i|\mathbf r|a\rangle\).

Full CI for two electrons

For H₂ and HeH⁺ in a minimal basis, the singlet space is \(\{\sigma_g^2,\ \sigma_u^2,\ (\sigma_g\sigma_u)\}\). The 3×3 Hamiltonian is diagonalised exactly within the basis, which shows how CI repairs the RHF dissociation curve.

Limits · MP2 overestimates correlation in small-gap systems and inherits UHF spin contamination. CIS excitation energies are typically 0.5–1 eV too high (no doubles relaxation).

Q3 · Geometry optimisation, Hessians and IR spectra

Optimisation

The energy is minimised in symmetry-adapted internal parameters (bond lengths, angles): golden-section search for one parameter, Nelder–Mead simplex for several, with steps of 0.03 Å or 2°. Each SCF starts from the previous orbitals.

Vibrational-subspace Hessian

Rather than all \(3N\times3N\) Cartesian pairs, the app builds an orthonormal basis of the vibrational subspace in mass-weighted coordinates. The translation and rotation vectors, \(\sqrt{m_A}\,\hat e_k\) and \(\sqrt{m_A}\,(\hat e_k\times\mathbf r_A)\), are projected out by Gram–Schmidt, leaving \(3N-6\) (or \(3N-5\)) directions \(\mathbf d_k\). Finite differences are taken only along these:

\[ H_{kk} = \frac{E_{+k}-2E_0+E_{-k}}{h_k^2},\qquad H_{kl} = \frac{E_{++}+E_{--}-E_{+k}-E_{-k}-E_{+l}-E_{-l}+2E_0}{2h_kh_l} + O(h^2). \]

That is \(1+2n+n(n-1)\) energies for \(n\) internal coordinates: 13 for H₂O instead of 163, 91 for CH₄ instead of 451. Each displaced geometry is an independent job on the worker pool. Eigenvalues give harmonic frequencies; eigenvectors give the normal modes.

Why not analytic gradients? An analytic RHF gradient needs derivative integrals, costing roughly 3–4 SCFs per gradient, and a finite-difference Hessian from gradients needs \(2(3N-6)\) of them. At these molecule sizes that is more work than the subspace scheme, and analytic MP2 gradients are much harder still.

IR intensities and scaling

\[ A_k = \frac{N_A\pi}{3c^2}\left|\frac{\partial\boldsymbol\mu}{\partial Q_k}\right|^2 \ \ \Rightarrow\ \ A_k\,[\text{km/mol}] = 42.2561\left|\frac{\partial\boldsymbol\mu}{\partial Q_k}\right|^2_{\text{D}^2\,\text{Å}^{-2}\,\text{u}^{-1}}, \]

with dipole derivatives from the same ± displacements. Harmonic frequencies exceed observed fundamentals (anharmonicity plus method error). A single least-squares scale factor \(s = \sum\nu_\text{calc}\nu_\text{obs}/\sum\nu_\text{calc}^2\) is fitted per molecule and compared with literature (HF/6-31G* 0.8953, MP2/6-31G* 0.9427).

Checked by · Subspace and full Cartesian Hessians agree to ≤ 0.04 cm⁻¹ (H₂O/6-31G, CH₄/STO-3G). Self-test: the internal basis is orthonormal and orthogonal to translations and rotations.

Q2 · Quantum Monte Carlo (VMC and DMC)

Variational Monte Carlo

For a trial wavefunction \(\Psi_T\), the energy is an average of the local energy over \(|\Psi_T|^2\), sampled by Metropolis:

\[ E_\text{VMC} = \frac{\int|\Psi_T|^2E_L\,d\mathbf R}{\int|\Psi_T|^2d\mathbf R},\qquad E_L = \frac{\hat H\Psi_T}{\Psi_T}. \]

If \(\Psi_T\) were exact, \(E_L\) would be constant and its variance zero. The variance \(\sigma^2(E_L)\) measures trial-function quality.

Diffusion Monte Carlo

In imaginary time, \(-\partial_\tau\Psi = (\hat H-E_T)\Psi\) projects out the ground state. With importance sampling \(f=\Psi_T\Psi\), walkers drift along \(\mathbf v = \nabla\ln\Psi_T\), diffuse, and branch with weight \(e^{-\Delta\tau(\bar E_L - E_T)}\):

\[ \mathbf R' = \mathbf R + \Delta\tau\,\mathbf v(\mathbf R) + \sqrt{\Delta\tau}\,\boldsymbol\xi,\qquad A = \min\!\left(1,\frac{|\Psi_T(\mathbf R')|^2\,G(\mathbf R\leftarrow\mathbf R')}{|\Psi_T(\mathbf R)|^2\,G(\mathbf R'\leftarrow\mathbf R)}\right), \]

The Metropolis correction removes most of the time-step error. The population is held near its target by adjusting \(E_T\). The mixed estimator \(\langle E_L\rangle_f\) equals the exact energy for nodeless ground states (He, H₂ singlet), so there is no fixed-node error. The remaining \(O(\Delta\tau)\) bias is removed by linear extrapolation over Δτ = 0.04, 0.02, 0.01.

Trial functions and the Jastrow factor

\[ \Psi_T^{\text{He}} = e^{-2(r_1+r_2)}\,e^{u(r_{12})},\qquad \Psi_T^{\text{H}_2} = \prod_{i=1,2}\left(e^{-r_{iA}}+e^{-r_{iB}}\right)e^{u(r_{12})},\qquad u(r) = \frac{r}{2(1+\beta r)} . \]

The Padé–Jastrow factor satisfies the exact electron–electron cusp \(u'(0)=\tfrac12\), which cancels the \(1/r_{12}\) singularity in \(E_L\). With \(u' = 1/2(1+\beta r)^2\) and \(u'' = -\beta/(1+\beta r)^3\):

\[ E_L = E_L^{(0)} - u'\,(\mathbf g_1-\mathbf g_2)\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}_{12} - u'' - \frac{2u'}{r_{12}} - u'^2,\qquad \nabla_{1,2}\ln\Psi_T = \mathbf g_{1,2} \pm u'\,\hat{\mathbf r}_{12}. \]
Checked by · Self-test: He DMC with Jastrow within 0.5 % of −2.903724 Eh, and the Jastrow cuts \(\sigma^2(E_L)\) at least 5× (measured ≈ 14×).

Numerical methods and infrastructure

Linear algebra

Symmetric eigenproblems (Hessians, CI matrices, Fock matrices, rotor Hamiltonians) use cyclic Jacobi rotations. These are robust and accurate for the matrix sizes here (≤ ~60), and return orthonormal eigenvectors. Least-squares fits solve the normal equations; DIIS solves its small linear system by Gaussian elimination.

Statistics of stochastic results

Successive MC/MD samples are correlated, so the naive \(\sigma/\sqrt n\) underestimates the error. Block averaging splits the run into \(n_b\) (10–20) blocks much longer than the correlation time and uses \(\text{SE} = \sqrt{\sum_b(\bar x_b-\bar x)^2/n_b(n_b-1)}\). Non-linear estimators (\(-\ln\langle\cdot\rangle\), \(U_4\)) are computed per block or propagated by the delta method. Linear extrapolations (DMC Δτ → 0) are inverse-variance weighted.

Parallelism and performance

  • Worker pool: independent jobs (temperatures, displaced geometries, replicas, lattice sizes) are spread over a user-selectable number of Web Workers. Each worker carries the simulation code and the full QC engine as source text.
  • WebAssembly: two hand-written C kernels (MD forces, two-electron integrals), compiled to wasm32 and embedded as base64. They are compiled synchronously in workers and asynchronously on the main thread, with JavaScript fallbacks.
  • Cache: results are stored in IndexedDB under a deep, key-sorted hash of every input. An earlier version hashed only top-level keys, so different geometries collided; this was found and fixed.
  • Reproducibility: seeded mulberry32 random streams; sessions can be exported and imported as JSON.

Self-tests

The Self-test page runs on load. Synchronous tests cover constants, solvers, integrals, Watson lines, UHF and Onsager limits. Asynchronous tests run on the pool: MD energy conservation, pool vs main thread, WASM vs JS kernels, DMC and integral engines. Each states its tolerance; the nav badge shows the pass count.
