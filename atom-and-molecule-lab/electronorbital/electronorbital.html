Orbital Shapes
s orbitals (ℓ=0) — Spherically symmetric probability clouds centered at the nucleus. Each shell has exactly one s orbital.
p orbitals (ℓ=1) — Dumbbell-shaped lobes along x, y, z axes. Three orbitals per subshell (px, py, pz), holding up to 6 electrons.
d orbitals (ℓ=2) — Four-lobed “cloverleaf” shapes. Five orbitals per subshell (dxy, dxz, dyz, dx²−y², dz²), up to 10 electrons.
f orbitals (ℓ=3) — Complex multi-lobed shapes. Seven orbitals per subshell, up to 14 electrons.
Filling Rules
Aufbau Principle: Electrons fill the lowest-energy orbitals first, following the diagonal rule.
Pauli Exclusion: No two electrons in the same atom can share identical quantum numbers. Each orbital holds max 2 electrons with opposite spins (↑↓).
Hund’s Rule: Within a subshell, electrons first occupy separate orbitals with parallel spins before pairing up, minimizing repulsion.
Spectral Series (Hydrogen)
When electrons transition between energy levels, photons are emitted or absorbed. The energy of the photon equals the difference in energy levels:
E = hf = hc/λ = 13.6 eV · (1/n₁² − 1/n₂²)
