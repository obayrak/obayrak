0. Abbreviations & Symbols — Quick Reference

Every abbreviated term in this app is explained here. Hover over any underlined term in the UI to see its definition inline.

Methods & algorithms
HF
Hartree–Fock — mean-field quantum chemistry method. The many-electron wavefunction is approximated as a single Slater determinant; each electron moves in the averaged field of all others.
SCF
Self-Consistent Field — iterative procedure to solve the HF equations. Build potential from orbitals → solve for new orbitals → repeat until eigenvalues converge to <10⁻⁷ Ha.
CI
Configuration Interaction — post-HF method that adds excited Slater determinants (Φᵢᵃ, Φᵢⱼᵃᵇ …) to capture electron correlation missing from HF.
MP2
Møller–Plesset 2nd order — perturbation-theory correction to HF. This code uses a similar 2nd-order correction to estimate E_corr via doubly-excited configurations.
PT2 / PT
Perturbation Theory (2nd order) — the CI correction here estimates E_corr⁽²⁾ = −Σ |⟨Φᵢᵃ|V₁₂|Φ₀⟩|² / (εₐ − εᵢ), the leading term beyond HF.
DIIS
Direct Inversion in the Iterative Subspace (Pulay, 1980) — SCF acceleration method that minimises the error vector norm across stored iterations. This code uses simpler linear damping (η = 0.45).
Basis sets
STO
Slater-Type Orbital — χ(r) = N·rⁿ⁻¹·exp(−ζr)·Yₗᵐ(θ,φ). Correct exponential decay and nuclear cusp, but two-electron integrals are expensive analytically.
GTO
Gaussian-Type Orbital — g(α,r) = N·exp(−αr²). Integrals factorise analytically (product of two Gaussians is a Gaussian). Used to approximate STOs.
STO-nG
STO approximated by n Gaussians (Pople basis family, 1969). STO-2G: n=2, ~5% error; STO-3G: n=3, ~1–2% error; STO-6G: n=6, ~0.1% error.
ERI
Electron Repulsion Integral — (μν|κλ) = ⟨g_μ g_ν | r₁₂⁻¹ | g_κ g_λ⟩. The most expensive step: O(n⁴) per atom. Used to build the Coulomb (J) and exchange (K) matrices.
Quantum numbers & orbital labels
n
Principal quantum number — n = 1, 2, 3, … Sets the shell (K, L, M …). Orbital energy scales roughly as −Z²/(2n²) for hydrogen-like atoms.
l
Angular momentum quantum number — l = 0 (s), 1 (p), 2 (d), 3 (f). Determines the orbital shape and the centrifugal barrier l(l+1)/(2r²).
s, p, d, f
Orbital type labels for l = 0, 1, 2, 3 respectively. e.g., 2p = orbital with n=2, l=1. Superscript = occupancy (electrons in that orbital).
w
Orbital occupancy — number of electrons in the orbital (0–2 for s; 0–6 for p; 0–10 for d). Spin-restricted: each spatial orbital holds ≤2 electrons.
HOMO
Highest Occupied Molecular/Atomic Orbital — the outermost (valence) orbital containing electrons. Its eigenvalue determines the ionization energy via Koopmans' theorem.
Energy quantities
ε (epsilon)
Orbital energy eigenvalue — solution to F̂χᵢ = εᵢχᵢ. Negative (bound state). By Koopmans' theorem, IE ≈ −ε_HOMO.
E_tot
Total electronic energy — E_HF = Σᵢ εᵢ − ½Σᵢⱼ(Jᵢⱼ − Kᵢⱼ). NOT the sum of orbital energies — that double-counts electron–electron repulsion.
E_corr
Correlation energy — E_corr = E_exact − E_HF < 0. Always negative (HF is a variational upper bound). Typically −0.04 Ha per electron pair.
IE
Ionization Energy — minimum energy to remove one electron from the neutral atom: A → A⁺ + e⁻. Measured in eV experimentally; in Ha or eV computationally.
Units
Ha (Hartree)
Atomic unit of energy. 1 Ha = 27.2114 eV = 627.5 kcal/mol = 4.3597 × 10⁻¹⁸ J. Natural unit in quantum chemistry — all HF energies are in Ha internally.
eV
Electronvolt. 1 eV = 0.036749 Ha. Standard unit for ionization energies and spectroscopy. Use the toggle above to display all columns in eV.
a₀ (Bohr)
Bohr radius — atomic unit of length. 1 a₀ = 0.52918 Å. All radial plots (r-axis) use Bohr radii. ⟨r⟩ is the mean electron–nucleus distance in a₀.
References & comparison columns
C–R
Clementi & Roetti (1974) — tabulated near-exact numerical Hartree–Fock results for atoms H–Kr. Used as the gold-standard HF reference here.
NIST
National Institute of Standards and Technology — source of precise experimental ionization energies (IE_exp column) from the NIST Atomic Spectra Database.
Koopmans
Koopmans' theorem — IE ≈ −ε_HOMO, assuming orbital relaxation is negligible upon ionization. Error ~0.5–2 eV. Improved by CI quasiparticle correction.
Δ%
Relative error — (your value − C–R reference) / |C–R reference| × 100%. Dimensionless: same value in Ha or eV. Green <2%, amber 2–8%, red >8%.
Potential terms
Ĵ (Coulomb)
Coulomb / direct operator — Ĵⱼ φᵢ = [∫ |χⱼ|² r₁₂⁻¹ dr₂] φᵢ. Represents the classical electrostatic repulsion of electron i from the charge cloud of electron j.
K̂ (exchange)
Exchange / Fock operator — K̂ⱼ φᵢ = [∫ χⱼ*(x₂) r₁₂⁻¹ φᵢ(x₂) dr₂] χⱼ. Pure quantum-mechanical; has no classical analogue. Cancels self-interaction (Ĵᵢ − K̂ᵢ = 0).
Y⁰(ab;r)
Slater Coulomb integral — Y⁰(ab;r) = (1/r)∫₀ʳ Pₐ Pᵦ dr′ + ∫ᵣ∞ Pₐ Pᵦ/r′ dr′. Used for the numerical exchange potential. Evaluated via cumulative trapezoid integration.
V_eff(r)
Effective potential — V_eff(r) = U_HF(r) − Z/r + l(l+1)/(2r²). Sum of HF mean-field potential, nuclear attraction (−Z/r), and centrifugal barrier (angular momentum term).
ζ (zeta)
Slater orbital exponent — controls radial decay rate: χ ~ exp(−ζr). Larger ζ → more compact, core-like orbital. Optimised by Slater's screening rules.
α (alpha)
Gaussian exponent — controls Gaussian width: g ~ exp(−αr²). Each STO-nG shell uses n different α values with fitted coefficients d to mimic the STO.

1. The Hartree–Fock Approximation

Hartree–Fock (HF) theory is the foundation of ab initio quantum chemistry. It approximates the many-electron wavefunction Ψ as a single Slater determinant of N spin-orbitals {χᵢ}, thereby building in the Pauli exclusion principle exactly while treating electron–electron repulsion in a mean-field sense.

Ψ_HF = (1/√N!) det[χ₁(x₁) χ₂(x₂) … χ_N(x_N)]

The energy is minimised under the orthonormality constraint ⟨χᵢ|χⱼ⟩ = δᵢⱼ, yielding the Fock equations via the variational principle.

1.1 The Fock Operator

Each spin-orbital satisfies an effective one-electron eigenvalue equation:

F̂ χᵢ = εᵢ χᵢ (canonical HF equations)

The Fock operator F̂ decomposes into three contributions:

F̂ = ĥ + Σⱼ(Ĵⱼ − K̂ⱼ) ĥ = −½∇² − Z/r (kinetic + nuclear attraction) Ĵⱼ φᵢ = [∫ χⱼ*(x₂) r₁₂⁻¹ χⱼ(x₂) dx₂] φᵢ (Coulomb / direct) K̂ⱼ φᵢ = [∫ χⱼ*(x₂) r₁₂⁻¹ φᵢ(x₂) dx₂] χⱼ (exchange / Fock)

1.2 Self-Interaction Cancellation

The Coulomb sum Σⱼ Ĵⱼ over all j includes j = i (unphysical self-repulsion). The exchange operator exactly cancels this term for same-spin orbitals:

K̂ᵢ φᵢ = Ĵᵢ φᵢ ⟹ (Ĵᵢ − K̂ᵢ)φᵢ = 0 for same-spin pair

2. STO-nG Basis Sets NEW

2.1 Slater-Type Orbitals (STOs)

A hydrogenic 1s STO with exponent ζ (zeta) is:

χ_STO(r) = N · r^(n-1) · exp(−ζ r) · Yₗᵐ(θ,φ) N = (2ζ)^(n+½) / √(2n)! (normalisation)

2.2 STO-nG: Fit with n Gaussians

Pople's STO-nG scheme approximates each STO by a fixed linear combination of n GTOs:

χ_STO(r) ≈ Σₖ₌₁ⁿ dₖ · g(αₖ, r) [STO-nG approximation] g(αₖ, r) = (2αₖ/π)^(3/4) · exp(−αₖ r²) (normalised 1s GTO) STO-2G: n=2 ~ fast, rough (error ~5%) STO-3G: n=3 ~ standard chemistry basis (error ~1–2%) STO-6G: n=6 ~ high accuracy (error ~0.1%)

2.3 Two-Electron Repulsion Integrals (ERIs)

(μν|κλ) = 2(α_μ+α_ν)^(1/2)(α_κ+α_λ)^(1/2) / [π(α_μ+α_ν+α_κ+α_λ)^(1/2)] × S_μν · S_κλ · F₀(t) F₀(t) = (π/4t)^(1/2) erf(√t) [Boys function]

3. Exchange Interaction IMPROVED

3.1 Exact Fock Exchange via Slater Y⁰ Integrals

Y⁰(ab; r) = (1/r) ∫₀ʳ Pₐ(r') P_b(r') dr' + ∫ᵣ^∞ Pₐ(r') P_b(r') / r' dr' V_x[a](r) = −Σ_{b≠a, ℓ_b=ℓ_a} (w_b/2) · Y⁰(ab;r) · P_b(r) / Pₐ(r)

3.2 Correction to Eigenvalues from Exchange

Δε_x / ε_HF ≈ −(1/2)(w−1)/w for an orbital with occupancy w For 1s² : ~10–15% correction to bare Hartree For 2p⁶ : ~3–6% (larger r, weaker exchange)

4. Correlation Energy & CI Correction NEW

4.1 The Correlation Energy Problem

E_corr = E_exact − E_HF < 0 (always negative; HF is an upper bound)

4.2 Configuration Interaction (CI)

|Ψ_CI⟩ = c₀|Φ₀⟩ + Σᵢₐ cᵢᵃ|Φᵢᵃ⟩ + Σᵢⱼᵃᵇ cᵢⱼᵃᵇ|Φᵢⱼᵃᵇ⟩ + … Φ₀ = HF reference (single determinant) Φᵢᵃ = singly excited: electron moved from occupied i → virtual a Φᵢⱼᵃᵇ = doubly excited: i,j → a,b

4.3 2nd-Order Perturbation Estimate (MP2 analogue)

E_corr⁽²⁾ = −Σᵢ₍occ₎ Σₐ₍virt₎ |⟨Φᵢᵃ|V_12|Φ₀⟩|² / (εₐ − εᵢ) where (ij||ab) = (ij|ab) − (ij|ba) [antisymmetrised ERI]

5. SCF Convergence & Mixing

5.1 Linear Mixing (damped SCF)

ρ_new = (1−η)·ρ_old + η·ρ_computed η = 0.45 (damping factor)

True DIIS (Pulay, 1980) minimises ‖e_n‖ across m stored iterations — recovers ~10× faster convergence for heavy atoms.

5.2 Convergence Criterion

Converged when: max_i |εᵢ^(k+1) − εᵢ^(k)| / |εᵢ^(k)| < 10⁻⁷ Ha

6. Unit System & Conversion

All quantities are computed in atomic units. The table above provides a toggle to display all energy columns in either Ha or eV. Conversion: 1 Ha = 27.21138 eV.

Conversion: 1 Ha = 27.21138 eV = 627.509 kcal/mol = 2625.5 kJ/mol 1 eV = 0.036749 Ha = 96.485 kJ/mol 1 a₀ = 0.52918 Å = 0.52918 × 10⁻¹⁰ m
Why Ha in quantum chemistry? In atomic units the Schrödinger equation for hydrogen reads −½∇²ψ − (1/r)ψ = Eψ without any physical constants. The ground-state energy is exactly −0.5 Ha = −13.606 eV. Computed orbital energies are exact multiples of Ha, making errors easy to read — e.g., an error of 0.02 Ha = 0.54 eV.
