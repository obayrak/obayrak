What is Stopping Power?

When a charged particle (projectile) moves through a medium, it continuously transfers energy to that medium via electromagnetic interactions. The stopping power S is the energy lost per unit path length:

S(x) = −dE_kinetic/dx  [energy / length]

Here we model the medium as a system of concentric charged shells. The projectile with charge zₚ experiences the Coulomb force from all enclosed charges via Gauss's Law. As it approaches the high-charge-density core, the force grows — this is the electromagnetic analogue of the Bragg peak.

Equation of Motion

m · r̈ = F(r) = zₚ · E(r)    (radial)

The electric field by region (positive = outward):

r < R₁: E = k·q₁·r/R₁³ (linear inside core)
R₁≤r<R₂: E = k·q₁/r²
R₂≤r<R₃: E = k(q₁+q₂)/r²
r≥R₃: E = k(q₁+q₂+q₃)/r²

Stopping Power from Coulomb Interaction

The local stopping power at radius r is the projection of the Coulomb force onto the direction of motion. For radial inward motion:

S(r) = −dKE/dr = −F(r)·v̂ = −zₚ·E(r)   (inward convention)

When the projectile moves inward and E·zₚ > 0 (repulsive force), the particle is decelerated — positive stopping power, energy deposited. When E·zₚ < 0 (attractive force), the particle accelerates — negative stopping power (energy gain from field).

Bragg Curve and Bragg Peak

The Bragg curve plots deposited energy dE/dx against penetration depth x = r₀ − r. The Bragg peak is the sharp maximum near the end of the range, where the projectile has slowed enough that the Coulomb interaction time is maximised. In real matter it arises from the v⁻² dependence of the Bethe–Bloch formula; here it is replicated by the rising Coulomb field as the projectile approaches the dense charged core.

Bethe–Bloch (classical): S ∝ z²/(v²) · ln(…)

This simulation captures the same qualitative behaviour: stopping power rises steeply as KE → 0 and r → 0.

Range

The range R is the total path length until KE = 0. From the work-energy theorem:

KE₀ = ∫₀^R S(x) dx → R = ∫₀^KE₀ dE/S(E)

In the simulation R is computed numerically as the depth at which the particle stops (velocity reverses or KE = 0).

Reading the Simulation

  • Projectile colour — hot red = high KE, cool blue = low KE. The glow shrinks as the particle loses energy.
  • Yellow/orange arrow — net Coulomb force F = zₚ·E(r). Points outward (repulsion) when slowing the particle.
  • Gradient trail — colour along the path encodes the KE at each point: bright = energetic entry, dim = near-range limit.
  • KE/PE bar — bottom bar shows the energy partition: red = kinetic, violet = potential. Total width is conserved when the interaction is purely conservative.
  • Energy chart (top-left) — KE(r) decreases inward; PE = zₚ·V(r) shows the potential energy landscape. Their sum = total mechanical energy (conserved).
  • Stopping power chart (top-right) — S(r) = −dKE/dr. Positive = particle losing KE; negative = particle gaining KE from attractive field.
  • Bragg curve (bottom, wide) — dE/dx vs penetration depth x = r₀ − r. The Bragg peak (maximum energy deposition) appears as a sharp spike near the end of range.
  • Scrub slider — pause and drag to inspect any radius manually.

Suggested Experiments

  • Increase q₁ with a positive zₚ — stronger repulsion, Bragg peak moves outward (shorter range).
  • Set q₁ < 0 with zₚ > 0 — attractive field accelerates the particle inward (negative stopping power region).
  • Increase E₀ — particle penetrates deeper, range increases, Bragg peak shifts inward.
  • Set q₁+q₂+q₃ = 0 — no external field; particle enters freely, all stopping happens in the inner regions.
