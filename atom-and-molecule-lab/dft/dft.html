Density Functional Theory — Overview

Explore the Hohenberg–Kohn theorems and Kohn–Sham formalism. DFT maps the many-body problem onto an effective single-particle system governed by the electron density ρ(r).

⬡ HK Theorem I

The external potential Vext(r) is determined uniquely (up to a constant) by the ground-state electron density ρ₀(r).

This means all ground-state observables are functionals of the density alone.

⬡ HK Theorem II

The functional E[ρ] reaches its global minimum at the true ground-state density, providing a variational principle for DFT.

The total energy satisfies E[ρ] ≥ E₀ for all trial densities with the correct particle number.

⬡ Kohn–Sham Equations

KS Single-particle Equation
[ -½∇² + VKS[ρ](r) ] ψᵢ(r) = εᵢ ψᵢ(r)
Each orbital ψᵢ satisfies a Schrödinger-like equation with an effective local potential VKS.
Effective KS Potential
VKS[ρ](r) = Vext(r) + VH[ρ](r) + VXC[ρ](r)
Sum of external (nuclear), Hartree (classical Coulomb), and exchange-correlation potentials.
Electron Density (from KS orbitals)
ρ(r) = Σᵢ fᵢ |ψᵢ(r)|²
Density reconstructed from occupied orbitals with occupation numbers fᵢ ∈ {0,1,2}.
Total KS Energy
E[ρ] = TS[ρ] + Eext[ρ] + EH[ρ] + EXC[ρ]
Kinetic energy of KS system + external field + Hartree + XC.

⬡ XC Approximations

LDA — Local Density Approximation
EXCLDA[ρ] = ∫ ρ(r) εXC(ρ(r)) d³r
Uses only the local density value; exact for uniform electron gas.
GGA — Generalized Gradient Approximation
EXCGGA[ρ] = ∫ f(ρ(r), ∇ρ(r)) d³r
Includes density gradient ∇ρ for improved accuracy in inhomogeneous systems.
LDA Exchange (Dirac)
εXLDA(ρ) = -¾(3/π)^(1/3) ρ^(1/3)

⬡ 1D Soft-Coulomb Equations

Nuclear potential (soft Coulomb, a = 0.5 a₀)
V_ext(x) = −Z/√((x+R/2)²+a²) − Z/√((x−R/2)²+a²)
Softening removes the 1/r singularity; standard for 1D model systems.
1D Hartree via soft-Coulomb convolution
V_H(x) = ∫ρ(x')/√((x−x')²+a²) dx'
Total energy (2 electrons, 1 KS orbital)
E = 2ε₁ − E_H[ρ] + E_XC[ρ] − ∫V_XC ρ dx + Z²/√(R²+a²)
Double-counting corrections restore the true total energy from KS eigenvalues.
FD Hamiltonian (second-order kinetic, Dirichlet BC)
H_{i,i} = 1/h² + V_KS(xᵢ), H_{i,i±1} = −1/(2h²)

Pulay/DIIS mixing is more stable than simple linear mixing for difficult systems. Energy tolerance of 10⁻⁸ Hartree is standard for production calculations.
