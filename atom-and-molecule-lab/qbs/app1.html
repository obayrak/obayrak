Physical Background

The bound state problem, potentials, and numerical methods implemented in this solver.

1. Radial Schrödinger Equation

For a central potential V(r), the full wavefunction separates as ψ(r,θ,φ) = R(r)Ylm(θ,φ). Substituting the reduced radial function u(r) = r R(r) gives the 1D equation:

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}\frac{d^2 u}{dr^2} + V_{\rm eff}(r)\, u(r) = E\, u(r)$$

Boundary conditions: u(0) = 0 (regularity at origin), u(r) → 0 as r → ∞ (bound state). This can be rewritten as:

$$\frac{d^2 u}{dr^2} = -k^2(r)\, u(r), \qquad k^2(r) = \frac{2\mu}{\hbar^2}\bigl[E - V_{\rm eff}(r)\bigr]$$

2. Nuclear Potential Forms

Seven nuclear potential forms are available. All share the parameters V₀ (depth), rᵥ (radius parameter, used as R = rᵥ·CC where CC = AT1/3 + AP1/3), and a (shape/range parameter).

2a. Woods-Saxon

A smoothed square well with diffuse surface, the standard nuclear optical model potential:

$$V_{\rm WS}(r) = -\frac{V_0}{1 + e^{(r-R)/a}}, \quad R = r_v \cdot \mathrm{CC}$$

2b. Square Well (Finite)

A sharp-edged potential well. Exactly solvable analytically (spherical Bessel functions), useful for benchmarking:

$$V_{\rm SW}(r) = \begin{cases} -V_0 & r \le R \\ 0 & r > R \end{cases}$$

2c. Harmonic Oscillator

The nuclear shell model potential. Here V₀ is the depth at the origin, and R = rᵥ·CC is the radius where the potential crosses zero:

$$V_{\rm HO}(r) = V_0\!\left(\frac{r^2}{R^2} - 1\right)$$

This corresponds to ½μω²r² − V₀ with ω² = 2V₀/(μR²).

2d. Morse Potential

Models diatomic molecule vibrations. V₀ = dissociation energy De, R = re·CC is the equilibrium distance, and a = 1/α is the inverse range parameter:

$$V_{\rm Morse}(r) = V_0\!\left[e^{-2(r-R)/a} - 2\,e^{-(r-R)/a}\right]$$

The minimum value is −V₀ at r = R. Analytically solvable: energies are En = −V₀ + ℏω(n+½) − [ℏω(n+½)]²/(4V₀).

2e. Gaussian Potential

A peaked well centered at R = rᵥ·CC with width a. Often used as a two-body nuclear force model:

$$V_{\rm G}(r) = -V_0\, e^{-(r-R)^2/a^2}$$

Set rᵥ = 0 (so R = 0) for a well centered at the origin, which is the most common nuclear use.

2f. Yukawa Potential

The original nuclear force potential from Yukawa's meson exchange theory. Range parameter a = ℏ/(mπc) ≈ 1.4 fm:

$$V_{\rm Y}(r) = -V_0\,\frac{a\,e^{-r/a}}{r}$$

Note: diverges as r → 0. Best used with ℓ ≥ 1 (centrifugal barrier regularizes the singularity) or with short-range repulsion. A core cutoff of r = 0.05a is applied internally.

2g. Pöschl-Teller Potential

An exactly solvable potential with a symmetric well centered at R. Bound state energies are known analytically:

$$V_{\rm PT}(r) = -\frac{V_0}{\cosh^2\!\left(\dfrac{r-R}{a}\right)}$$

For a well centered at r = 0 (set rᵥ = 0), the n-th bound state energy is En = −ℏ²/(2μa²)·(λ−n)² where λ = ½(−1 + √(1 + 8μa²V₀/ℏ²)).

3. Effective Potential (full)

$$V_{\rm eff}(r) = V_{\rm nuc}(r) + V_{\rm C}(r) + V_\ell(r) + V_{\rm SO}(r)$$

3a. Coulomb Potential (Uniformly Charged Sphere)

$$V_{\rm C}(r) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{Z_P Z_T e^2}{2R_C}\left(3 - \dfrac{r^2}{R_C^2}\right) & r \le R_C \\[8pt] \dfrac{Z_P Z_T e^2}{r} & r > R_C \end{cases}$$

3b. Centrifugal Barrier

$$V_\ell(r) = \frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2}$$

3c. Spin-Orbit Potential (Thomas term)

$$V_{\rm SO}(r) = -\left(\frac{\hbar}{m_\pi c}\right)^2 \langle \ell \cdot s \rangle \cdot V_{\rm so} \cdot \frac{e^{(r-R)/a_{\rm so}}}{(1+e^{(r-R)/a_{\rm so}})^2 \cdot a_{\rm so} \cdot r}$$

4. Numerov Method (4th-order)

$$u_{n+1} = \frac{2u_n\!\left(1 - \tfrac{5}{12}h^2 k_n^2\right) - u_{n-1}\!\left(1 + \tfrac{1}{12}h^2 k_{n-1}^2\right)}{1 + \tfrac{1}{12}h^2 k_{n+1}^2}$$

5. Finite Difference Method (2nd-order)

$$u_{n+1} = 2u_n - u_{n-1} - h^2 k_n^2\, u_n$$

6. Energy Search by Node Counting

Binary search over E (and optionally V₀, rᵥ, a). The nr-th eigenstate has exactly nr nodes. If count > nr: E too high → decrease E_max. If count ≤ nr: E too low → increase E_min.

Yukawa Notes

Singularity at r=0 The Yukawa potential V ~ −V₀a/r diverges at the origin. A core cutoff at r = 0.05a is applied internally. For ℓ ≥ 1, the centrifugal barrier naturally regularizes the behavior. For ℓ = 0, increase r_max and use a smaller step h to verify convergence. The Yukawa range in nuclear physics is a ≈ 1.4 fm (pion Compton wavelength).
