The Wave Equation

The wave equation is a second-order linear hyperbolic PDE governing the propagation of disturbances through a medium at finite speed c. It appears in acoustics, elasticity, electromagnetism, seismology, and quantum mechanics.

∂²u/∂t² = c² ∇²u

u is the field variable (displacement, pressure, field strength), c is the phase velocity, and ∇² is the Laplace operator. In each dimension:

1D
∂²u/∂t² = c² ∂²u/∂x²
2D
∂²u/∂t² = c²(∂²u/∂x² + ∂²u/∂y²)
3D
∂²u/∂t² = c²(∂²u/∂x² + ∂²u/∂y² + ∂²u/∂z²)

With viscous damping (dissipative medium), the equation becomes:

∂²u/∂t² + 2γ ∂u/∂t = c² ∇²u

where γ is the damping coefficient. The general solution is a superposition of plane waves: u = A·exp[i(k·r − ωt)], with dispersion relation ω² = c²k² (in the undamped case).

Finite Difference Time-Domain (FDTD) Method

FDTD uses second-order central differences for both time and space derivatives, achieving O(Δt²,Δx²) accuracy. Discretising: xᵢ = i·Δx, tⁿ = n·Δt, uᵢⁿ ≈ u(xᵢ,tⁿ).

∂²u/∂t² ≈ (uᵢⁿ⁺¹ − 2uᵢⁿ + uᵢⁿ⁻¹) / Δt²
∂²u/∂x² ≈ (uᵢ₊₁ⁿ − 2uᵢⁿ + uᵢ₋₁ⁿ) / Δx²

Combining (r = cΔt/Δx = Courant number), the explicit update stencil:

1D stencil: uᵢⁿ⁺¹ = (2−2γ)uᵢⁿ − (1−2γ)uᵢⁿ⁻¹ + r²(uᵢ₊₁ⁿ − 2uᵢⁿ + uᵢ₋₁ⁿ)
2D stencil (5-point): uᵢⱼⁿ⁺¹ = (2−2γ)uᵢⱼⁿ − (1−2γ)uᵢⱼⁿ⁻¹ + r²(uᵢ₊₁ⱼ + uᵢ₋₁ⱼ + uᵢⱼ₊₁ + uᵢⱼ₋₁ − 4uᵢⱼ)ⁿ
3D stencil (7-point): uᵢⱼₖⁿ⁺¹ = (2−2γ)uᵢⱼₖⁿ − (1−2γ)uᵢⱼₖⁿ⁻¹ + r²(Σ±1-neighbors − 6uᵢⱼₖ)ⁿ

Explicit schemes require only values from the two previous time levels — no matrix inversion needed. Each time step costs O(N^d) operations for d-dimensional N-point grid.

CFL Stability Analysis

The Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy (CFL) condition is a necessary stability requirement for explicit time-stepping schemes. It states that the numerical domain of dependence must contain the physical domain of dependence.

1D
r = cΔt/Δx ≤ 1
2D
r·√2 ≤ 1
r ≤ 1/√2 ≈ 0.707
3D
r·√3 ≤ 1
r ≤ 1/√3 ≈ 0.577

Violation causes exponential growth of round-off errors (numerical instability). This simulator uses adaptive Δt: dt = CFL_target · dx / (c · √d), keeping the simulation always stable regardless of wave speed setting.

Von Neumann stability analysis: substitute uᵢⁿ = ξⁿ·exp(ikᵢΔx) and require |ξ| ≤ 1 (amplification factor). This yields exactly the CFL condition above.

Energy and Conservation Laws

Total wave energy (kinetic + potential) for a 1D string:

E(t) = ½ ∫₀ᴸ [ ρ(∂u/∂t)² + T(∂u/∂x)² ] dx

For lossless media (γ=0) with Dirichlet or Neumann BCs, dE/dt = 0 — energy is conserved. The discrete FDTD scheme preserves a modified energy exactly when r=1 in 1D, providing long-time stability.

With damping: dE/dt = −2γ ∫ρ(∂u/∂t)² dx ≤ 0. Energy decays exponentially at rate 2γ for all modes. The simulator tracks discrete energy Eⁿ = Δx Σᵢ [(uᵢⁿ⁺¹−uᵢⁿ)²/Δt² + c²(uᵢ₊₁ⁿ−uᵢⁿ)²/Δx²].
