The Physics of the Fourier Transform
From time domain to frequency domain — the mathematics behind signal decomposition.
Core Idea
Any periodic or finite-energy signal can be expressed as a weighted sum of sinusoids. The Fourier Transform projects a signal onto a basis of complex exponentials, revealing which frequencies are present and at what amplitude and phase.
Continuous Fourier Transform
For a continuous signal x(t), the Fourier Transform X(f) is defined as the integral below. The complex exponential e−j2πft acts as a matched filter — when the signal contains frequency f, the integral accumulates; all other frequencies cancel by orthogonality.
Forward Transform
$$ X(f)= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)\,e^{-j2\pi ft}\,dt $$
The inverse transform reconstructs the original signal from its spectrum:
Inverse Transform
$$ x(t)= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} X(f)\,e^{j2\pi ft}\,df $$
Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT)
In digital systems we work with N equally-spaced samples. The DFT maps them to N complex frequency bins. Each bin X[k] gives the amplitude and phase at frequency k·Fs/N:
DFT Analysis Equation
$$ X[k]= \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n]e^{-j2\pi kn/N} $$
Only the first N/2 bins are unique for real signals (Hermitian symmetry). |X[k]| = amplitude, ∠X[k] = phase at that frequency.
Frequency Resolution
Δf = Fs / N. More samples or lower sample rate → finer frequency bins. Here N = 2048.
Nyquist Theorem
Max representable frequency = Fs / 2. Signals above this alias. Always keep Fs > 2 × fmax.
Parseval's Theorem
Σ|x[n]|² = (1/N)·Σ|X[k]|². Energy is conserved between time and frequency domains.
Linearity
FFT{ax + by} = a·FFT{x} + b·FFT{y}. Sum of sinusoids → sum of their individual spectra.
Fast Fourier Transform (FFT)
The Cooley-Tukey radix-2 algorithm reduces DFT complexity from O(N²) to O(N log₂N) by recursively splitting even and odd indices. The core operation is the butterfly:
Butterfly — Decimation in Time
$$ X[k]=E[k]+W_N^kO[k] $$
DFT Complexity
O(N²) N=2048 → 4,194,304 ops
FFT Complexity
O(N log₂ N) N=2048 → 22,528 ops · 186× faster
Window Functions
Spectral leakage occurs when a frequency doesn't land exactly on a bin. Window functions taper the signal edges to reduce leakage. The Hann window is:
Hann Window
$$ w[n]= 0.5 \left( 1-\cos \frac{2\pi n}{N-1} \right) $$

Fourier Series of Common Waveforms
Square Wave Fourier Series
$$ x_{\mathrm{sq}}(t)= \frac{4}{\pi} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{ \sin\left((2k+1)2\pi f_0t\right) }{2k+1} $$
Sawtooth Wave Fourier Series
$$ x_{\mathrm{saw}}(t)= \frac{2}{\pi} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n} \sin(2\pi nf_0t) $$
Square Wave
Odd harmonics: f, 3f, 5f… at amplitudes 1, 1/3, 1/5… Spectrum falls at −6 dB/octave.
Sawtooth Wave
All harmonics: f, 2f, 3f… at amplitudes 1, 1/2, 1/3… Rich harmonic content.
Triangle Wave
Odd harmonics only, amplitudes fall as 1/n². Smoother rolloff at −12 dB/oct.
AM Signal
Carrier plus two sidebands at fc ± fm. Demonstrates spectral shifting by modulation.
Applications
Audio / Music
Pitch detection, equalizers, noise reduction, MP3 (MDCT), speech synthesis.
Communications
OFDM in WiFi/LTE/5G — IFFT at transmitter, FFT at receiver. Each subcarrier is one bin.
Image Processing
2D FFT for filtering, JPEG compression (DCT), edge detection, fast convolution.
Medical Imaging
MRI k-space is the 2D Fourier transform of the image. CT uses filtered back-projection.
User Manual
How to use the FFT Simulator to explore signal decomposition.
Getting Started
  1. Open the Simulation tab. A Square Wave preset loads automatically with 4 harmonic components.
  2. The top canvas is the time-domain view. Faded colored lines are individual components; bright white is their sum.
  3. The bottom canvas is the FFT spectrum. Colored dots mark each component's frequency peak.
  4. Press Play in the header to animate an oscilloscope-style scanning playhead.
Play Controls
  1. The Play / Pause button starts and stops the animated playhead sweeping across the time domain.
  2. The playhead reveals the signal left-to-right, as on a real oscilloscope. The FFT updates live based on signal captured so far.
  3. Watch the FFT peaks build up as the playhead captures more complete cycles of each frequency.
  4. Use the Speed buttons (0.5×, 1×, 2×, 4×) to control how fast the playhead moves.

Guided Experiments
  1. Spectral Leakage: Load Pure Sine at 10 Hz with Rectangular window. Move freq to 10.5 Hz — the peak smears. Switch to Hann or Blackman to suppress it.
  2. Phase Cancellation: Add two same-frequency components with Phase at 0° and 180° and equal Amp. The composite signal cancels to zero.
  3. Aliasing: Set Sample Rate to 256 Hz. Add a component above 128 Hz. Its FFT peak appears at the wrong — aliased — frequency.
  4. Live FFT: Press Play and watch the spectrum build as the playhead scans. Peaks grow and stabilize once a full cycle is captured.
