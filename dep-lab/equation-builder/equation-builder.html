Foundational Identity

The Universal Conservation Equation

Almost every conservation law in physics, chemistry, biology, and economics is a dialect of the same sentence: the rate of change of a quantity equals what flows in, minus what flows out, plus what is created internally, minus what is destroyed internally. Mass balances, energy balances, population dynamics, chemical kinetics, electrical charge, and even household finances all reduce to bookkeeping against this one identity.

The Transport Equation

Where the Universal Conservation Equation bookkeeps a single well-mixed amount, the Transport Equation bookkeeps a density spread continuously through space. It is the same statement written in the language of fields: the rate a quantity accumulates at a point, plus the net rate it is carried away from that point by its own flux, equals whatever is created there minus whatever is destroyed there. Fluid density, heat, chemical species, population density, traffic flow, and even option prices in finance are all governed by a member of this same equation family.

+ = Source − Sink
∂u/∂t

Transport
Net rate the quantity is carried away from that point by its own flow — advection, diffusion, conduction, or any combination.

Local Generation
Created at that point without needing to flow in: chemical reaction, radioactive production, heating.
Sink
Local Destruction
Destroyed at that point: absorption, decay, consumption, cooling.
Reading it against the Universal Equation: integrating the transport equation over a fixed volume and applying the divergence theorem turns ∇·(Flux) into exactly the Input − Output crossing that volume's boundary, while Source − Sink becomes Generation − Consumption inside it — the two master equations are the same conservation statement, one written for a lumped total, the other for a continuous field.

The Principle of Stationary Action

Every equation built so far in this lab came from asking "what enters, what leaves, what is created, what is destroyed?" — the accumulation method. There is a second, entirely different way to arrive at the same physics: instead of bookkeeping flows, ask which path through time makes a single number — the action S — stationary. Newton's laws, Maxwell's equations, and the Schrödinger equation all fall out of exactly one requirement, δS = 0, applied to a different dynamical variable each time.

The Principle of Maximum Entropy

The Universal, Transport, and Variational Equations all ask a version of the same question about a single, definite trajectory: what enters, what leaves, or what path is stationary? The Statistical Equation asks a different kind of question entirely: given everything we know about a system — and nothing more — what is the single most probable distribution of states it can be in? Maximizing entropy subject to whatever constraints are actually known, rather than assumed, is what turns a vague notion of "disorder" into a precise probability distribution — and that distribution turns out to be exactly the Boltzmann factor already sitting inside every relaxation equation in this lab.

Reading it against the other three tabs: the Universal and Transport Equations bookkeep a definite quantity as it moves; the Variational Equation finds the one path a system actually follows out of infinitely many possible paths; the Statistical Equation finds the one probability distribution over states that best represents ignorance of everything except a few known constraints. All four are optimization or bookkeeping principles for building equations rather than equations to be independently memorized — and the Boltzmann factor produced here is exactly the same e^(−t/τ)-style relaxation that drives the Water Tank, Cooling Coffee, and Carbon in Atmosphere modules, just derived from probability instead of flow.
