01 — Deriving the Polynomials

Solutions of Laguerre's Equation

Where the equation comes from, how the Frobenius method forces a polynomial out of an infinite series, and the two closed-form recipes — Rodrigues' formula and the generating function — that build Ln(x) directly.

The Equation

Laguerre's differential equation in its plain form is

Laguerre's equation (α = 0)
$$ x\,y'' + (1-x)\,y' + n\,y = 0 ,\qquad x \in [0,\infty) $$

It generalizes to the associated (or generalized) Laguerre equation by introducing a real parameter α > −1, which shifts the singular behaviour at the origin:

Associated Laguerre equation
$$ x\,y'' + (\alpha+1-x)\,y' + n\,y = 0 $$

Both have a regular singular point at x = 0 and an irregular singular point at x = ∞. Physically, this single equation shows up whenever a radial Schrödinger-type problem is reduced by stripping off the correct asymptotic behaviour at both ends — most famously, it is exactly what's left of the hydrogen atom's radial equation once you factor out e-r and rl (Tab 4).

Frobenius Series & the Termination Trick

Since x = 0 is a regular singular point, we try a Frobenius series $y = x^{s}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} c_k x^k$. Plugging in and matching the indicial equation forces s = 0 for the solution regular at the origin (the second, singular solution is discarded on physical grounds — see the box below). Substituting the plain series $y=\sum c_k x^k$ into the associated equation gives a two-term recursion:

Recursion for series coefficients
$$ c_{k+1} = \frac{k-n}{(k+1)(k+\alpha+1)}\,c_k $$

This is the entire story. Unless n is a non-negative integer, the ratio $c_{k+1}/c_k \to 1/k$ as $k\to\infty$, which is exactly the behaviour of $e^{x}$ — the series converges but blows up exponentially at large x, giving a physically unacceptable, non-normalizable function. If n = 0, 1, 2, … the numerator $(k-n)$ vanishes at $k=n$ and every subsequent coefficient is forced to zero: the infinite series terminates into a polynomial of exact degree n. This termination condition is precisely what quantizes the principal quantum number in the hydrogen atom.

⚠
The second, linearly independent solution to Laguerre's equation (built from the s ≠ 0 root or via reduction of order) diverges logarithmically at x = 0 and is called the Laguerre function of the second kind. It is mathematically valid but almost never physical, since it isn't square-integrable against the natural weight e−x. This page, like its Bessel and Legendre companions, only tracks the regular solution.

Two Closed-Form Constructions

Rodrigues' formula
$$ L_n^{(\alpha)}(x) = \frac{x^{-\alpha}e^{x}}{n!}\,\frac{d^n}{dx^n}\!\left(e^{-x}x^{n+\alpha}\right) $$
Generating function
$$ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} L_n^{(\alpha)}(x)\,t^n = \frac{1}{(1-t)^{\alpha+1}}\exp\!\left(\frac{-xt}{1-t}\right),\quad |t|<1 $$

Rodrigues' formula differentiates a simple product n times to manufacture the polynomial directly — the same mechanism used for Legendre and Hermite polynomials. The generating function packages every $L_n^{(\alpha)}$ at once as Taylor coefficients in t; differentiating or integrating the generating function in x or t is how most recurrence relations in Tab 2 are derived without touching the ODE again.

Explicit Series Form

Closed-form polynomial
$$ L_n^{(\alpha)}(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} (-1)^k \binom{n+\alpha}{n-k}\frac{x^k}{k!} $$

Setting α = 0 recovers the plain Laguerre polynomials Ln(x) that solve the original equation. The table below is generated live from this formula.

02 — Identities & Behaviour

Properties of Laguerre Polynomials

Recurrence relations, derivative identities, special values, and what happens to Ln(α)(x) as n grows — each one checked live against direct evaluation.

Recurrence Relations

Three-term recurrence (degree n+1 from n, n−1)
$$ (n+1)L_{n+1}^{(\alpha)}(x) = (2n+\alpha+1-x)L_n^{(\alpha)}(x) - (n+\alpha)L_{n-1}^{(\alpha)}(x) $$
Order-shift relation
$$ L_n^{(\alpha)}(x) = L_n^{(\alpha+1)}(x) - L_{n-1}^{(\alpha+1)}(x) $$
Derivative identity
$$ \frac{d}{dx}L_n^{(\alpha)}(x) = -L_{n-1}^{(\alpha+1)}(x) $$
x times the derivative
$$ x\frac{d}{dx}L_n^{(\alpha)}(x) = nL_n^{(\alpha)}(x) - (n+\alpha)L_{n-1}^{(\alpha)}(x) $$

The three-term recurrence is how every numerical routine on this page actually evaluates $L_n^{(\alpha)}$ — climbing up from $L_0=1$ and $L_1=1+\alpha-x$ is far more stable than expanding the explicit sum of binomial coefficients for large n, since the explicit form involves large cancelling terms.

Asymptotic Behaviour

For fixed x and n → ∞, the Plancherel–Rotach asymptotics show $L_n^{(\alpha)}(x)$ oscillates with a slowly varying envelope that grows like $n^{\alpha/2-1/4}$, with the oscillation frequency increasing near $x=0$ and the polynomial decaying smoothly for $x$ beyond roughly $4n$. This is the same qualitative story as Bessel functions at large order: a polynomial of degree n has exactly n real, positive, simple roots, all packed inside $(0, \approx 4n{+}2\alpha)$, increasingly dense near the origin.

ℹ
Those n positive roots are not a curiosity — they are exactly the abscissas used in Gauss–Laguerre quadrature, the optimal way to numerically integrate $\int_0^\infty f(x)e^{-x}dx$. See Tab 3 for the connection to orthogonality.
03 — Inner Products on [0, ∞)

Orthogonality & Completeness

Laguerre polynomials are orthogonal not in the ordinary sense, but against the exponential weight that comes baked into the differential equation itself — the same weight that makes the hydrogen radial wavefunctions normalizable.

The Orthogonality Relation

Weighted inner product
$$ \int_0^{\infty} x^{\alpha}e^{-x}\,L_n^{(\alpha)}(x)\,L_m^{(\alpha)}(x)\,dx = \frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}{n!}\,\delta_{nm} $$

The weight $w(x) = x^{\alpha}e^{-x}$ is exactly what turns the self-adjoint (Sturm–Liouville) form of the associated Laguerre equation into a proper eigenvalue problem: writing the equation as $\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(x^{\alpha+1}e^{-x}y'\right) = -n\,x^{\alpha}e^{-x}y$ makes the weight and the orthogonality interval ([0,∞)) fall straight out of Sturm–Liouville theory, with eigenvalues n = 0,1,2,…

Gauss–Laguerre Quadrature

The n roots $x_i$ of $L_n(x)$ give the optimal nodes for integrating against the natural weight: $\int_0^\infty f(x)e^{-x}dx \approx \sum_{i=1}^n w_i f(x_i)$, exact for any polynomial f of degree up to $2n-1$, with weights $w_i = \dfrac{x_i}{(n+1)^2\left[L_{n+1}(x_i)\right]^2}$.

Quadrature weight formula
$$ w_i = \frac{x_i}{(n+1)^2\,[L_{n+1}(x_i)]^2} $$

04 — Where Laguerre Lives in Physics

The Hydrogen Atom Radial Equation

Separating the Schrödinger equation in spherical coordinates leaves a radial ODE that, after stripping off the correct boundary behaviour at r→0 and r→∞, becomes exactly the associated Laguerre equation.

From Schrödinger to Laguerre

For a Coulomb potential $V(r) = -e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0 r$, separating $\psi(r,\theta,\phi) = R(r)Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)$ leaves the radial equation

Radial Schrödinger equation
$$ -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r^2\frac{dR}{dr}\right) + \left[\frac{\hbar^2 l(l+1)}{2mr^2} - \frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r}\right]R = E\,R $$

Substituting $\rho = 2r/(na_0)$ (with $a_0$ the Bohr radius) and factoring out the two pieces of asymptotic behaviour the wavefunction must have — $e^{-\rho/2}$ so it decays at infinity, and $\rho^{l}$ so it stays finite at the origin against the centrifugal barrier — via $R(\rho) = e^{-\rho/2}\rho^{l}v(\rho)$ turns the equation for $v(\rho)$ into exactly the associated Laguerre equation with $\alpha = 2l+1$ and degree $n-l-1$. Termination of that series (Tab 1) is precisely the condition that quantizes the energy.

Radial wavefunction
$$ R_{nl}(r) = N_{nl}\,\left(\frac{2r}{na_0}\right)^{l} e^{-r/na_0}\, L_{n-l-1}^{(2l+1)}\!\!\left(\frac{2r}{na_0}\right) $$
Energy levels
$$ E_n = -\frac{13.6\ \text{eV}}{n^2} ,\qquad n = 1,2,3,\dots $$
⚠
On convention: there are two historical definitions of the associated Laguerre polynomial floating around physics textbooks, differing by a factor of $(n+l)!/(n-l-1)!$ (older books like Schiff differentiate $L_{n+l}$ directly; modern texts and this page use the Abramowitz & Stegun / CRC convention satisfying the ODE in Tab 1 directly). The orbital shapes are identical either way — only the bookkeeping of constants out front differs. To stay convention-proof, every $R_{nl}(r)$ plotted on this page is normalized numerically (trapezoidal quadrature on $\int_0^\infty R^2 r^2\,dr=1$) rather than trusting a particular closed-form prefactor.

Quantum Number Rules

Principal
$$ n = 1, 2, 3, \dots $$

Sets the energy and overall size; degree of the Laguerre piece is n−l−1.

Azimuthal
$$ \ell = 0, 1, \dots, n-1 $$

Sets orbital shape and angular momentum magnitude √[ℓ(ℓ+1)]ħ.

Magnetic
$$ m = -\ell, \dots, \ell $$

Sets the orientation / z-projection of angular momentum, mħ.
