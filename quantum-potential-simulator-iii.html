How the scattering quantities are computed

For each ℓ the radial equation is integrated outwards (Numerov method) beyond the range of the potential and matched to the free solutions. Far away the wave is shifted by the phase shift δℓ:

uℓ(r) → sin(kr − ℓπ/2 + δℓ), k = √(2mE)/ℏ
σ = (4π/k²) Σ (2ℓ+1) sin²δℓ, f(θ) = (1/k) Σ (2ℓ+1) eiδℓ sin δℓ Pℓ(cos θ), dσ/dΩ = |f(θ)|²

The multiple of π in δℓ is fixed by counting nodes, so δ → 0 at high energy and Levinson’s theorem δℓ(0) = Nℓπ counts the bound states. At low energy k cot δ₀ = −1/a + r₀k²/2 defines the scattering length a and the effective range r₀. A resonance appears where δℓ rises through 90°; its width is Γ = 2/(dδ/dE) and its lifetime τ = ℏ/Γ.
