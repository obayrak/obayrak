How the simulator solves the problem

Writing ψ(r,θ,φ) = u(r)/r · Yℓm(θ,φ) turns the three-dimensional Schrödinger equation into a one-dimensional radial equation:

−(ℏ²/2m) u″(r) + [ V(r) + ℏ²ℓ(ℓ+1)/(2mr²) ] u(r) = E u(r), u(0) = u(rmax) = 0

The simulator replaces u″ by a finite difference on an even grid. The equation becomes a symmetric tridiagonal matrix whose eigenvalues are the energies. They are found one by one with Sturm-sequence bisection, and each wavefunction by inverse iteration, normalised so that ∫u² dr = 1. The number of nodes of u counts the radial quantum number nr.

The semiclassical (WKB) estimate solves

∫ √( 2m[E − V(r) − ℏ²(ℓ+½)²/2mr²] ) / ℏ dr = (nr + ½) π

between the classical turning points. The Langer replacement ℓ(ℓ+1) → (ℓ+½)² makes it exact for the harmonic oscillator and the Coulomb potential.
