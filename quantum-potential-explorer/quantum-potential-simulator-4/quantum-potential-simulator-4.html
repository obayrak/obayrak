How the atom is calculated

Each electron moves in the average field of the nucleus and all other electrons. The simulator solves the Kohn–Sham equations of density-functional theory self-consistently for a spherical atom:

[ −½∇² − Z/r + VH[ρ](r) + Vxc[ρ](r) ] φnℓ = εnℓ φnℓ, ρ(r) = Σ fnℓ |φnℓ|²

VH is the electrostatic (Hartree) potential of the electron cloud and Vxc the exchange–correlation potential in the local-density approximation (Slater exchange, Perdew–Zunger correlation). The radial equations are integrated with the Numerov method on a logarithmic grid, and the potential is updated until it no longer changes (self-consistency). For neon the result agrees with the NIST LDA reference to 0.0003 hartree.

The ionisation energy is the energy difference between the ion and the atom (ΔSCF), which is much more accurate than −ε of the highest orbital. The calculation is non-relativistic and spin-averaged, so it is less accurate for heavy elements (Au, Hg, actinides) and for atoms with many unpaired spins (N, O). The ground term is predicted with Hund’s rules.
