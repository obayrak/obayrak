    3.1 Factorization and the single Darboux step (T1)

    With the nodeless ground state \(\psi_0\) at \(E_0=-(\hbar^2/2\mu)\kappa_0^2\), \(H-E_0 = A^\dagger A\), \(A = \frac{d}{dr} - \frac{\psi_0'}{\psi_0}\). The partner \(AA^\dagger\) has

    $$V^{(1)}_{\rm eff} = -V_{\rm eff} + 2E_0 + 2\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}\Big(\frac{\psi_0'}{\psi_0}\Big)^2,\qquad S^{(1)}(k) = S(k)\,\frac{\kappa_0+ik}{\kappa_0-ik}.$$

    It deletes \(E_0\), raises ν by one, and changes every phase shift by \(\arctan(k/\kappa_0)\). With Coulomb, \(\psi_0'/\psi_0\to-\kappa_0 - \eta_0/r\) with \(\eta_0 = Z_1Z_2e^2/(2\kappa_0\,\hbar^2/2\mu)\), which leaves a long-range \(-Z_1Z_2e^2/(\kappa_0 r^2)\) tail, so the simple factor is only approximate there.

    3.2 Phase-equivalent removal (T2, Baye 1987)

    Two Darboux steps at the same energy combine into

    $$\tilde V_{\rm eff} = V_{\rm eff} - 2\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}\frac{d^2}{dr^2}\ln I_b(r),\qquad I_b(r)=\int_0^r \psi_b^2\,dt,$$

    and every other solution maps to \(\tilde\varphi = \varphi - \psi_b \int_0^r\psi_b\varphi\,dt / I_b\). Because \(\psi_b\to 0\) at large r, \(\tilde\varphi\to\varphi\): the S-matrix is unchanged at every energy. Near the origin \(I_b\propto r^{2l+3}\), which adds \(2(2l+3)/r^2\) and turns ν into ν + 2. Removing all states with \(2n+L \lt G\) gives the shallow potential with the universal core \(/r^2\). In general, N removals give \((l+2N)(l+2N+1)/r^2\) (Baye's eq. 13).

    3.3 Generalized Levinson theorem and the redundant pole

    $$\delta_l(0)-\delta_l(\infty) = \Big(N_b + \frac{\nu-l}{2}\Big)\pi,\qquad \delta_l(\infty) = \frac{(l-\nu)\pi}{2}.$$

    One fewer bound state and two more units of ν leave the difference unchanged. The S-matrix keeps its pole at \(k=i\kappa_b\), but no normalizable solution sits there any more: it is a redundant (Ma) pole, produced by the \(e^{-2\kappa_b r}\) tail of \(\tilde V - V\). The "Levinson-anchored" convention on the Phase shifts tab plots δ with this offset.

    3.4 Isospectral family (T3)

    $$\tilde V_\lambda = V_{\rm eff} - 2\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}\frac{d^2}{dr^2}\ln\big[1+\lambda I_b(r)\big],\qquad \tilde\psi_b = \frac{\sqrt{1+\lambda}\,\psi_b}{1+\lambda I_b},\qquad \lambda>-1.$$

    All energies and S are unchanged; the ANC becomes \(C_b/\sqrt{1+\lambda}\). The S-matrix residue does not fix \(C_b\) here because \(\tilde V_\lambda - V\propto e^{-2\kappa_b r}\) blocks the usual residue–ANC relation. λ → ∞ reproduces T2. The slider controls \(s = \ln(1+\lambda)\in[-2.5, 2.5]\), i.e. \(\lambda\in[-0.92, 11.2]\).

    3.5 Adding a bound state (T4, Gel’fand–Levitan)

    With φ the regular solution at \(\varepsilon = -(\hbar^2/2\mu)\kappa^2\) (it grows as \(e^{\kappa r}\)) and a norming constant c > 0,

    $$\tilde V = V - 2\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}\frac{d^2}{dr^2}\ln\Big(1 + c\int_0^r\varphi^2\,dr'\Big),\qquad \tilde u = \frac{\sqrt{c}\,\varphi}{1 + c\int_0^r\varphi^2},\qquad \int_0^\infty\tilde u^2 = 1.$$

    This is the λ-family formula with the normalisable state replaced by the growing solution. Nothing is singular at the origin, so ν and l are unchanged. The Jost function gains a zero at \(k=i\kappa\): \(\tilde S = S[(k+i\kappa)/(k-i\kappa)]^2\), i.e. \(\tilde\delta = \delta + 2\arctan(\kappa/k)\). The shift is +π at threshold (one more bound state) and vanishes at high energy. Every existing level is kept.

    The app sets \(c = s/\int_0^{r_t}\varphi^2\) with \(r_t\) the outer turning point at ε, so s = 1 centres the new state near \(r_t\). For l = 0, \(\tilde V - V\) falls off as \(e^{-2\kappa r}\). For l > 0 it keeps a tail of about \(4\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}l(l+1)/(\kappa r^3)\), because \(\int_0^r\varphi^2\) carries the 1/r corrections of \(i_l(\kappa r)\). Applied to the shallow T2 partner, T4 restores a bound state at the removed energy but keeps the singular core, so the result is not phase-equivalent to the deep potential.

    4 Spin ½ on spin 0: one SUSY chain per j

    For a nucleon (or ³He, t) on a spin-0 core, \(V_{lj} = V_c + 2\langle\mathbf l\cdot\mathbf s\rangle V_{so}(r)\), with \(2\langle\mathbf l\cdot\mathbf s\rangle = l\) for \(j=l+\frac12\) and \(-(l+1)\) for \(j=l-\frac12\). Each (l, j) channel is an independent radial problem with its own \(S_{lj}\), so the transformations act per channel (T2 removes 0p₃/₂ and 0p₁/₂ separately).

    $$A(\theta) = f_C + \frac{1}{2ik}\sum_l e^{2i\sigma_l}\big[(l+1)(S_{l^+}-1) + l\,(S_{l^-}-1)\big]P_l,\qquad B(\theta) = \frac{1}{2ik}\sum_l e^{2i\sigma_l}\big(S_{l^+}-S_{l^-}\big)P_l^1,$$

    \(d\sigma/d\Omega = |A|^2+|B|^2\) and \(A_y = 2\,\mathrm{Im}(AB^*)/(|A|^2+|B|^2)\), for the amplitude \(A + iB\,\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat n\), \(\hat n\parallel\mathbf k\times\mathbf k'\) (Basel convention). With \(V_{so} = 0\) the split result equals the spin-0 result and \(A_y = 0\). Without the j split, a single j = l + (j − l) is used in every partial wave.

    7 Complex potentials (absorption)

    With \(V_N - iW\), the same formulas hold with the bilinear c-product \(\int\psi^2\) (no complex conjugation). Forbidden states acquire complex energies, found by continuation from W = 0 in four steps. S and \(\sigma_R = \frac{\pi}{k^2}\sum(2l+1)(1-|S_l|^2)\) are preserved by T2 and T3. T1 and T4 are hidden in this mode, B(E2) and radii are indicative only, and capture is not computed.

    Many removals (²¹²Po, ²³⁸U: 11)Errors accumulate to ~10⁻⁴.Raise the tolerance slider to 10⁻³ for these presets.
    Weakly bound or halo statesThe tail must fit inside rmax, and the ANC is read at 20 fm.Use rmax ≥ 10/κ (⁸B uses 100 fm).
    Low energies under a high barrierS is not finite: those energies are dropped. Capture integrals underflow when 2πη reaches several hundred.Increase Emin; the capture tab proposes Emin at 2πη = 150.
    T1 with CoulombLong-range \(1/r^2\) tail: S approximate, and resonance poles shift.Use T1 only as a contrast.
    T4 with l > 0\(r^{-3}\) tail: S at rmax only approaches the exact rule.Raise rmax; the rule row is shown for l = 0.
    T4 with CoulombThe S factor is not the simple square.Read S and δ directly.
    Square wellPartner potentials have spikes at R.Expected; S is still preserved.
    Pauli rule for nucleonsOscillator counting ignores j and partial filling.Set G and analysed L by hand, or use the j-split presets.
    ε or T3 target not boundThe transformation is skipped, or falls back to an existing bound state.Check the T3 menu and the notes.
    Spin ½ without the j splitOne j per l only; there is no Ay.Tick the j-split box.
    Absorption onT1/T4 hidden; B(E2), radii indicative; no capture.Set Wv = WD = 0 for structure and capture.

    1 Quick start

    1. Pick a system. Choose a preset in System → Preset. The page computes it immediately; the status line at the top right shows the time and the number of energies.
    2. Read the verdict. Overview summarises what changed and what did not; Scoreboard lists every observable as “invariant” or “changes” for each transformation.
    3. Explore. Use the partial-wave chips (l = 0, p3/2, …) on the Potentials, Phase shifts, S-matrix and Cross sections tabs. Move the λ slider to change the T3 partner.
    4. Make it yours. Edit any field of Binary system & potential. When you leave a field the preset becomes “Custom” and the page recomputes; Compute does the same at once, and Reset to preset restores the preset values.
    5. Go beyond elastic. Open Capture & rates, pick the final bound state and press Compute.
    6. Export. Every plot has a Copy CSV button; Copy parameters (JSON) copies the full set-up.

    3 Input reference
