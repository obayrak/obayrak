2 · Models to Test

Model ranking is performed on the same experimental points. AIC/BIC penalize unnecessary parameters, so a model is not selected only because it has more adjustable parameters. The two spin-orbit models fit Vso, rso, aso with the radial shape fixed to Woods–Saxon and the j branch fixed to j = L+1/2, matching the convention that the imaginary absorption is always Woods–Saxon during a fit.

3 · Optimizer

h_step, R_match idx and L_max are taken directly from Parameters → Grid Settings, so the fit uses the exact radial grid shown there. L_min and L_step are deliberately ignored: a partial-wave amplitude is a sum over every L from 0 to L_max, and thinning it would corrupt both the fit and the main calculation. They remain display controls for the S-matrix / Argand tabs.
The fit uses only the experimental energies and angles, avoiding a full energy/angle grid. After fitting, press Apply best fit and COMPUTE for the full high-resolution calculation.

Contents

1  ·  Overview & Tab Map

For every requested \(E_{\rm cm}\) and every partial wave \(L=0\ldots L_{\max}\) the code solves the radial equation, extracts the Coulomb-normalized S-matrix \(S_L\), and builds all observables from it. One shared kernel serves both COMPUTE and the Global Fit, so the theory that enters \(\chi^2\) is the same theory that is plotted in the result tabs (verified point-by-point in the Fit Validation panel, Section 18).

Capabilities:

• Five radial shapes for the real and imaginary volume terms (Woods–Saxon, WS², Cosh/sech², Gaussian, Yukawa), plus real and imaginary surface (derivative) terms
• Uniform-sphere Coulomb potential, Coulomb–Hankel matching at \(R_M\)
• Complex S-matrix, absorption/phase display, Argand diagrams in three display modes
• Shape-elastic, reaction and total nuclear cross sections; Coulomb–nuclear \(d\sigma/d\Omega\) and ratio to Rutherford
• Near-side / far-side amplitude decomposition (Fuller 1975)
• Automatic numerical grid (\(h\), \(R_M\), \(L_{\max}\)) with explicit convergence checks and a one-pass refinement
• Global fit of angular distributions or excitation functions: 8 potential models, 5 optimizers, AIC/BIC ranking, an 11-item validation checklist
• Retrieval of experimental data from IAEA EXFOR with a unit/frame metadata audit, plus a multi-dataset library to pool several EXFOR retrievals (e.g. many energies) before fitting
• Plain-text .dat export of every plot; light and dark themes

2  ·  Quick Start (Forward Calculation)

• 1. Parameters → Nuclear System: enter A₁, Z₁ (projectile), A₂, Z₂ (target) and rc. The defaults describe α + 8Be.
• 2. Parameters → Grid Settings: set Ecm min/max/step and the angular range. Leave Automatic numerical grid ticked unless you want manual control of h, Rmatch and Lmax (Section 4).
• 3. Choose the real potential shape and depths, enable or disable the surface and imaginary terms (Section 5).
• 4. Press COMPUTE or Ctrl+Enter (works from any tab).
• 5. Open the result tabs, pick Ecm or L in the selectors, and export data if needed.

Remember: the result tabs display the outcome of the last COMPUTE. After changing any Parameters value press COMPUTE again; the tab-level Update buttons only redraw stored results with a different view (axes, selected L/E), they do not re-solve the equation.

3  ·  Parameters — Nuclear System

KINEMATICS USED THROUGHOUT
$$\mu c^2=\frac{A_1A_2}{A_1+A_2}\,(931.494\ \text{MeV}),\qquad k=\frac{\sqrt{2\mu c^2E_{\rm cm}}}{\hbar c},\qquad \eta=\frac{Z_1Z_2\,e^2\,\mu c^2}{(\hbar c)^2\,k}$$

• A₁, Z₁: projectile mass number and charge; A₂, Z₂: target mass number and charge. Masses are approximated by \(A\times931.494\) MeV.
• rc (fm): Coulomb radius parameter, \(R_C=r_c\,(A_1^{1/3}+A_2^{1/3})\); the Coulomb potential is that of a uniform charged sphere.
• Energies are always Ecm (MeV) in Parameters and the result tabs. Laboratory energies enter only through the frame selectors in Global Fit (Section 13).
• If \(Z_1Z_2=0\) then \(\eta=0\): pure nuclear scattering. The Rutherford reference vanishes, so the \(\sigma/\sigma_R\) panels and the ratio observable are undefined; use \(d\sigma/d\Omega\) instead.
• Loading an EXFOR dataset overwrites A₁, Z₁, A₂, Z₂ automatically (Section 14); rc is left untouched.

GRID RELATIONS

Grazing angular momentum (classical strong-absorption estimate, used by the Grazing button, the automatic grid and the EXFOR grid update):

$$E_{\rm cm}=V_C(R_{\rm int})+\frac{(\hbar c)^2\,L_g(L_g+1)}{2\mu c^2R_{\rm int}^2}\ \Rightarrow\ L_g=\frac{-1+\sqrt{1+\dfrac{8\,\mu c^2R_{\rm int}^2}{(\hbar c)^2}\big(E_{\rm cm}-V_C(R_{\rm int})\big)}}{2},\qquad R_{\rm int}=r_c\,(A_1^{1/3}+A_2^{1/3})$$ $$L_{\max}=\min\!\Big(50,\ \lceil L_g\rceil+\big\lceil 4+2\sqrt{L_g}\big\rceil\Big)\quad(\ge1)$$

Below the Coulomb barrier (\(E_{\rm cm}\le V_C(R_{\rm int})\)) the wave is taken as sub-barrier and \(L_g=0\). \(R_{\rm int}\) reuses the Coulomb radius from Section 3.

Two rules that are easy to miss.
• Lmin and Lstep are display controls. A scattering amplitude is a sum over every L from 0 to Lmax, so COMPUTE and the Global Fit always use the full ladder; thinning it would corrupt the physics, not just the plot.
• With “Automatic numerical grid” ticked, h_step, R_match idx and Lmax are overwritten at every COMPUTE and every Global Fit. Untick it to keep hand-chosen values.

5  ·  Parameters — Optical Potential Terms

Five independent terms can be added to the Coulomb and centrifugal parts; the spin-orbit term is optional and off by default. Each radius is \(R_x=r_x\,(A_1^{1/3}+A_2^{1/3})\).

POTENTIAL AS IMPLEMENTED
$$U(r)=V_C(r)+V_N(r)+V_{\rm surf}(r)+V_{\rm so}(r)\langle\mathbf L\!\cdot\!\mathbf S\rangle-i\big[W_{\rm vol}(r)+W_{\rm surf}(r)\big]$$
REAL SURFACE (derivative form)
$$V_{\rm surf}(r)=4V_s\,\frac{e^{(r-R_s)/a_s}}{\big[1+e^{(r-R_s)/a_s}\big]^2}$$
IMAGINARY SURFACE (derivative form)
$$W_{\rm surf}(r)=4W_s\,\frac{e^{(r-R_{ws})/a_{ws}}}{\big[1+e^{(r-R_{ws})/a_{ws}}\big]^2}$$
IMAGINARY VOLUME
$$W_{\rm vol}(r)=\Big|\,\text{shape}(r;\,W_0,R_w,a_w)\Big|$$
REAL VOLUME
$$V_N(r)=\text{shape}(r;\,V_0,R_v,a_v)\quad(\text{negative for }V_0>0)$$
SPIN-ORBIT (derivative form)
$$V_{\rm so}(r)=V_{so}\,\frac{1}{r}\frac{d\,\text{shape}(r;\,1,R_{so},a_{so})}{dr}$$

• The Yukawa shape is evaluated with \(r\ge0.05\) fm to avoid the \(1/r\) origin singularity (this applies to the spin-orbit Yukawa shape too).
• Potentials are local and energy-independent: one parameter set describes every energy in the grid. They are central and spin-independent unless the spin-orbit term is enabled, in which case the potential becomes \(L\)- and \(j\)-dependent through \(\langle\mathbf L\!\cdot\!\mathbf S\rangle\); COMPUTE must then be run separately for the \(j=L+\tfrac12\) and \(j=L-\tfrac12\) branches to see both.
• Unticking a term removes it from the plots and from the radial equation used by COMPUTE. The Global Fit ignores these checkboxes and switches terms on or off according to the selected model (Section 15); the imaginary shape is always Woods–Saxon during a fit, and so is the spin-orbit shape on the two models that include one (the j branch is likewise fixed to j = L+1/2 during the fit).

6  ·  Running a Calculation

What COMPUTE does, in order:

• If the automatic grid is on: select h, RM, Lmax from the physics scales (Section 19)
• For every energy and every \(L=0\ldots L_{\max}\): Numerov integration of the radial equation → match to Coulomb–Hankel waves at RM → \(S_L\)
• Integrated cross sections, then \(d\sigma/d\Omega\) on the angular grid, then the near/far decomposition
• Redraw every tab; a progress overlay is shown while computing

Status line: reports the number of energies, partial waves and angles used, and flags the optical-theorem consistency and \(|S(L_{\max})-1|\) so an insufficient Lmax is visible immediately (raise it, or use the Grazing/Auto button, if flagged).

• Reset restores the default nuclear system, grid and potential values (it does not touch Global Fit).
• Cost scales as (number of energies) × (Lmax+1) × Ngrid.

7  ·  Potentials & Wave Function Tabs

Potentials. Two plots: the real components (Coulomb, nuclear, surface, centrifugal for \(L>0\), and their sum) and the imaginary components. Imaginary curves are drawn as \(-W\), consistent with \(U=V-iW\). The centrifugal term is \(\hbar^2L(L+1)/2\mu r^2\) for the L chosen in the selector.

• Controls: X range (fm), real and imaginary Y ranges, Auto-scale, Update. Defaults: r = 0–15 fm.
• The Ecm selector on this tab has no effect on the curves because the potentials are energy-independent.
• Export: potential_L{L}.dat (a fixed export grid independent of the axis limits).

Wave Function. Plots \(u_L(r)=r\psi_L(r)\) (real part, imaginary part, modulus) for the selected L and Ecm, normalized to the maximum modulus, with the matching radius marked by a dotted line.

• The radial integration starts from \(u_0=0\), \(u_1=h^{L+1}\); the overall scale is arbitrary and cancels in \(S_L\), hence the normalization for display.
• Absorption shows up as a reduced amplitude inside the nucleus; beyond RM the solution is represented by the Coulomb–Hankel waves.
• Only L values of the display ladder (Lmin, Lstep) are stored. Export: wavefunction_L{L}_E{E}MeV.dat.

8  ·  S-Matrix & Argand Tabs

\(S_L\) is defined relative to the Coulomb waves, \(S_L=\eta_Le^{2i\delta_L}\) with \(0\le\eta_L\le1\): \(S_L=1\) means no nuclear effect; \(|S_L|<1\) measures absorption. The tab shows \(|S_L|\) and \(\arg S_L\) vs. L for the chosen Ecm, plus a table of \(|S|\), phase, Re and Im for the display ladder.

Argand diagram (unit circle, equal aspect ratio):

• Scatter: all L at the chosen energy, colored and labelled by L
• Trajectory: one L (selector enabled only in this mode) versus every energy; needs at least two energies
• All trajectories: one colored path per L versus energy
• Low L lies near the origin (strong absorption); peripheral high L clusters near (1, 0). On the unit circle, \(\eta_L=1\) (purely elastic).

Export: smatrix_all.dat (all energies × display-ladder L).

9  ·  Cross Sections & Differential σ Tabs

SHAPE-ELASTIC (plotted as σ_elastic)
$$\sigma_{\rm el}=\frac{10\pi}{k^2}\sum_{L=0}^{L_{\max}}(2L+1)\,|1-S_L|^2\ \ \text{mb}$$
REACTION
$$\sigma_{R}=\frac{10\pi}{k^2}\sum_{L=0}^{L_{\max}}(2L+1)\,\big(1-|S_L|^2\big)\ \ \text{mb}\quad(\ge0)$$
NUCLEAR TOTAL
$$\sigma_{\rm tot}=\sigma_{\rm el}+\sigma_R$$
DIFFERENTIAL (mb/sr)
$$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=10\,\big|f_C(\theta)+f_N(\theta)\big|^2$$
COULOMB AMPLITUDE (as coded)
$$f_C(\theta)=-\frac{\eta}{2k\sin^2(\theta/2)}\exp\!\Big[-i\eta\ln\sin^2\tfrac{\theta}{2}+2i\sigma_0\Big]$$
NUCLEAR AMPLITUDE
$$f_N(\theta)=\frac{1}{2ik}\sum_{L}(2L+1)\,e^{2i\sigma_L}(S_L-1)\,P_L(\cos\theta)$$
RUTHERFORD REFERENCE (mb/sr)
$$\frac{d\sigma_R}{d\Omega}=\frac{10\,\eta^2}{4k^2\sin^4(\theta/2)}$$

The factor of 10 in each cross-section formula converts \(\text{fm}^2\) to millibarn (1 fm² = 10 mb), so \(\sigma\) and \(d\sigma/d\Omega\) come out directly in mb and mb/sr.

• Cross Sections tab: the three curves vs. Ecm with linear/log axes and manual ranges, plus a table with an optical-theorem consistency column and the maximum \(\big||S|^2-1\big|\) as a unitarity check — both should sit at round-off level when the numerics are good.
• For charged projectiles the total elastic cross section (Coulomb + nuclear) diverges at \(\theta=0\); the σelastic shown here is the nuclear shape-elastic part only.
• Differential tab: select Ecm; left plot dσ/dΩ with the Rutherford curve, right plot \(\sigma/\sigma_R\); independent log/linear switches and Y limits for each. Identical-particle symmetrization is not applied.

10  ·  Near/Far Tab

The nuclear amplitude is split with Legendre functions of the second kind \(Q_L\) (Fuller 1975; formulas in Theory §9). The Coulomb amplitude is shared equally, \(f_C/2\) per side, and the sums run over every \(L=0\ldots L_{\max}\).

• Left plot: total, near-side, far-side and Rutherford \(d\sigma/d\Omega\) at the chosen Ecm. Right plot: each divided by Rutherford. Bottom plot: near, far and total for all energies.
• Individual near and far cross sections may exceed the total because of interference. Far-side dominance at large angles is the signature of a nuclear rainbow.
• \(Q_L\) is undefined at \(|\cos\theta|=1\); the angular grid on this tab therefore excludes \(\theta\le0.1^\circ\) and \(\theta\ge179.9^\circ\).
• Export: nearfar_E{E}MeV.dat.

12  ·  Global Fit — Workflow

“Global” means that one energy-independent parameter set is adjusted so that the model reproduces all points (every energy and angle) at once by minimizing \(\chi^2\). Each experimental point is evaluated at its own \(E_{\rm cm}\) and \(\theta_{\rm cm}\): there is no energy grid, angular grid or interpolation, and no point is dropped, clipped or re-weighted by the fit itself.

• 1. Set the nuclear system in Parameters (automatic after an EXFOR retrieval) and enter starting values for the potential.
• 2. Panel 1A: search, select, retrieve and load an EXFOR dataset — or Panel 1: paste or import data and set data type, format, unit, frames and uncertainty mode. Press Validate data.
• 3. Optional: Run diagnostics (results in Panel 8).
• 4. Panel 2: tick the models to test. Panel 3: choose the optimizer and its settings.
• 5. Press GLOBAL FIT. Stop ends the run after the current calculation; models already finished are kept.
• 6. Read Panels 4–8 (Sections 16–18).
• 7. Apply best fit to Parameters, then COMPUTE for the full-resolution calculation.

• Numerical grid: h_step, R_match idx and Lmax are taken from Grid Settings, with the automatic grid evaluated first when ticked. The \(\theta\) grid, E step, Lmin and Lstep play no role in a fit — only the data points' own \((E,\theta)\) count. Ecm min/max still matter because the automatic grid derives h, RM and Lmax from \(k_{\max}\), \(k_{\min}\) and the grazing wave at Emax: for pasted data, set Ecm min/max so that they bracket your data (EXFOR loading does this for you).
• Fixed during a fit: A₁, Z₁, A₂, Z₂, rc, the grid, the imaginary shape (always Woods–Saxon) and every parameter not listed for the chosen model.
• Starting point: the values currently in Parameters, clamped to the bounds of Section 15.

EXFOR re-synchronization. If an EXFOR dataset has been retrieved, GLOBAL FIT re-applies its nuclear system (A₁, Z₁, A₂, Z₂) at the start of the fit. To fit pasted data of a different system, press Clear EXFOR first.

$$\Delta\sigma=\text{err}\ \ \text{or}\ \ |\sigma|\frac{\text{err}}{100}\ \ \text{or}\ \ |\sigma|\,\text{err};\qquad \Delta\sigma_{\rm default}=|\sigma|\frac{p}{100}$$

• Unit factors to mb: b × 1000, mb × 1, µb × 10−3, nb × 10−6, pb × 10−9. Uncertainties receive the same unit factor and the same Jacobian as the values.
• The Jacobian is not applied to the ratio observable (it is frame-independent). Points whose \(\theta_{\rm lab}\) is kinematically forbidden (\(\tau\sin\theta_{\rm lab}>1\), possible when \(A_1>A_2\)) are rejected.
• Frame conversions are non-relativistic and use mass numbers, not exact masses.

Validation report (shown under the text box after Validate data): number of points, observable and internal unit, the unit factor and frame conversions actually applied, and warnings for defaulted or non-positive uncertainties and for rejected lines.

Check the two frame selectors first. Both default to laboratory. Data that are already in the c.m. system must be switched to c.m.; otherwise energies, angles and cross sections are converted a second time and \(\chi^2\) is meaningless.

14B  ·  Global Fit — Multi-dataset Library (Panel 1B)

Panel 1A's Retrieve / Load into Global Fit pair handles one dataset at a time: retrieving a second dataset overwrites the first in the fit textarea. Panel 1B sits underneath it and removes that limitation — it lets several EXFOR retrievals (typically the same reaction at different energies, since Global Fit already treats “global” as one fit across many energies at once) accumulate in a pool, then loads the whole pool into Global Fit in a single step.

How to use this panel
1. RetrieveUse Panel 1A as usual: search, select, and retrieve one dataset.
2. Add to libraryPress Add retrieved dataset to library. The dataset's points and metadata are snapshotted into the pool under its DatasetID.
3. RepeatRetrieve the next dataset (a different energy, or a different EXFOR entry for the same reaction) and add it too. Re-adding the same DatasetID replaces its earlier snapshot rather than duplicating it.
4. Load poolPress Load library into Global Fit once all the datasets you want are in the pool.

• What gets snapshotted. Adding a dataset to the library copies its retrieved points (E, θ, y, Δy) together with its own energy/angle frame, cross-section unit, differential flag, official-verification status, and the projectile/target (A, Z) parsed from its reaction notation — independently of Panel 1A, so a later retrieval or Clear EXFOR never touches datasets already in the library.
• Pooling on load. Load library into Global Fit converts every library entry onto one common basis — Elab (MeV), θlab (deg) for angular data, and a common cross-section unit (mb) — before writing the combined rows into the Panel 1 data box, then runs the normal validation/parse pass exactly as a single EXFOR load would. Each entry's own recorded unit (b, mb, µb, nb, pb) is converted to mb; any entry whose angle/energy is natively reported in the c.m. frame is converted back to lab (angle transform and the solid-angle Jacobian for differential cross sections) so that Global Fit's own lab→c.m. pass, applied once to the pooled set, is correct for every point.
• Nuclear system. A₁, Z₁, A₂, Z₂ are set from the first pooled dataset that has an identifiable reaction, exactly as a single EXFOR load would; Grid Settings (Ecm min/max, θ min/max) and Lmax are then re-bracketed to the full pooled range.
• Mixed pools. Only one data type is fit at a time: if the library holds both angular and excitation datasets, the majority type (by point count) is used and the rest are skipped. σ/σRutherford (ratio) datasets cannot be pooled with absolute cross sections and are skipped the same way. Skipped datasets, a mismatched nuclear system between pooled entries, and any entry whose units were not officially verified are all named explicitly in the status line under the panel — nothing is dropped silently.
• Remove / Clear. Each pooled entry has its own Remove button; Clear library empties the whole pool. The library is independent of Panel 1A's Clear EXFOR, which only resets the current search/selection, not the pool.

Typical workflow: search once, then for each energy of interest: select the dataset → Retrieve selected dataset → Add retrieved dataset to library. Repeat across energies, then press Load library into Global Fit once. This replaces retrieving and loading each energy separately (which would overwrite the previous one) with a single combined fit across all pooled energies.

16  ·  Global Fit — Statistics & Model Ranking

CHI-SQUARE
$$\chi^2=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{y_i^{\rm th}-y_i^{\rm exp}}{\Delta y_i}\right)^{2}$$
REDUCED CHI-SQUARE
$$\nu=\max(1,\,N-k),\qquad \chi^2_\nu=\frac{\chi^2}{\nu}$$
AKAIKE CRITERION
$$\mathrm{AIC}=\chi^2+2k$$
BAYESIAN CRITERION
$$\mathrm{BIC}=\chi^2+k\ln N$$

• \(N\): number of valid points; \(k\): number of free parameters of the model; \(y^{\rm th}\): theory from the shared kernel at each point's own \((E_{\rm cm},\theta_{\rm cm})\).
• Ranking is by BIC, lowest first; rank 1 is highlighted and defines the best fit. AIC and \(\chi^2_\nu\) are shown for comparison and do not change the order. Both criteria charge a penalty per free parameter, so a nine-parameter model only wins if its \(\chi^2\) drops enough to pay for it.
• \(\chi^2_\nu\approx1\) indicates a fit consistent with the quoted uncertainties; \(\chi^2_\nu\gg1\) means the model (or the uncertainties) cannot account for the data. The statistics assume independent Gaussian errors: common-normalization (systematic) uncertainties are not modelled.

19  ·  Numerical Accuracy & the Automatic Grid

The S-matrix is formed from \(u(R_M)\) and a central-difference derivative \(u'(R_M)\), matched to point-Coulomb Hankel waves. Accuracy therefore depends on h (Numerov truncation error), on RM lying outside both the nuclear tail and the Coulomb radius, and on Lmax being large enough that \(S_L\to1\).

AUTOMATIC h AND RM
$$h=\min\!\Big(0.05,\ \frac{a_{\min}}{14},\ \frac{0.18}{k_{\max}}\Big)\ \ \big(0.01\le h\le0.05\ \text{fm, rounded to }0.005\ \text{fm}\big)$$ $$R_M=\max\!\Big(R_n+10\,a_{\max},\ \frac{8}{k_{\min}},\ R_C+6\,a_{\max},\ 12\Big)\ \ \big(8\ \text{fm}\le R_M\le37\ \text{fm}\big)$$

• \(R_n=\max(r_v,r_w,r_s,r_{ws})(A_1^{1/3}+A_2^{1/3})\); \(a_{\min}\), \(a_{\max}\) are the smallest and largest of the four diffusenesses (with floors of 0.05 fm and 0.5 fm respectively); \(k_{\max},k_{\min}\) belong to Emax, Emin. R_match idx is then set to RM/h, rounded and floored at 50.
• Two hard limits: the radial box is 40 fm and \(N_{\rm grid}\le5000\); for very small h the point cap effectively shortens the usable box, so keep RM comfortably inside it.

Convergence criteria (probed at Emin, the midpoint and Emax; probe waves L = 0, the grazing wave, and Lmax; probe angles spanning 30°–150°):

TestComparisonTolerance
h_steph vs. h/2 at the same physical RM: max |ΔSL| and the relative change of dσ/dΩ5×10−3 (both)
RMSL at RM vs. a few fm further out, from the same propagated solution2×10−3
Lmaxdσ/dΩ with waves to Lmax vs. to Lmax plus a conservative margin, at Emin and Emax2×10−3

• Relative differences use the larger of the two compared values (with a small floor) as the scale so that interference minima do not inflate them.
• Refinement pass: if h fails it is halved (not below 0.005 fm); if RM fails it moves outward; if Lmax fails it grows by a margin tied to \(\sqrt{L_{\max}}\), up to 100. COMPUTE and GLOBAL FIT apply one such pass automatically; the Panel 6 Stability check reports the outcome explicitly for the best fit.
• Manual choice: if you untick the automatic grid, halve h and raise Rmatch and Lmax until the results stop changing, and keep \(\rho_M\gtrsim5\) at the lowest energy.

21  ·  Assumptions & Limitations

• Model: local, central-plus-spin-orbit, energy-independent optical potential; no non-locality and no dispersive energy dependence. The spin-orbit extension is optional and disabled by default.
• Kinematics: non-relativistic (\(k=\sqrt{2\mu E}/\hbar\), lab↔c.m. transformations); masses approximated by mass number × 931.494 MeV.
• Observables: elastic scattering and total absorption. The reaction cross section is the absorbed flux; no individual reaction channels or inelastic excitations are computed.
• Coulomb: uniform-sphere potential inside \(R_C\), point Coulomb outside; matching is to point-Coulomb waves, so RM must lie beyond both \(R_C\) and the nuclear tail.
• Identical particles: no Bose/Fermi symmetrization is applied (relevant e.g. for α + α or 12C + 12C).
• Numerics: radial box 40 fm, \(N_{\rm grid}\le5000\), \(h\ge0.005\) fm; the derivative at RM is a second-order accurate finite difference.
• Statistics: \(\chi^2\) uses the supplied uncertainties as independent Gaussian errors; systematic normalization errors are not included.
• Environment: single HTML file computing in the browser’s main thread. Plotly, MathJax and EXFOR need internet access; the theme choice is stored in the browser.

Optical Model

Nuclear elastic scattering is described via partial-wave decomposition of the two-body radial Schrödinger equation with a complex (optical) potential. The solutions are matched asymptotically to Coulomb–Hankel functions to extract the S-matrix, from which all observables follow.

1  ·  Radial Schrödinger Equation

For a two-body system with projectile mass A₁, target mass A₂, and charges Z₁, Z₂, the reduced-mass radial wave function \(u_L(r)\) satisfies:

RADIAL EQUATION
$$\left[-\frac{(\hbar c)^2}{2\mu c^2}\frac{d^2}{dr^2} + U_{\rm eff}(r,L) \right] u_L(r) = E_{\rm cm}\, u_L(r)$$

Parameters:

• \(u_L(r)\): reduced radial wave function \(u=rR(r)\)
• \(L\): orbital angular momentum quantum number
• \(E_{\rm cm}\): center-of-mass energy (MeV)
• \(\mu\): reduced mass of the system

where \(\mu c^2 = \dfrac{A_1 A_2}{A_1+A_2}\times 931.494\ \text{MeV}\).

$$U_{\rm eff}(r,L) = V_C(r) + V_N(r) - i\,W(r) + V_{\rm surf}(r) + V_{\rm so}(r)\,\langle\mathbf L\!\cdot\!\mathbf S\rangle + \frac{(\hbar c)^2 L(L+1)}{2\mu c^2 r^2}$$

• \(V_C\): Coulomb repulsion
• \(V_N\): real nuclear attraction
• \(W\): imaginary absorption (reaction channels)
• \(V_{\rm surf}\): surface imaginary term (derivative form)
• \(V_{\rm so}(r)\langle\mathbf L\!\cdot\!\mathbf S\rangle\): optional spin-orbit term (derivative form, off by default) — see §2
• last term: centrifugal barrier

2  ·  Potential Components

Coulomb potential (uniform sphere):

$$V_C(r) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{2R_C}\left(3 - \dfrac{r^2}{R_C^2}\right) & r \le R_C \\ \dfrac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{r} & r > R_C \end{cases}$$

• \(Z_1,Z_2\): projectile/target charge numbers
• \(R_C = r_c (A_1^{1/3}+A_2^{1/3})\): Coulomb radius

Woods-Saxon
$$V_{\rm WS}(r) = -\frac{V_0}{1+e^{(r-R)/a}}$$
WS-Squared
$$V_{\rm WS^2}(r) = -\frac{V_0}{\left[1+e^{(r-R)/a}\right]^2}$$
Cosh / sech²
$$V_{\rm sech^2}(r) = -\frac{V_0}{\cosh^2\!\left(\frac{r-R}{a}\right)}$$
Gaussian
$$V_{\rm Gauss}(r) = -V_0\,e^{-\left(\frac{r-R}{a}\right)^2}$$
Yukawa
$$V_{\rm Yukawa}(r) = -V_0\,\frac{e^{-(r-R)/a}}{r}$$

• \(V_0\): depth (MeV)
• \(R = r_0 (A_1^{1/3}+A_2^{1/3})\): nuclear radius
• \(a\): diffuseness (surface thickness)

Spin-orbit potential (optional, off by default):

SPIN-ORBIT TERM
$$V_{\rm so}(r)\,\langle\mathbf L\!\cdot\!\mathbf S\rangle = V_{\rm so}\,\frac{1}{r}\frac{dF_{\rm so}(r)}{dr}\,\langle\mathbf L\!\cdot\!\mathbf S\rangle$$

This is the standard Thomas-type derivative form used for nuclear spin-orbit coupling: the radial shape \(F_{\rm so}(r)\) is any one of the five profiles above (Woods–Saxon, WS², Cosh/sech², Gaussian or Yukawa), selected independently of the real and imaginary terms, evaluated at its own radius \(R_{\rm so}=r_{\rm so}(A_1^{1/3}+A_2^{1/3})\) and diffuseness \(a_{\rm so}\). Taking \(-\frac{1}{r}dF_{\rm so}/dr\) instead of \(F_{\rm so}\) itself concentrates the term at the nuclear surface, peaking near \(r\approx R_{\rm so}\) and vanishing at the origin and at large \(r\).

For spin-\(\tfrac12\) particles coupled to orbital angular momentum \(L\), the operator \(\langle\mathbf L\!\cdot\!\mathbf S\rangle\) takes one of two eigenvalues depending on the total angular momentum \(j=L\pm\tfrac12\):

$$\langle\mathbf L\!\cdot\!\mathbf S\rangle = \begin{cases} +\dfrac{L}{2} & j = L+\tfrac12 \ \ (\text{“plus” branch})\\[4pt] -\dfrac{L+1}{2} & j = L-\tfrac12 \ \ (\text{“minus” branch})\\[4pt] 0 & \text{spin-averaged} \end{cases}$$

• \(V_{\rm so}\): spin-orbit strength (MeV)
• \(F_{\rm so}\): chosen radial shape (same five options as the real/imaginary terms)
• \(R_{\rm so},\,a_{\rm so}\): spin-orbit radius and diffuseness (fm)
• \(j\) branch: “plus” (\(j=L+\tfrac12\)), “minus” (\(j=L-\tfrac12\)) or “spin-averaged” (\(\langle\mathbf L\!\cdot\!\mathbf S\rangle=0\), no contribution)
• For \(L=0\) the term is forced to zero (the \(j=L-\tfrac12\) branch is not physical for \(L=0\), and there is no orbital motion to couple to spin)
• Disabled (\(V_{\rm so}\equiv0\)) unless the spin-orbit checkbox is ticked in Parameters
• The Global Fit workbench can also include this term (two of its models fit \(V_{so}, r_{so}, a_{so}\) with the shape fixed to Woods–Saxon and \(j=L+\tfrac12\); see Manual §15)

3  ·  Numerov Integration

NUMEROV RECURRENCE
$$u_{n+1} = \frac{2\left(1 - \tfrac{5}{12}h^2 k_n^2\right)u_n - \left(1 + \tfrac{1}{12}h^2 k_{n-1}^2\right)u_{n-1}}{1 + \tfrac{1}{12}h^2 k_{n+1}^2}$$

• \(h\): radial step size
• \(k^2(r) = \dfrac{2\mu}{\hbar^2}(E-U_{\rm eff})\)
• Numerov is stable and \(O(h^6)\) accurate

4  ·  S-Matrix via R-Matrix Matching

S-MATRIX ELEMENT
$$S_L \;=\; \frac{u'(r_M)\,H_L^-(r_M) \;-\; u(r_M)\,\bigl(H_L^-\bigr)'(r_M)}{u'(r_M)\,H_L^+(r_M) \;-\; u(r_M)\,\bigl(H_L^+\bigr)'(r_M)}$$

• \(r_M\): matching radius (outside nuclear region)
• \(H^\pm\): Coulomb Hankel waves
• ensures continuity with exact asymptotic solution

5  ·  Cross Sections

ELASTIC
$$\sigma_{\rm elastic} = \frac{\pi}{k^2}\sum_L (2L+1)|1-S_L|^2$$
REACTION
$$\sigma_{\rm reaction} = \frac{\pi}{k^2}\sum_L (2L+1)(1-|S_L|^2)$$

• \(k=\sqrt{2\mu E}/\hbar\): wave number
• Reaction cross section measures absorption
• While the projectile or the target is not excited, \( \sigma_{\rm reaction}=\sigma_{\rm fusion}\)
• The total nuclear cross-section \( \sigma_{\rm total }=\sigma_{\rm elastic } + \sigma_{\rm reaction} \)

9 · Near/Far Decomposition (Fuller 1975)

The scattering amplitude is split into contributions from classical trajectories on the near side (Coulomb-side) and far side (nuclear rainbow side) using Legendre functions of the second kind \(Q_L\):

TRAVELLING-WAVE BASIS
$$P_L(\cos\theta) = \frac{1}{2}\left[P_L^+(\cos\theta) + P_L^-(\cos\theta)\right]$$ $$P_L^{\pm}(\cos\theta) = P_L(\cos\theta) \pm \frac{2i}{\pi}Q_L(\cos\theta)$$
NEAR-SIDE AMPLITUDE
$$f^{\rm near}(\theta) = \frac{f_C(\theta)}{2} + \frac{1}{2ik}\sum_L(2L+1)e^{2i\sigma_L}(S_L-1)\frac{P_L^-}{2}$$
FAR-SIDE AMPLITUDE
$$f^{\rm far}(\theta) = \frac{f_C(\theta)}{2} + \frac{1}{2ik}\sum_L(2L+1)e^{2i\sigma_L}(S_L-1)\frac{P_L^+}{2}$$

• \(Q_L(\cos\theta)\): Legendre function of the second kind (forward recurrence)
• \(f = f^{\rm near} + f^{\rm far}\) — decomposition is exact
• Far-side rainbow and Airy oscillations appear at large angles
• Near-side interference with Coulomb causes small-angle oscillations

Physical interpretation. Near-side trajectories are deflected toward smaller angles (repulsive Coulomb side), while far-side trajectories travel around the nucleus and are associated with the nuclear-rainbow side. The Coulomb amplitude is split equally, \(f_C/2\) per side. Note: individual near/far components can exceed the total cross section because of interference between amplitudes. The angular range is \(\theta_{min}\)–\(\theta_{max}\), as set in Grid Settings.

10 · Argand Diagram — S-Matrix in Complex Plane

Argand Diagram: The S-matrix elements (S_L(E)) trace trajectories in the complex plane as the energy increases. All trajectories must remain inside the unit circle \(|S_L| \le 1\) due to unitarity. A resonance appears as a clockwise loop crossing the real axis Re(S).

Each S-matrix element \(S_L = \eta_L e^{2i\delta_L}\) is a complex number lying inside or on the unit circle:

$$S_L = \eta_L e^{2i\delta_L}, \qquad 0 \le \eta_L \le 1, \quad \delta_L \in \mathbb{R}$$

• \(\eta_L = 1\), \(\delta_L = 0\): no interaction (high L, peripheral)
• \(\eta_L = 1\): purely elastic scattering, \(S_L\) on unit circle
• \(\eta_L = 0\): complete absorption (strong imaginary potential)
• As L decreases, \(S_L\) spirals inward from (1, 0) toward the origin
• The trajectory in the Argand plane reveals resonance structures and absorption

NUCLEAR PHASE SHIFT
$$\delta_L^{\rm nuc} = \frac{1}{2}\arg(S_L) - \sigma_L$$
