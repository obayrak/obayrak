1 · The Nuclear Shell Model

The nuclear shell model (independent-particle model) treats each nucleon as moving independently in an average potential generated by all other nucleons. Proposed by Maria Goeppert Mayer and J. Hans D. Jensen (Nobel Prize 1963), it explains the existence of magic numbers — nucleon numbers at which nuclei are exceptionally stable — by analogy with the closed electron shells of noble gases.

Unlike atomic orbitals, nuclear orbits arise from a combination of central potential (Woods-Saxon or harmonic oscillator) and a strong spin-orbit coupling term, which is essential for reproducing the observed magic numbers.

2 · The Mean-Field Potential

The single-particle Hamiltonian is:

Single-particle Hamiltonian: Ĥ = T + Vcentral(r) + VLS(r) · L̂·Ŝ

Woods-Saxon form (realistic): V(r) = −V₀ / [1 + exp((r − R)/a)]

where: R = r₀ · A1/3 (nuclear radius), r₀ ≈ 1.27 fm, a ≈ 0.67 fm

The spin-orbit term VLS ∝ −κ · (dV/dr) · L̂·Ŝ splits each ℓ > 0 orbital into two levels with j = ℓ + ½ and j = ℓ − ½. The j = ℓ + ½ state is lowered in energy (more bound), breaking the harmonic-oscillator degeneracy and producing the correct magic numbers.

3 · Quantum Numbers & Level Labels

Each single-particle state is labelled nℓⱼ where:

  • n = principal (radial) quantum number (1, 2, 3, … counts nodes + 1)
  • ℓ = orbital angular momentum: s(0), p(1), d(2), f(3), g(4), h(5), i(6)
  • j = total angular momentum = ℓ ± ½
  • Degeneracy of each level = 2j + 1 (magnetic substates)
  • Parity of level = (−1)ℓ

Protons and neutrons occupy separate sets of levels (Pauli exclusion applies independently to each species).

5 · Magic Numbers

Magic numbers occur when a shell gap is particularly large, leading to exceptionally tightly bound, spherical nuclei. The observed magic numbers are:

2 8 20 28 50 82 126

Simple harmonic oscillator gives degeneracy-magic numbers of 2, 8, 20, 40, 70 … The spin-orbit interaction lowers the highest-j orbital of each shell into the gap below, transforming 40→28, 70→50, 112→82, and producing the empirically correct sequence. The number 2 and 8 appear naturally in both the HO and SO pictures.

6 · Ground-State Spin and Parity

The ground-state spin-parity Jπ is determined by the valence nucleons — those outside filled (sub)shells:

  • Even-Even nuclei: All nucleons pair up (j = 0 coupling). Always Jπ = 0+
  • Odd-A nuclei (odd proton, even N): Spin = j of last unpaired proton; Parity = (−1)ℓ
  • Odd-A nuclei (odd neutron, even Z): Spin = j of last unpaired neutron; Parity = (−1)ℓ
  • Odd-Odd nuclei: Spin from Nordheim rules: |jp − jn| ≤ J ≤ jp + jn, parity = πp·πn; the actual J is difficult to predict without detailed calculations
Parity rule: π = (−1)ℓ  →  s,d,g,i orbitals: + parity · p,f,h orbitals: − parity

Nordheim strong rule (odd-odd): If (jp = ℓp+½ and jn = ℓn+½) or (jp = ℓp−½ and jn = ℓn−½):
  → J = |jp − jn|
Otherwise: J = jp + jn
