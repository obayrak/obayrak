⬡ Physics Derivation — How V(r), E(r), U(r) are obtained
Step 1 · Gauss's Law for E(r)

Gauss's law relates the electric flux through a closed Gaussian surface (sphere of radius r) to the enclosed charge:

∮ E⃗ · dA⃗ = Q_enc / ε₀

For a spherical shell of radius r, by symmetry E is radial and constant on the surface, so:

E(r) · 4πr² = Q_enc(r) / ε₀
E(r) = k · Q_enc(r) / r²

where k = 1/(4πε₀) ≈ 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²

Step 2 · Enclosed charge Q_enc(r)

The enclosed charge changes as we cross each shell boundary. Inside a conductor, E = 0, so charge redistributes on surfaces.

Step 3 · Electric Potential V(r)

V is obtained by integrating E from infinity inward (choosing V(∞) = 0):

V(r) = −∫∞r E(r') dr'

Outside all shells (r ≥ R₄):

V(r) = k(q₀+q₁+q₂) / r

Between shells, integrate piecewise. Inside a conductor, E=0 so V is constant throughout that shell.

VK = k(q₀+q₁+q₂)/R₄ + k·q₂·(1/R₃−1/R₄)
... + k·q₀·(1/R₁ − 1/R₂) + k·(q₀+q₁)/R₂

Each jump is computed by continuous integration across vacuum gaps.

Step 4 · Potential Energy U(r)

The electric potential energy of a test charge q_t placed at radius r is simply:

U(r) = q_t · V(r)

This represents the work done by an external agent to bring q_t from infinity to position r, or equivalently, the energy stored in placing it there.

Wext = U(r) − U(∞) = q_t · V(r)

If U(r) < 0 the particle is attracted (lower energy closer), if U(r) > 0 it is repelled.

Continuity & Boundary Conditions

V(r) is always continuous across any surface (otherwise infinite E would exist). E(r) can be discontinuous (jump) at a surface charge layer.

Vin(R) = Vout(R) [always]
Eout − Ein = σ/ε₀ [surface charge σ]

Inside a conducting shell: E = 0, so V = constant equal to the value at the shell surface.

Superposition Principle

Because the Laplace/Poisson equation is linear, potentials from each charge superpose:

Vtotal(r) = Vcore + VK + VL

Each shell contributes k·q_i/r outside it, and k·q_i/R_i (constant) inside it. The simulation computes this piecewise integral exactly for the current parameters.
