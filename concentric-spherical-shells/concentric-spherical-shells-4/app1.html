Setup

A solid insulating sphere (core, radius R₁, charge q₁) is surrounded by two concentric insulating shells of finite wall thickness t. Shell K carries charge q₂ (inner radius R₂−t, outer radius R₂); Shell L carries q₃ (inner R₃−t, outer R₃). Charges are fixed.

1 · Electric Field E(r) — Gauss's Law

∮ E · dA = q_enc / ε₀ → E(r) = k · q_enc / r²
  • r < R₁ (inside core):
    E(r) = k · q₁ · r / R₁³
  • R₁ ≤ r < R₂:
    E(r) = k · q₁ / r²
  • R₂ ≤ r < R₃:
    E(r) = k · (q₁ + q₂) / r²
  • r ≥ R₃:
    E(r) = k · (q₁ + q₂ + q₃) / r²

2 · Potential V(r) — Superposition

V(r) = V_core(r) + V_K(r) + V_L(r)
Shell (r ≥ R): V = kQ/r | Shell (r < R): V = kQ/R
Core inside: V = (k q₁ / 2R₁)(3 − r²/R₁²)

3 · Potential Energy and Force

U(r) = qₚ · V(r) F(r) = qₚ · E(r)

V(r) is a property of the shell system only; U and F depend on the test charge qₚ.

Reading the simulation

  • White sphere — test charge moving radially under the Coulomb force.
  • Yellow arrow — force F = qₚE. Reverses when qₚ or E changes sign.
  • Blue/red arrows — E field vectors; blue = outward (+E), red = inward (−E).
  • Colour-coded streamlines — static field lines coloured by E sign and magnitude (blue–violet = positive field, red–violet = negative).
  • Cut-face colour plate — the flat disc exposed on the cut face is a vertex-coloured mesh: cyan = strong +E, red = strong −E, dark = near-zero. The distinct colour bands at R₁, R₂, R₃ show the field-jump discontinuities at each shell.
  • Shell thickness — K and L shells have physical wall thickness t; the coloured annuli on the cut face show the shell cross-sections.
  • Left chart — E(r) vs r. White dot = particle.
  • Right chart — V(r) and U(r). Two markers track the particle.

Tips

  • Use Half cutaway or Quarter cutaway to see the colour plate and thick shells.
  • Increase thickness t to make the shell walls more prominent.
  • Set q₁ + q₂ + q₃ = 0 — the plate goes dark outside R₃ (zero net external field).
  • Negative qₚ flips the force arrow without affecting E or V.
